# Political intrigue: beats

The signature structure is the **faction web**: a handful of factions, each tied to every other by something concrete. Arcs, sessions and scenes are all ways of pulling on those ties until one snaps.

## Build the web first

- **4 to 6 factions.** Three feels like a duel with a referee; seven and the players lose the names.
- **A tie between every pair.** A tie is a noun the party could steal, reveal or break: a debt, a marriage, a hostage, a shared crime, a contract, a grudge with a date on it. "They dislike each other" is not a tie.
- **One load-bearing tie per faction.** If it snaps, that faction changes sides or collapses. Write down which one it is.
- **A hinge event.** One event on a known date forces every faction to act: a vote, a coronation, a treaty signing, a will reading, the end of a regency.
- **The party's entry tie.** At least one faction already has a claim on a player character: a debt, a relative, a patron, an old favour. Check `get_party` backstories before inventing one.

## Arc structure: the Squeeze

Five movements, usually 4 to 8 sessions. Pressure rises toward the hinge, then the debts come due.

1. **Seating.** The party is placed in the web: hired, invited, summoned or blamed. Every faction makes contact within the first session or two, each with a small, reasonable ask.
2. **First ledger.** The party does favours and receives them. Each one creates a debt. The players learn who owes whom by watching who flinches.
3. **Pressure.** One load-bearing tie is threatened (by the party, a rival, or chance). Factions realign. Somebody the party trusted asks for something they cannot comfortably give.
4. **The hinge.** The scheduled event happens. Every faction plays its leverage. The party holds at least one piece that can swing the outcome.
5. **Reckoning.** Debts are collected. The new order shows who won, and what the party's choices cost each side. Seed the next web from the losers.

## Between-session faction turn

Run this after every session, then offer the DM the results as a short list to keep or discard.

1. **What did each faction learn?** Only what its people saw, heard or were told. A faction with no one in the room learns nothing.
2. **One move each.** Advance the agenda one step, or react to the party. Not both.
3. **A visible sign.** Every move leaves a trace the party can notice: a changed seating order, a new guard at a door, a broadsheet headline, a cancelled dinner.
4. **Debts check.** Note any favour that is now due and which NPC will come to collect it.
5. **One wildcard.** Once per arc, let something outside the web happen (a fever, a flood, a death from old age) and let every faction scramble.

## Session structure: the Court Day

1. **News and seating (short).** Open with what changed since last time, shown through the room: who is sitting closer to the throne, who is absent, whose banner has been taken down.
2. **The ask.** A faction, or the party's own patron, makes a request with a clear deadline inside the session.
3. **Rounds.** Two to four short conversations or errands the players choose between. Each yields one piece of leverage or one debt. Time is the cost: they cannot visit everyone.
4. **The set piece.** A council, banquet, audience or trial where leverage is played in public. End it on a decision the players make.
5. **The ledger.** Close by stating aloud who now owes whom. Let the players see the web shift.

## Scene structure: the three-sided negotiation

1. **Positions.** Each side states what it demands. Keep it short and specific: "the river charter, signed today".
2. **Interests.** Under each position is a need: money, safety, face, time. The party discovers interests by asking, by legwork, or by reading a tell.
3. **Leverage on the table.** Each side shows or hints at what it holds. Bluffs are allowed; the party can call them.
4. **The third side moves.** The mediator, rival bidder or affected group changes the shape of the deal: a new offer, a threat to walk, a public appeal.
5. **Close on a debt.** Whatever is agreed, somebody gave more than they wanted. Name the debt it creates.

## Worked example: the Toll Vote of Carrowmere

**Premise.** The Bridge Council of Carrowmere, a river city, votes in three weeks on renewing the toll that pays for its wall. The party is hired by the council clerk, Isolde Fane, to keep order during the debate.

**Factions.**
- **House Velde** holds the toll charter. Public: "the toll keeps the wall standing." Real: toll money quietly services Velde's crushing debt.
- **The Bargemen's Compact**, the river workers' guild. Public: "abolish the toll, feed the docks." Real: its chair, Hob Tarrance, has taken foreign silver to vote no.
- **The Chapter of the Ashen Scale**, a temple with a counting-house. Public: neutral charity. Real: holds Velde's debt and wants the toll charter as collateral.
- **The Envoy of Ostrevan**, from the kingdom upriver. Public: friendship and trade. Real: a free river lets Ostrevan grain undercut local farms until the city depends on it.

**Ties.**

| Pair | Tie |
| --- | --- |
| Velde and Ashen Scale | Velde owes the Chapter 9,000 crowns (Velde's load-bearing tie) |
| Compact and Ostrevan | Tarrance's silver (the Compact's load-bearing tie) |
| Velde and Compact | A bridge collapse ten years ago killed eleven bargemen; Velde was never charged |
| Ashen Scale and Compact | The Chapter runs the dockside almshouse the bargemen need each winter |
| Ostrevan and Velde | The envoy holds the Velde heir's gambling markers |
| Ashen Scale and Ostrevan | The Chapter's prior wants a new mother-house built upriver, on Ostrevan land |

**Outline.**
1. **Session 1, Seating.** A brawl between bargemen and Velde guards on the bridge. Each faction approaches the party afterward with a small ask (escort a letter, find a missing witness, attend a dinner). The clerk warns them that every gift in Carrowmere is a loan.
2. **Session 2, First ledger.** The party's choices earn them a debt from two factions and one enemy. Legwork can uncover the bridge-collapse survivor or the heir's markers.
3. **Session 3, Pressure.** A bargeman is found beaten, wearing Velde colours that do not fit him. The Compact demands justice; Velde demands the party clear its name. The Chapter offers to mediate, for a price.
4. **Session 4, The hinge.** The vote. Ostrevan's envoy makes a public offer to fund the wall himself if the toll falls. The party holds whichever leverage they earned; nothing they did not earn is available.
5. **Session 5, Reckoning.** Whoever won collects. The losers come to the party with new asks.

**Options to offer the DM at the vote.** Velde breaks with the Chapter and confesses the debt (honest, ruinous); the Compact splits when Tarrance's silver is exposed; the envoy's offer is accepted and the city owes Ostrevan instead. Each opens a different next arc.
