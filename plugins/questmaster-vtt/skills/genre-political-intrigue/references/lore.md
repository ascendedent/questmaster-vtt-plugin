# Political intrigue: lore and texture

Intrigue lives in small signals: who stands where, what colour the wax is, which word was left out of a toast. Give the DM physical details that carry political meaning, so the players can read the room without a single exposition speech.

## Lore textures

- **Precedence.** Who enters first, who sits nearest the ruler, who speaks before whom. Changes in precedence are news.
- **Seals and signatures.** A document is only as powerful as its seal. Lesser seals, privy seals, a dead lord's seal still in use, a seal stolen and missed.
- **Charters and grants.** Old rights written down: the right to tax a bridge, to fish a river, to sit while others stand. Every charter has a clause someone has forgotten.
- **Marriage contracts.** Dowries, inheritance clauses, what happens to the land if there is no heir. Marriage is treaty-making with dinner.
- **Ledgers.** Debts, tithes, wages. The person who keeps the book has power over the person whose name is in it.
- **Hostages and wards.** Children raised at a rival court as guarantees of good behaviour. They grow up with divided loyalties.
- **Mourning and celebration.** Funerals and weddings force everyone into the same room under rules of courtesy. Perfect for intrigue.

## Sensory palette

**Sights**
- Wax seals in house colours on a stack of letters, one colour conspicuously missing.
- An empty chair at the council table, still with its cushion.
- A banner taken down, leaving a cleaner rectangle of stone behind it.
- Ink on the fingers of a lord who claims never to write his own letters.
- Two rivals wearing the same colour by accident; their stewards will both be dismissed.

**Sounds**
- The usher's staff striking the floor three times; the hush that follows.
- Coins counted in the next room through a door left ajar.
- A laugh that stops a beat too soon when the party enters.
- Pages turning during a speech, which is an insult if it is the ruler's speech.
- Quills scratching in the gallery: someone is taking minutes nobody ordered.

**Smells**
- Beeswax candles in the great hall, tallow in the servants' passage: status by scent.
- Orris powder and rosewater over unwashed velvet.
- Sealing wax melting; a burnt letter in a grate, still warm.
- The river on a hot day, reaching even the council chamber.

**Textures**
- Heavy brocade that rustles when its wearer turns, so nobody in it can move quietly.
- Grit of drying sand on a freshly signed document.
- The cold weight of a chain of office.
- A glove offered in greeting and not removed: an insult you can feel.

## Naming guidance

- **Houses:** a family name tied to land or an ancient deed. Two syllables reads as old, three reads as grand. Examples: Merrowin, Velde, Astreth, House Coldharrow, the Brannock line.
- **Offices:** archaic, specific, slightly absurd. Examples: Keeper of the Lesser Seal, Warden of the Inner Stair, Master of Precedence, Clerk of the Third Bench, Steward of the Bridge Tolls.
- **Guilds and compacts:** named for tools, trades or oaths. Examples: the Bargemen's Compact, the Worshipful Order of Weighers, the Sworn Masons of the Eastgate.
- **Temples with treasuries:** an image of balance or measure. Examples: the Chapter of the Ashen Scale, the House of the Even Hand.
- **Foreign powers:** a different sound pattern from local names, so players can tell at once who is from elsewhere. If locals use hard consonants (Velde, Brannock), give the foreigners open vowels (Ostrevan, Iolaire).
- **Nicknames:** courts nickname everyone. "The Ledger" for a chancellor, "Second Spoon" for a noble who always eats after the ruler. Nicknames tell the players what the court thinks.

## In-world documents

Each of these can be handed out through `stage_secret`, aimed at the character most likely to come across it.

- **A seating chart** with three names crossed out and rewritten. The crossings-out are the story.
- **A council minute** with one line struck through so heavily the paper has torn.
- **A debt note**: "Received of the Chapter, nine thousand crowns, to be repaid upon the renewal of the charter or upon demand." Signed and witnessed by someone now dead.
- **A marriage clause**: "Should the union produce no heir within five years, the eastern pastures revert to the bride's house." The wedding was four years and ten months ago.
- **A broadsheet** with a satirical woodcut: a fat goose wearing a chain of office. Everyone knows who the goose is.
- **An intercepted letter in a simple cipher.** Decoding it takes a minute. Deciding what to do with it takes a session.
- **A petition** with ninety signatures and one mark that is clearly a councillor's private seal.
- **An invitation** to a supper, unsigned, with a sprig of rosemary tucked inside. Rosemary means remembrance; someone is reminding the party of a debt.

## Rumour table

Read one aloud at the start of a session, or stage it as a secret to a character with contacts.

1. The heir has been seen at a moneylender's door twice this week.
2. The garrison was not paid on the first of the month.
3. The envoy's courier rode out at night, without a lamp.
4. The prior has ordered new account books bound in black.
5. Someone bought every white rose in the market; there is a wedding nobody has announced.
6. A clerk from the archive has not been seen for three days.
