# Mystery: beats

The signature structures are the **three-clue rule** and the **clue-to-scene graph**. The truth sits in the middle; revelations are the conclusions the players must reach about it; clues are traces of the truth scattered across scenes; scenes are where the players go to look.

## Step by step

1. **The truth.** One paragraph, DM-only: who, what, when, how, why, and what the culprit has done since.
2. **Revelations.** The 3 to 6 conclusions the players must reach to understand the truth. Write each as a sentence they could say aloud: "The bell is short of bronze."
3. **Clues.** At least three per revelation, of different kinds, in different scenes. A clue is a fact the players can find, not a conclusion.
4. **Scenes.** The places, people and events that hold the clues. Each scene holds clues pointing to at least two other scenes.
5. **Entry points.** At least three scenes the players can start from.
6. **The proactive clock.** What the culprit does each day the players have not caught them. Every move leaves a new clue.
7. **Red herrings.** One or two real secrets that look like the crime from the wrong angle.

## Clue kinds

- **Physical:** a wound, a stain, a missing tool, a footprint, a locked door left unlocked.
- **Testimony:** what a person saw, heard or believes. Often partial, sometimes mistaken.
- **Document:** a ledger, a letter, a daybook, a receipt, a will.
- **Behaviour:** someone acting out of character: hurrying, avoiding, paying, burning something.

A good revelation has clues of at least two kinds, so players who prefer talking and players who prefer searching both reach it.

## Arc structure: the layered mystery

Usually 2 to 4 sessions. Each layer is a revelation that opens the next set of scenes.

1. **The surface.** What everyone believes happened ("he fell"). The first revelation breaks it.
2. **The method.** How it was actually done. Opens the physical scenes.
3. **The motive.** Why. Opens the people.
4. **The hidden cause.** What the crime was protecting. Often bigger than the crime itself.
5. **The proof and the choice.** The players know; now they must prove it, or decide what to do without proof.

## Session structure: the investigation session

1. **The hook.** The crime scene, or the news of it. Something wrong is visible in the first five minutes.
2. **Open the board.** Show the players at least three leads at once. Let them choose.
3. **Legwork.** Scenes in the order the players choose. Track which clues they have found.
4. **Pressure.** At the midpoint, or when the table stalls, the culprit makes a move from the clock.
5. **The turn.** A revelation that reframes earlier clues. Let the players make the connection themselves.
6. **Close on a decision.** Accuse, wait, set a trap, or follow the new lead. Never end on "and you are stuck".

## Scene structure: the investigation scene

- **Arrival.** One strong first impression (a smell, a sound, a wrongness).
- **The obvious.** What anyone sees without trying. Usually one clue.
- **The hidden.** What a search, a question or an expertise finds. One or two clues.
- **The person.** Someone here knows something, wants something, or is hiding something.
- **If they linger or leave.** What changes if the players stay too long or come back later (the evidence is cleaned, the witness leaves town).

## Worked example: the Silence at Hessick Foundry

**The truth.** Master bellfounder Oswin Garr cast the new temple bell short of bronze, splitting the stolen metal's value with Brother Calloway, the temple's warden, who approved payment for the full weight. Garr, dying of a cough from years of fumes, decided to confess and recast the bell honestly. He wrote "C. at midnight, to settle" in his daybook. Calloway came, struck him with a tamping iron, and rolled him into the casting pit to look like a fall. He paid the night carter to say Garr had been drunk. He now wants the bell blessed and hung before anyone tests it.

**Revelations.**
- **R1:** Garr was murdered, not killed by a fall.
- **R2:** The bell is short of bronze.
- **R3:** Calloway arranged the skim with Garr.
- **R4:** Garr meant to confess, and Calloway was with him that night.

**Clues** (at least three per revelation, kinds mixed).

| Revelation | Clue | Kind | Scene |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | The head wound is square; the pit's edge is rounded brick | Physical | Undertaker |
| R1 | The carter says Garr was drunk; the tavern keeper says Garr had not touched drink in a year | Testimony | Carter's yard, Tavern |
| R1 | A tamping iron scrubbed clean and hung on the wrong hook | Physical | Foundry |
| R1 | "C. at midnight, to settle" | Document | Widow's house |
| R2 | The bell rings flat to a trained ear | Physical | Temple |
| R2 | The merchant's sales of copper and tin fall short of the weight on the temple's invoice | Document | Metal merchant |
| R2 | Garr sent his journeyman home before the pour and weighed the metal alone | Testimony | Journeyman |
| R2 | Ingots hidden under the foundry floor | Physical | Foundry |
| R3 | The temple accounts, signed by Calloway, approve the full weight | Document | Temple |
| R3 | The carter was paid by "a man in grey with a temple ring" | Testimony | Carter's yard |
| R3 | The hidden ingots are wrapped in old altar linen | Physical | Foundry |
| R3 | Calloway pushes to hang and bless the bell before it is rung | Behaviour | Temple |
| R4 | The widow says Garr was coughing blood and had asked for a priest | Testimony | Widow's house |
| R4 | The sexton saw Calloway return after midnight with soot on his hems | Testimony | Temple |
| R4 | The daybook entry, read again once Calloway is a suspect | Document | Widow's house |

**Entry points.** The foundry (the body was found there), the widow (she asks for help), the temple (the bell's hanging is announced).

**Red herrings, each true about something.**
- **The journeyman's argument.** Tamsin Reeve shouted at Garr that evening. True: she is leaving to found a rival foundry with a backer, and taking two customers. Following it reveals her secret workshop, and her testimony about the weighing (an R2 clue).
- **The widow sold the tools.** True: she owes a moneylender. Following it reveals Garr's illness (an R4 clue).
- **The bell is cursed.** Townsfolk say so because it rings flat. True about the skim (an R2 clue).

**Proactive clock.**
- **Day 1:** Calloway hurries the blessing ceremony forward. (Behaviour clue.)
- **Day 2:** He offers to buy the metal merchant's ledger "for the temple archive". (The merchant mentions it.)
- **Day 3:** He plants the tamping iron in Tamsin's workshop. It is too clean, and Tamsin was seen elsewhere all night. (New physical and testimony clues.)
- **Day 4:** The bell is hung and blessed. Proof now requires taking it down.

**Options for the DM at the end.** Expose Calloway at the blessing, in front of the town; take the proof quietly to the temple's superiors, who may bury it; or let the widow decide, since the skim money could save her house.
