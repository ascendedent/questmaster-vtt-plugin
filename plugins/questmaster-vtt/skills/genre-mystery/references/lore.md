# Mystery: lore and texture

Mysteries are built from small, physical facts. Give the DM details precise enough to be clues and ordinary enough to be overlooked. The best clue is described in the same tone as the furniture.

## Lore textures

- **Routine.** Every household, workshop and street has one. Who opens the shutters, when the bread arrives, which dog barks at whom. A clue is often a break in routine.
- **Trades and their marks.** Each trade leaves traces: a dyer's blue nails, a smith's burn scars, a scribe's callus, a miller's flour in the seams. Let the players read people by their work.
- **Time.** Bells, candles, tides, market days. A world without clocks still knows the hour. Timelines are built from "after the second bell" and "before the tide turned".
- **Paper.** Ledgers, letters, wills, receipts, pawn tickets, prayer cards. In a mystery, everyone writes things down eventually.
- **Small-town knowledge.** Everyone knows everyone's business, and everyone is wrong about something. The gap between what the town believes and what happened is the mystery.
- **Magic and its limits.** Decide early what investigation magic exists, how much it costs, and what it cannot do (encounters.md). Write it into the world: a temple that charges for speaking to the dead, a law against truth magic in courts.

## Sensory palette

**Sights**
- A chair pushed back from the table at an angle nobody sits at.
- A clean rectangle on a dusty shelf where a box used to be.
- Two cups on the drying rack in a house where one person lives.
- A candle burned down to the holder in a room the victim supposedly left at dusk.
- Ink still wet on the last page of a daybook.

**Sounds**
- A floorboard that creaks in the dead man's study, though nobody has walked in for a day.
- The bell that rings flat, every hour, reminding the town.
- A yard gate that swings open without a sound; its hinges were oiled this week, though the rest of the ironwork is rust-red.
- A conversation that stops when the players enter, then resumes on a different subject.

**Smells**
- Bitter almond on a cup that has been rinsed but not scrubbed.
- Fresh lime-wash on one wall of a cellar.
- Tallow in a house that only burns beeswax.
- Hot metal and wet soot from a foundry that should have been cold.
- A perfume the victim never wore, faint on a pillow.

**Textures**
- Grit on the hem of a cloak, from a road the wearer says they never walked.
- A tool handle still faintly damp, though the workshop has been closed for a day.
- The give of a loose stone in a hearth.
- Rough, recently reused parchment, scraped and written over, with older letters showing through.

## Naming guidance

- **Ordinary names hide better.** In a mystery, the culprit should not have the most sinister name in the town. Give everyone plain, working names: Oswin Garr, Tamsin Reeve, Brother Calloway, Widow Annick Garr, Hob the carter.
- **Names that mean a trade** help players remember who is who: Reeve, Cooper, Fletcher-style surnames, or nicknames like "Carter" and "the Sexton".
- **Places named for their past use:** the Old Tannery, Drowner's Steps, the Widow's Bridge. Each implies a story the gossip network will tell.
- **One name, one person.** Never give two NPCs similar names (Calloway and Galloway). Players must be able to say a name and be understood.
- **Inns and taverns** are clue hubs; name them memorably: the Copper Kettle, the Lamb and Ledger, the Last Bell.

## In-world documents

Stage each as a `stage_secret` aimed at the character most likely to find or read it, with its exact text. Give each clue its own small plot node linked to the revelation it supports.

- **A daybook page:** "Ordered tin, 40 lb. Paid Hob. Cough worse. C. at midnight, to settle."
- **A pawn ticket:** "One silver brooch, swan design, pledged 3 crowns, to be redeemed within the month." The month ends tomorrow.
- **A temple invoice:** "Received of the Warden, for the casting of the great bell, 900 lb bronze at the agreed rate." Signed in two hands.
- **A half-burned letter** recovered from a grate: only the middle third survives, and it is about money, not love.
- **An apothecary's register** of poisons sold "for rats", with one entry in a different ink.
- **A laundry list** from a wealthy house, listing one shirt more than the household owns.
- **A will** that names a beneficiary nobody in the family has heard of.
- **A broadsheet** reporting a different death in a nearby town three years ago, by the same method.

## Clue-writing checklist

For every clue you draft, answer:

1. **What does it say?** The exact words or description the player receives.
2. **Where is it?** The scene.
3. **Who finds it best?** The character to aim the `stage_secret` at.
4. **What does it point to?** The revelation (as a linked plot node) and the next scene.
5. **What if it is missed?** The other clues for the same revelation.
6. **Does it point anywhere wrong?** If so, which red herring, and what truth waits at the end of it.
