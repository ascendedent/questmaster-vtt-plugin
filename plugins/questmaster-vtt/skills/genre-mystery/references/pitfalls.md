# Mystery: pitfalls and fixes

Each entry names a failure mode the assistant should catch in the DM's prep or its own drafts, and the fix.

## The single-clue chokepoint

- **Symptom:** The whole mystery depends on the players finding one hair on one pillow.
- **Fix:** Apply the three-clue rule to every revelation. On the plot board, count incoming links to each revelation node; any revelation with fewer than three clue nodes linked into it is a chokepoint. Offer the DM two more clues for it, in different scenes.

## The failed roll that ends the investigation

- **Symptom:** "You search the study and find nothing." The players have nowhere to go.
- **Fix:** Failure costs, it does not block. On a failed search, they still find the clue, but a servant sees them, or it takes all afternoon, or the culprit hears they were there. Or they find a different clue for the same revelation.

## The red herring that leads nowhere

- **Symptom:** The players spend a session chasing a suspect who turns out to be irrelevant, and feel cheated.
- **Fix:** Every red herring is true about something. The suspect lied because of a debt, an affair or a second crime. Following the herring reveals that truth, gives the players a real NPC, and usually yields one clue about the actual crime.

## The pixel hunt

- **Symptom:** The players must ask exactly the right question or search exactly the right drawer.
- **Fix:** Put clues where people would naturally look. If a player describes searching the desk, they find what is in the desk. Reward approach, not phrasing.

## The solution only the DM can see

- **Symptom:** The truth makes sense in the DM's head, but the clues do not actually point to it.
- **Fix:** Use the clue-writing checklist (lore.md). For each revelation, ask: could a sharp player reach this from these clues alone? If not, add a clue that bridges the gap.

## The players solve it early

- **Symptom:** In the first hour, a player says "it's the warden". They are right.
- **Fix:** Let them be right. Do not change the culprit. Shift the problem from "who" to "how do we prove it" and "what will the culprit do now that they know we know". Offer the DM the culprit's next move on the clock.

## The NPC detective solves it

- **Symptom:** A clever NPC explains the solution while the players watch.
- **Fix:** NPC investigators hold half the answer and trade it. They can be right about method and wrong about motive. The players make the final connection.

## Too many suspects

- **Symptom:** Nine suspects, and the players cannot keep them straight.
- **Fix:** Three to five suspects is plenty. Give each a distinct trade, look and tell. Offer a `save_monitor_preset` with their portraits as a suspect board for the table screen (the DM makes portraits in the app).

## The invisible culprit

- **Symptom:** The culprit appears for the first time in the final scene.
- **Fix:** The culprit should appear early and be likeable or ordinary. Fair play requires the players to have met them, ideally in a scene about something else.

## Divination breaks it

- **Symptom:** A cleric casts a spell and the mystery is over.
- **Fix:** Treat each spell as one more clue source (encounters.md). The dead know only what they saw. The culprit knows magic exists and plans for it. Answer the question asked, honestly and narrowly.

## Clue overload without synthesis

- **Symptom:** The players have fifteen clues and no idea what they mean.
- **Fix:** Encourage synthesis at the table. At the end of a session, ask the players to state what they believe. In the recap, list what they have found grouped by scene, not by revelation (grouping by revelation gives the answer away).

## Forgotten clues between sessions

- **Symptom:** A week later, nobody remembers the pawn ticket.
- **Fix:** Every clue is a staged secret, so once the DM pushes it the players can find it again in the app. Remind the DM to open each session with a short "what do you know" from the players.

## The info-dump denouement

- **Symptom:** The culprit, once caught, explains everything in a long speech.
- **Fix:** Let the players explain it; the culprit confirms, denies or corrects one detail. Save one small surprise (a motive deeper than expected) for the culprit to reveal.

## The proactive clock that never ticks

- **Symptom:** The culprit waits patiently while the players investigate for a week.
- **Fix:** Advance the clock one step each in-world day, or each time the players get close. Every step leaves a new clue, so pressure also helps stuck players.

## The DM's twist versus the clues

- **Symptom:** A late twist contradicts clues the players already have.
- **Fix:** Twists must fit every clue found so far. If they do not, the twist is wrong, not the clues.
