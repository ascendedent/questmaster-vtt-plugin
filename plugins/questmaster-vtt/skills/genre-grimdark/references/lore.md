# Grimdark: Lore

Grimdark lore is practical. It lives in ledgers, writs, ration tallies, bounty lists and letters home. The history that matters is recent: last year's harvest, the battle three months ago, the treaty that broke in spring. Grand myths exist, but people mostly use them to justify what they already wanted to do.

## Lore textures

- **Debased everything.** Coins clipped and mixed with lead; titles sold; oaths sworn to three lords in a year; holy days moved to suit the army.
- **Paperwork of war.** Requisition writs, conscription rolls, pardons, passes, bounty notices. Power is whoever holds the stamp.
- **Recent atrocity.** Every region has a recent event everyone references and no one agrees on: who burned the mill at Askel, who started the hanging at the crossroads.
- **Faded glory.** A banner from a victory nobody remembers the cause of; a statue of a king with the head replaced; a road built for an empire that is gone.
- **Ordinary decency.** Small customs that survive: leaving bread on the step for travelers, burying enemy dead with their faces covered, a truce at harvest that both armies still mostly keep.
- **Scarcity economics.** Prices change weekly; salt is currency; a pair of boots is worth a horse in winter.

## Sensory palette

### Sights
- Crows on a gibbet, and a child chasing them off for the sake of the man hanging there.
- A field of stubble that was harvested by one army and burned by the other.
- Banners stitched over with a new device, the old one showing through.
- A town gate with a list of the hanged nailed to it, updated in fresh chalk.
- Smoke on the horizon, every horizon, every day.

### Sounds
- A camp at night: coughing, a fiddle, a whetstone, a man crying quietly and nobody mentioning it.
- A market where the haggling is too quiet because nobody has anything to sell.
- Church bells rung for a victory, and nobody in the square cheering.
- Wheels in mud, endlessly.

### Smells
- Wet wool, wood smoke, horse.
- Vinegar used to wash wounds, and what it cannot cover.
- Bread baked with sawdust to stretch the flour.
- Tallow, cheap and rancid.

### Textures and temperature
- Mud to the shin, frozen at the surface in the morning.
- Coins worn smooth and greasy from too many hands.
- Rope-burned palms; armor straps stiff with old sweat.
- A cold that never quite leaves the bones by midwinter.

## Naming guidance

- **People** have short, plain names and earned nicknames. The nickname tells the story: Odile Marsh, Hesk of the Ford, Brannigan Two-Pay, Old Mother Kettle, Sergeant Ashwick, Lisbet the Counter.
- **Companies** take a color, an animal or a founder's name: the Brindle Company, the Mudlarks, Halloran's Lances, the Saltboots. Avoid grandiose "black" or "dark" names; plain is grimmer.
- **Places** are working names: Grennick, Askel Mill, the Salt Road, Hangman's Ford, the Burned Farms, Tollbridge. Places get renamed by what happened there.
- **Faiths and saints** are local and practical: Saint Hollis of the Granary, the Lady of Wells, the Patient Brother.
- **Lords and pretenders** have long formal names nobody uses and short names everyone does.
- Avoid names from famous grim fantasy settings, and avoid edgy compound names (Bloodreaver, Skullgrim). Real brutality wears plain names.

## In-world documents

Each of these is a `stage_secret`, aimed at the character most likely to find or use it:
- **A requisition writ:** signed and sealed, demanding grain from a village that has already been stripped. The signature is a clue to who ordered the famine.
- **A ration ledger:** daily counts that drop steadily, then rise suddenly, which means someone stopped eating, or stopped being counted.
- **A bounty notice:** the party's ally, described accurately, with a price higher than the party has ever been paid.
- **A pardon with blanks:** sealed by the crown, name left empty. Worth more than a castle to the right person.
- **A letter home, never sent:** a soldier's letter describing the burning of a village the party has visited, signed by someone they have met.
- **A conscription roll:** names of a village's young men, with half of them struck out and "dead" written beside them in the same week.
- **A merchant's price list:** salt, bread and medicine at prices that tell the story of the siege month by month.
- **A truce agreement:** carefully worded, with one clause that both sides believe means opposite things.
- **A prophet's broadsheet:** the end of the world announced for next spring, and on the back, a well-organized list of camp duties.

## Delivering lore

- Let documents carry the history. One document should reveal who did the indefensible thing, and why it seemed necessary at the time.
- Give every legend a practical use: the saint's relic is valuable because people will trade food for a touch of it.
- Keep a running count somewhere visible: the price of bread, the number on the gate list. Changes in the count are lore.
