# Grimdark: NPCs

Grimdark NPCs are survivors. Each one has done something to stay alive that they would not defend in peacetime, and each one is still a person. Give every NPC a want, a fear and a tell the DM can perform. The twist keeps them from being a flat villain or a flat saint; the best twists make the party need them and distrust them at once.

## How to use these

- Every useful NPC has a price. Store it in their `secret`, and decide when they will ask for it (usually after the party depends on them).
- Pair NPCs: for every ally whose price is high, offer one whose price is low and whose help is small.
- At least one NPC per arc is simply decent, at real cost to themselves. Protect them from being punished for it every time.

## Archetypes

### The kind mercenary captain
- **Twist:** genuinely kind to her own company, and her company is hungry. Everyone outside it is a resource.
- **Want:** to get all sixty of her people through the winter.
- **Fear:** the day she has to choose which of them to leave behind.
- **Tell:** knows the name of every soldier in her company and none of the villagers she taxes.
- Example: Captain Odile Marsh of the Brindle Company, who buries her dead with their pay in their hands.

### The idealist officer
- **Twist:** his ideals got a village burned last year. He has decided the answer is more ideals, harder.
- **Want:** proof that it was worth it.
- **Fear:** that his men already know it was not.
- **Tell:** quotes the oath of his order at moments of doubt, and his voice drops on the same line every time.

### The quartermaster
- **Twist:** steals from the army's stores, and the theft feeds an orphanage behind the tannery.
- **Want:** to keep both the job and the orphans fed until spring.
- **Fear:** an audit.
- **Tell:** counts everything twice out loud and gets the second count different, on purpose.

### The executioner
- **Twist:** has a conscience and a quota. He gives the condemned a choice of how, and remembers what they chose.
- **Want:** to be paid enough to stop.
- **Fear:** recognizing someone on the scaffold.
- **Tell:** washes his hands in snow before shaking anyone's.

### The deserter
- **Twist:** deserted to warn his home village, which was then burned anyway by his own former unit.
- **Want:** the name of the officer who gave the order.
- **Fear:** being taken back, more than being hanged.
- **Tell:** stands at attention without meaning to when anyone shouts.

### The healer who triages
- **Twist:** decides who lives by who will be most useful to the town afterward, and is usually right.
- **Want:** someone else to make the choice, just once.
- **Fear:** that she has stopped feeling it.
- **Tell:** asks every patient their trade before asking where it hurts.
- Example: Sister Abra Vane, who keeps a list of everyone she chose not to treat and reads it on feast days.

### The inquisitor's clerk
- **Twist:** forges confessions to minor crimes so the accused get a flogging instead of the fire.
- **Want:** to save one more before he is caught.
- **Fear:** that the inquisitor already knows and is letting him do it, for a reason.
- **Tell:** ink on every finger and a sudden stammer when asked about procedure.

### The noble in exile
- **Twist:** the claim to the throne is real, and so is the fact that she would be a worse ruler than the usurper.
- **Want:** her name restored, and with it the lands that fed her family.
- **Fear:** being useful to someone only as a banner.
- **Tell:** thanks servants elaborately and never learns their names.

### The fixer
- **Twist:** sells to every side, keeps scrupulous honesty with each, and has quietly decided which side will win.
- **Want:** to still be needed when the war ends.
- **Fear:** peace that comes too suddenly.
- **Tell:** never sits with his back to a door, and always sits with his back to the window.

### The prophet of the ditch
- **Twist:** preaches the end of the world to refugees, and organizes them better than any lord. The prophecy is a lie; the organization is real.
- **Want:** to keep his people together long enough to reach the coast.
- **Fear:** the moment they no longer need a prophet.
- **Tell:** changes the date of the end whenever the march is ready to move.

### The turncoat
- **Twist:** betrayed the party's side to save his family, and has been feeding that side real intelligence ever since to balance the scales.
- **Want:** forgiveness, or at least a quick death if it comes to that.
- **Fear:** his children learning what he did.
- **Tell:** overpays for everything, as if paying off a debt nobody named.

## Performing them

- Grimdark NPCs talk about cost constantly and without drama: prices, rations, distances, the dead.
- Give each a ritual of decency (a burial custom, a promise always kept, a refusal to curse) so the party can see what they are holding onto.
- Let them make the case for their worst act in one sentence, and make the sentence convincing.
