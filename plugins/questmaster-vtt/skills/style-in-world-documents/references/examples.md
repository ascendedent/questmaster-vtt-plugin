# In-world documents: weak vs strong

Five pairs. The weak versions are what a first draft usually produces; the strong ones keep a real author with a real slant, and a truth layer the DM can run.

## 1. The encyclopedia voice

**Weak**

> The Order of the Waystaff was founded two hundred years ago to protect pilgrims on the shrine road. Over time it became corrupt and now extorts the travelers it once protected.

**Strong**

Source: a road-tithe token issued by the Order, stamped on thin brass, carried by every pilgrim.

> By grace of the Staff, the bearer has paid the road-tithe of three silver and walks under the Order's protection from the Mere Gate to the Shrine. Let none hinder the bearer. Let the bearer hinder none. The tithe is owed again at the Shrine for the road home.

**Why:** The weak text hands the players the twist; the strong one lets them notice that protection is charged in both directions and draw the conclusion themselves.

## 2. Costume archaism

**Weak**

> Hearken ye, for in ye olden dayes the kinge didst ride forth and smite ye foul wyrme most valiantly, and verily it was slain.

**Strong**

> In the ninth year of Queen Ysmay, in the month of salting, the beast came down upon the herds of Coldwater. The Queen rode out with forty lances, of whom eleven returned, and the beast did not come down again.

**Why:** Period feel comes from formulas, reckonings and measures, not misspelling; and "eleven returned" and "did not come down again" (rather than "was slain") are details the party can question.

## 3. A narrator who knows too much

**Weak**

> Today the steward poisoned the baron, though no one suspects him, and he hid the vial behind the chapel's third pew.

**Strong**

Source: the diary of Wenlow, the baron's chambermaid.

> The baron took ill after supper again, the same as Tuesday. Steward Alric sat up with him all night and would not let me take a turn. In the morning the chapel smelled of burnt sugar, which I put down to the new candles.

**Why:** The diarist reports what she saw and misreads it; the players link the steward, the chapel and the smell for themselves.

## 4. A truth layer too vague to run

**Weak**

> DM truth layer: some of the hymn is true and some is not. The priests have their reasons.

**Strong**

> - True: Saint Ammet ended the fever by walling the sick quarter shut for forty days. Most of the people inside died.
> - Distorted: the hymn says she "carried the fever out in her own arms". The temple traded a quarantine for a miracle.
> - Why: the temple's land deeds rest on her sainthood, and the families of the walled quarter still live in the parish.
> - Points to: the old poor-roll in the temple undercroft, which lists every household that was walled in.

**Why:** A truth layer only works at the table if it names the fact, the lie, the motive and where the proof is kept.

## 5. A lecture instead of a lead

**Weak**

> Three paragraphs on the founding of a mountain hold, its kings, its wars and its decline, with no name, date or place the party could follow.

**Strong**

Source: a bronze dedication plate set into the hold's outer gate.

> This gate was raised by the hands of Clan Brask for the Lord of Highmoor, who paid in full. The second key rests with the Warden of the Lower Halls.

DM truth layer: the lord never paid. Clan Brask kept the second key as security and wrote "paid in full" to shame him in public. The Warden's post is empty; the key went into the last Warden's tomb.

**Why:** Two lines on a gate give the party a second key and a Warden to go and find; the lecture gives them nothing to do.

## Patterns across the pairs

- Every strong text has an author the players could name.
- Every strong text leaves out something the truth layer puts back.
- Every strong truth layer ends with a place, a person or an object.
- None of the strong texts is longer than its weak twin. Voice is not word count.

## Quick source tags to start from

Each one already carries a slant. Pick one, name the author, then write in that person's voice.

- A tax assessor's report, written to justify a raise in the levy.
- A deserter's letter home, written to explain why he is not coming back.
- A shrine's miracle book, kept to attract pilgrims and their offerings.
- A rival guild's price list, posted to undercut the old guild.
- A grave marker, paid for by the person who caused the death.
- A ship's log, rewritten by the mate after the captain went overboard.
