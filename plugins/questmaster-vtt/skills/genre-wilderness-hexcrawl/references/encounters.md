# Wilderness Hexcrawl: encounters

Wilderness encounters are situations already in motion when the party arrives, and most should be survivable without fighting. Spend prep on the situation and the terrain; let QuestMaster rate any fight that does happen (`rate_encounter`, `propose_encounter`).

## Design principles

- **Signs before sightings.** Tracks, spoor, a stripped carcass, smoke, silence where birds should be. The party gets a chance to avoid, prepare or hunt.
- **Distance first.** Open country starts encounters far off. Decide what the other side is doing and wants before deciding if it is a fight.
- **Terrain is the tactic.** The river, the scree slope and the dry grass decide more than the stat block.
- **Name two outs.** Flee, hide, bribe with food, sacrifice the cart, climb, cross water. Write at least two before play.
- **Cost over damage.** The best wilderness result is a choice between losing time, losing supply or taking a risk.

## The encounter check, kept clean

- Roll once per watch the party is exposed (twice in dangerous country), not once per hex.
- Read every result as one of four things: nothing; a sign; a local event (weather turn, rockfall, a herd crossing); a creature or group at a distance, doing something.
- When it is a creature, also pick its activity: hunting, fleeing something worse, wounded, guarding young, migrating, feeding, already fighting something else.
- A result that has happened gets struck or changed. The second wolf pack is the first one, hungrier, following the party.

## Combat situations

### The ford ambush
- **Situation:** raiders hit when the cart is midstream and the party is split across two banks.
- **Stakes:** the cart and a week of food.
- **Complications:** the current drags at anyone who stops moving; one raider is cutting the mules loose.
- **Outs:** cut the cart free and let it go; fight from the far bank; shout an offer, because the raiders are hungry, not murderous.

### The only water
- **Situation:** a territorial beast holds the one spring within a day in any direction.
- **Stakes:** water for the next two days.
- **Complications:** it is guarding young, and killing it dooms them. A second spring lies past the scree at the cost of half a day.
- **Outs:** wait until it drinks and leaves at dusk; draw it off with fresh meat; take the long way and tick the supply.

### The stampede
- **Situation:** a herd panicked by something bigger pours down the valley toward the camp.
- **Stakes:** mounts, tents and anyone asleep.
- **Complications:** the real threat arrives a minute behind the herd and is hunting, not fleeing.
- **Outs:** reach the rock outcrop; ride with the herd; use the dust as cover to slip away from the hunter.

## Social situations

### The toll at the broken bridge
- **Situation:** three armed people have built a gate on a ruined bridge and want payment. They are refugees, not bandits.
- **Stakes:** the crossing, or a day's detour.
- **Complications:** a second family claims the same bridge and arrives mid-negotiation.
- **Outs:** pay; help them repair it for free passage; find the shallows they do not want found.

### The other campfire
- **Situation:** at dusk, a fire half a mile off. Rival survey, herders, a deserter, or something that has learned fires attract travelers.
- **Stakes:** information, allies, or a night of not sleeping.
- **Complications:** whoever it is has already seen the party's fire.
- **Outs:** approach openly, scout first, douse the fire and move camp in the dark.

### The silent trade
- **Situation:** a flat stone at a pass holds goods left for trade. Take something, leave something of fair value.
- **Stakes:** a needed item (rope, salt, a map scrap) and the goodwill of people never seen.
- **Complications:** what counts as fair value is not obvious, and taking without leaving gets remembered.
- **Outs:** leave something personal; leave nothing and take nothing; wait and watch who comes.

## Exploration situations

### Lost in the fog
- **Situation:** a failed navigation roll; the party walked for hours and the landmarks are wrong.
- **Stakes:** a day of supply, and where they come out.
- **Complications:** being lost puts them somewhere new, never back at the start.
- **Outs:** climb for a vista when the fog lifts; follow water downhill; trust the mules.

### The burn
- **Situation:** a mile of blackened forest from last year's fire, visible for days.
- **Stakes:** no forage or shade, but tracks show perfectly in the ash.
- **Complications:** something walked through recently on two legs, barefoot, in a straight line.
- **Outs:** go around (half a day), cross fast, or follow the tracks.

### The rising river
- **Situation:** rain in the hills the party cannot see; the river climbs while they watch.
- **Stakes:** cross now in danger, or wait three days for it to drop.
- **Complications:** the rival is on the other side and will not wait.

## Puzzle situations

### The map that lies
- **Situation:** a found map is accurate except where the land changed: the river shifted, the tower fell, the lake dried to a meadow.
- **Play:** let the players reconcile the old map with the vistas they see; each correct match reveals one site the map marks that nobody living has visited.

### The waystones
- **Situation:** carved stones along old routes carry a symbol code for water, danger, shelter and do not stop here.
- **Play:** one symbol per stone; each confirmed meaning (by surviving it) unlocks the rest of the route. The code is a stage-able secret for the character who studies old tongues.

## Escalation ladders

- **Supply:** full, tight, short, desperate. At tight, forage becomes a choice; at short, someone has to go hungry; at desperate, every encounter is about food.
- **Pursuit:** signs, shadowing, contact, hunted. Move up one rung per day the party ignores it.
- **Weather:** clear, turning, bad, dangerous. Announce the next rung a watch before it arrives so the party can plan.
