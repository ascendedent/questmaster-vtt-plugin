# Wilderness Hexcrawl: pitfalls

Each failure mode below is common at real tables. Each comes with the fix to apply in prep.

## The empty-hex slog
- **Symptom:** the party crosses hex after hex of "trees, more trees", and the players start checking their phones.
- **Fix:** fast-forward empty stretches in one sentence and spend the table's time only on watches with a choice in them. Every region needs a landmark visible from the next one, so even an empty day moves the party toward something.

## The fight treadmill
- **Symptom:** every encounter roll becomes combat, travel becomes a string of skirmishes, and the party rests after each.
- **Fix:** write tables where at least half the entries are signs, events or meetings. When a creature appears, roll its activity first. A wounded bear fleeing a forest fire is not a fight unless the party makes it one.

## Bookkeeping overload
- **Symptom:** tracking pounds of food, arrows, encumbrance and per-hex speed eats the session.
- **Fix:** count supply in days and one scarce item in uses. Ask for one tick per day. If a number is not creating a decision, stop tracking it.

## No reason to leave town
- **Symptom:** a beautiful sandbox and a party that sits in the tavern asking what to do.
- **Fix:** give every expedition a charter with a deadline, and seed rumors that point at three different sites. Players explore to answer questions, not because the map is there.

## Weather as a damage roll
- **Symptom:** storms and cold just subtract hit points, so players stop caring about weather.
- **Fix:** let weather change plans: fog blocks navigation, rain floods the ford, heat forces night travel. Announce it before the route choice so it matters to the choice.

## Geography as a railroad
- **Symptom:** every direction except the intended one is "impassable cliffs".
- **Fix:** make other routes costly, not closed. The cliffs can be climbed with a day and a rope; the swamp can be crossed with a guide who wants a favor. Players who pay the cost should get somewhere.

## Overprepping the whole map
- **Symptom:** the DM writes 200 hexes and burns out before session 3.
- **Fix:** prep the base, the first region in full, and one line per region beyond it. Expand each region the session before the party is likely to enter it. Prep what the party can see from where they stand.

## Tables that never change
- **Symptom:** the same goblin patrol rolls up for the fourth time, and nothing about the world has moved.
- **Fix:** strike or mutate an entry once it happens. The goblins return as the ones the party spared, now with a grudge or a deal. Rewrite each table after every faction move.

## Lost as a punishment loop
- **Symptom:** failed navigation means walking back to where they started, and the session goes nowhere.
- **Fix:** being lost always costs supply and always delivers the party somewhere new. Lost is a discovery engine with a price, never a reset.

## Survival features trivialize travel
- **Symptom:** one character's wilderness feature or spell removes every pressure.
- **Fix:** let it answer the question it is built for (finding food, not getting lost) and keep the other pressures live: weather, deadlines, rivals, pursuit, the cost of the route. Make that character the hero of the problem they solve instead of ignoring it.

## Flight and teleportation skip the map
- **Symptom:** by mid levels the party flies or teleports past the whole frontier.
- **Fix:** keep the reasons to walk: you cannot teleport somewhere nobody has seen, the survey requires ground truth, and the herders only talk to people who arrive on foot. Shift play toward what is found rather than how to get there.

## The trip home skipped
- **Symptom:** the find is reached and the next session starts back in town.
- **Fix:** the return is where consequences land. Something follows, someone is hurt, the weather turned. Give the way home one beat even if it is short.

## Assuming the shape of the land
- **Symptom:** prep assumes a place is on the coast or by a lake, and the campaign's continent map says otherwise.
- **Fix:** `get_continent_gazetteer` gives names, bearings and rough positions but not coastlines or water. Ask the DM before placing a harbor, a beach or a lake shore, and build discoveries on the land that the map already shows.

## Rumors that are always true
- **Symptom:** players treat every rumor as a quest marker.
- **Fix:** each region gets one wrong rumor, wrong in an interesting way (the "dragon" is a herder clan's kite festival; the "gold" is pyrite, but the mine beneath it is real). Note which is which in the secret's source.

## The whole map shown on day one
- **Symptom:** the players see every region, site and road at the first session, and nothing is left to discover.
- **Fix:** show only what the party has seen or been told. Map annotations start hidden and reveal as the party sees them; rumors arrive as staged secrets. Let the players' own map be incomplete and partly wrong.

## Review checklist before a travel session
- Is there a choice of route with different costs in the first ten minutes?
- Is the supply count written down and readable by the players?
- Does every region the party might enter have a visible landmark and a filtered table?
- Does the session end on a decision, not a fight?
