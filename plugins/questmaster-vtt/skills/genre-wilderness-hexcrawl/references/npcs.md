# Wilderness Hexcrawl: NPCs

People in the wild are scarce, so each one matters. Every NPC on the road should know something about the land the party does not, want something the party has, and be a reason to come back to a place on the map.

## How to use these

- Seed 1 or 2 per region, not more. A crowded wilderness stops feeling wild.
- Tie every NPC to a location (`upsert_location`) and, where it fits, a faction. Store the twist in the NPC's `secret`, the tell in their notes.
- Give each one a piece of route knowledge: a shortcut, a safe camp, which spring is bad. That knowledge is what the party trades for.

## Archetypes

### The guide who always turns back
- **Twist:** knows the first half of every route by heart and has never crossed Crowfoot Ford. She hires on hoping one party will be strong enough to carry her past her own fear.
- **Want:** to see what lies past the ford, and to be paid in full.
- **Fear:** that the party learns she has never been there and leaves her behind.
- **Tell:** gives precise detail about the near country (which spring is brackish, where the ewes lamb), then switches to proverbs about the far side.
- Example: Marrit Selle, who touches her boot knife every time someone says "ford".

### The last garrison
- **Twist:** a sergeant and two soldiers still hold a border tower for a crown that dissolved eleven years ago. They collect tolls, log travelers and send reports nobody reads.
- **Want:** relief, orders, and proof that the reports arrive somewhere.
- **Fear:** hearing aloud that nobody is coming.
- **Tell:** demands the password and accepts any answer given with confidence; stamps every paper with a seal worn smooth.
- Use: their logbook is a decade of who passed this way, priceless to any mystery.

### The rival who is losing
- **Twist:** the rival expedition is better funded and two days ahead, and has lost a third of its people to something its leader will not name.
- **Want:** to reach the site first and not come home alone.
- **Fear:** that her backers learn the cost.
- **Tell:** counts heads out loud at every halt and speaks of the missing in the present tense.
- Example: Ysolde Carrow, who offers an alliance with one condition she will not explain.

### The trapper who feeds the problem
- **Twist:** the beast troubling the valley stays because he leaves bait for it. He sells its shed quills to an alchemist downriver and does not want it dead.
- **Want:** the party to drive it off, never kill it.
- **Fear:** the settlers learning why it never leaves.
- **Tell:** knows exactly where the beast will be, and is never once surprised by it.

### The waystation host with two ledgers
- **Twist:** he sells guests' routes to the bandits and pays the bandits to leave his guests alive. His inn is the safest on the road because he runs the danger.
- **Want:** steady traffic and no surprises.
- **Fear:** a new bandit chief who will not take his money.
- **Tell:** asks every guest which road they take tomorrow and writes it down "for the weather book".

### The surveyor who stayed
- **Twist:** the missing surveyor the party was hired to find is alive, has married into a hill clan, and will not go back. Her maps were drawn wrong on purpose to hide the valley.
- **Want:** to be reported dead.
- **Fear:** her old patron's men finding the pass.
- **Tell:** ink-stained fingers she scrubs with ash; draws in the dirt while she talks, then wipes it out.

### The muleteer who listens to mules
- **Twist:** every superstition she has about her animals is correct, because she reads their behavior better than any ranger reads tracks.
- **Want:** her mules home alive. She will say plainly that she values a mule above a stranger.
- **Fear:** the stretch of road where the whole string stopped and refused, last spring.
- **Tell:** asks the lead mule, Pepper, for its opinion at every fork, and waits for an answer.

### The child scout
- **Twist:** the kid tagging along for coppers is a lookout from a hidden settlement, sent to judge whether the party is dangerous.
- **Want:** to give a good report and to hold a real sword once.
- **Fear:** being the reason strangers find home.
- **Tell:** asks two party members the same question and compares the answers.

### The clan elder who wants a witness
- **Twist:** the herders hold older title to the land than the company that claims it, and the elder wants outsiders to witness the boundary stones before the company moves them.
- **Want:** the party to walk the boundary and put their names to it.
- **Fear:** violence that gives the company an excuse.
- **Tell:** measures distance in songs ("the ridge is four verses away") and is always right.

### The deserter with orders
- **Twist:** he fled a company patrol because he was ordered to burn a winter camp. He still carries the written order, which is evidence.
- **Want:** to put the order in honest hands, or sell it to whoever pays.
- **Fear:** hanging before anyone reads it.
- **Tell:** wears his uniform coat inside out, buttons and all.

### The ferryman who charges in news
- **Twist:** his ferry is the only crossing for forty miles. He takes no coin: the fare is one piece of news he has not heard, and he knows everything that has crossed in ten years.
- **Want:** to be the one who knows.
- **Fear:** a bridge.
- **Tell:** repeats your news back to you, slightly improved, and you hear it that way at the next village.

## Quick voices for the road

- Speaks in landmarks, never miles: "past the split oak, before the white water".
- Hums while walking and stops dead when the birds stop.
- Asks what you ate today before asking your name.
- Refuses to say the name of a mountain aloud and uses "the old man" instead.
