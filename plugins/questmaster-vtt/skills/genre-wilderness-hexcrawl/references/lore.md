# Wilderness Hexcrawl: lore

Wilderness lore is layered: the land, then the people who passed through, then the people who named it, then the people who renamed it. Show the layers through objects and place names, not paragraphs.

## Lore textures

- **Three eras on one hillside.** A cairn older than anyone remembers, a collapsed watchtower from a vanished kingdom, and last year's campfire ring. Every site should show at least two eras.
- **Names that drifted.** The herders call the river the Slow Mother; the company map says Brennock Water; the garrison log says River 4. Each name tells you who is speaking.
- **Maps that disagree.** No two maps of a region match. Each error points at the mapmaker's interest (the company's map omits the clan cairns; the monastery's map omits the hidden valley).
- **Folk rules with reasons.** "Never camp where the grass is short" (because elk graze where predators cannot ambush, and so do predators). Give players sayings they can test.
- **Seasonal truth.** A meadow in summer is a lake in spring. A pass is a road in July and a grave in November.

## Sensory palette

**Sights**
- A single crow following the party for three days, always one tree behind.
- Fence posts with no fence, marching off over a hill.
- Frost on the inside of the tent.
- A cliff face scored with old tool marks in neat rows.
- Smoke rising straight up in still air: someone is cooking and not hiding.

**Sounds**
- Ice on the lake ticking and booming as the sun warms it.
- The wind changing pitch as it crosses the ridge, a long low note at dusk.
- A bell somewhere in the forest, rung once, never again.
- Hooves on stone where there should be grass.
- Total silence from the frogs, all at once.

**Smells**
- Resin and wet ash from an old burn.
- Sour mud and rotting reeds in the flats.
- Sheep, woodsmoke and cheese: a herders' camp half a mile upwind.
- Snow coming: a clean, metallic edge in the air an hour before it falls.
- Carrion, sweet and heavy, in a gully nobody wants to check.

**Textures**
- Sleeves stiff with dried river mud.
- Rope gone slick with frost.
- Lichen that crumbles to powder under a boot.
- Hot spring water that tastes of eggs and makes skin feel soapy.
- A carved trail marker worn smooth by a hundred palms.

**Weather vocabulary**
- Fog that sits in the valleys while the ridges are clear.
- Sleet that turns the ground to grease by noon.
- A dry wind that cracks lips and spooks horses.
- Thunderheads building over the peaks every afternoon at the same hour.

## Naming guidance

- **Describe what it looks like, in plain words:** the Knuckles, Splitwater, the Lean, Old Smoke, Ninefold Falls.
- **Name for what happened there:** Tallow Flats (a supply caravan burned), Saltwife Spring (someone's wife set salt out for the dead), the Long Wait (a pass where a party was snowed in).
- **Layer names by who speaks:** give each major feature a local name, a company name and an old name. Example: the Slow Mother, Brennock Water, and on the oldest stones a word nobody can read.
- **Keep names short and sayable.** Players will repeat them for months; two syllables beat five.
- **Avoid naming everything.** Unnamed places feel more unexplored. Let the party name what they discover and write their name into the location.

## In-world documents

- **A surveyor's field book page:** bearings, a sketch of a ridge, a note in the margin ("water foul here, do not let the horses drink"), and a last entry that stops mid-line.
- **A company handbill:** "Land for the Taking in the Sallowmere. Surveys Paid in Silver." The fine print names the filing office and the first-survey clause.
- **A trapper's tally stick:** notches for pelts, a different notch for something he stopped counting.
- **Waystone graffiti:** generations of travelers' marks, the newest one a warning scratched over an older welcome.
- **A cairn letter:** a note left under a stone for whoever follows: directions, a debt owed, a plea to tell someone's mother.
- **A garrison log:** ten years of traveler entries, then a page of tally marks with no names.
- **A child's map:** drawn by a scout from the hidden settlement, wildly out of scale and completely accurate about where the dangers are.

## Making discoveries feel earned

- Reveal a site through three hints before arrival: a rumor, a sign on the trail, a vista.
- Give each find one thing the party can carry home as proof (a carved stone, a stuffed bird nobody has seen, a page from a dead surveyor) and one thing they cannot (a view, a feeling, a door too heavy to open yet).
