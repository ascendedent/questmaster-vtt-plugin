# Cosmic Horror: Pitfalls

Cosmic horror fails in two directions: the threat becomes a monster to punch, or the players become helpless spectators. Each pitfall below has a fix you can apply to a draft before the DM sees it.

## Structure pitfalls

### The stalled investigation
- **Symptom:** the party missed the one clue and the session grinds to a halt.
- **Fix:** three clues per conclusion, in three places, found by three methods (talk, search, study). If all three are missed, the next brush with the edge delivers the conclusion with a cost attached.

### The entity as a boss fight
- **Symptom:** the finale is a damage race against the cosmic thing, with hit points.
- **Fix:** the finale is a closing, not a kill. The party fights servants and cultists while doing something else (the ritual in reverse, burning the archive, holding the line until dawn). The thing is a thread and a set of effects, never a stat block.

### Total loss as the only ending
- **Symptom:** every session ends with the party worse off and nothing saved. Players disengage.
- **Fix:** write the small win for every session before play. Make it concrete and achievable, and let it land fully when earned.

### The lore dump
- **Symptom:** a scholar explains the cosmology in a speech.
- **Fix:** nobody in the world understands the whole. Split the truth across documents, witnesses and sites, each partial and each a little wrong.

### Revealing too much, too soon
- **Symptom:** the party sees the thing in session one and it never scares them again.
- **Fix:** keep the entity to effects and edges. Each glimpse shows a little more and never the whole. If the DM wants a dramatic reveal, reveal its scale, not its shape.

## Mechanics pitfalls

### Constant sanity rolls
- **Symptom:** every strange thing prompts a save, the dice drown the dread, and the outcomes feel random.
- **Fix:** costs come from choices, not ambushes. A mark is taken when an investigator chooses to read, look or understand, at a revelation, and the player picks which mark from two or three.

### Knowledge with no reward
- **Symptom:** learning only hurts, so smart players stop investigating.
- **Fix:** every mark grants something useful (a sense, a ward, a weakness, a way in). Knowledge is damage and power at once.

### The death spiral
- **Symptom:** a character with marks gets worse until they are unplayable.
- **Fix:** cap marks per character, offer a way to retire a marked character with dignity, and give the small faith or a survivor NPC a way to ease one mark at a real price.

## Tone pitfalls

### Apostrophe names and tentacles
- **Symptom:** every entity is a pile of consonants and every manifestation is tentacles.
- **Fix:** use titles and plain phrases (see lore.md). Describe wrongness through measurement, sound and absence: water flat on a slope, birds that sing it back correctly.

### Random weirdness
- **Symptom:** strange things happen with no pattern, and players stop trying to understand them.
- **Fix:** give each site and each manifestation two or three consistent rules. Players who learn the rules can survive; that is what makes the investigation matter.

### Cultists as robes
- **Symptom:** cultists are faceless evil people to be killed without thought.
- **Fix:** every cult has members with names, children and reasons. Some can be saved, some will surrender, some were recruited by kindness the party failed to offer.

### Madness as a joke or a reveal
- **Symptom:** a character "goes insane" with stock behavior, or an NPC's real-world mental illness is the twist.
- **Fix:** marks are specific supernatural effects, not depictions of mental illness. See safety.md.

## Player experience pitfalls

### Helpless investigators
- **Symptom:** nothing the party does changes the outcome; they are witnesses to a story.
- **Fix:** offer an exit at every loop, small wins every session, and at least one faction whose fate depends on the party's choices.

### The one smart player
- **Symptom:** the player who likes puzzles solves everything; others drift.
- **Fix:** aim clues at different characters through `stage_secret`, so the fighter finds the tracks, the cleric reads the sermon and the rogue lifts the letter.

### Ignoring the characters' lives
- **Symptom:** the investigation is abstract; the party has nothing personal at stake.
- **Fix:** read `get_party` and tie one clue or one victim to a character's past as a hook the player can pull. Never rewrite a backstory without the DM's say.
