# Cosmic Horror: Lore

Cosmic lore is about measurement failing. Distances, dates, counts and names stop agreeing with each other. The most effective lore is mundane paperwork with one impossible detail in it, written by someone competent who did not expect what they found.

## Lore textures

- **Wrong measurement.** A road that is longer walking east than walking west. A well that is deeper than the hill it sits in. A census with one more family than there are houses.
- **Recurrence.** It has happened before, at regular intervals, and someone wrote it down each time without knowing about the others.
- **Indifference.** The thing is not aimed at anyone. Its effects are like weather: they fall on the good and the guilty alike.
- **Contagion of knowing.** Understanding spreads it. Copies of the book are worse than the original because more people read them.
- **Old competence.** Previous investigators were clever and careful. Their notes are good. That is what makes their last entries frightening.
- **Local adaptation.** People who live near it have built habits that work without knowing why: no fishing on the ninth day, reed charms in every window, children taught to count backward.

## Sensory palette

### Sights
- Shadows that point a few degrees off from the light.
- Water lying flat and still on a slope.
- Stars that are slightly closer together over the site than anywhere else.
- A crowd where every person blinks at the same moment.
- A building whose windows are all the same size from every distance.

### Sounds
- A low tone felt in the teeth rather than heard.
- Birds singing a melody back correctly the second time.
- Footsteps that keep pace a half beat behind your own.
- A congregation saying a prayer, and one voice saying it backward underneath.
- Total silence in a marsh at midday.

### Smells
- Wet stone and copper in a dry room.
- Salt far from any sea.
- Something like ozone after the lightning, with no storm.
- Old ink, and under it something sweet and organic.

### Textures and temperature
- Surfaces that are warm on the side away from the fire.
- Stone that gives slightly under pressure, like a gum.
- Paper that feels damp but leaves the fingers dry.
- A cold that comes from inside the chest rather than the air.

## Naming guidance

- **Entities do not have proper names the party can pronounce.** Give them titles, descriptions or numbers used by the people who fear them: the Fourth Listener, What Answers in the Well, the Unmeasured Valley, the Guest Beneath the Weir.
- **Avoid apostrophes and unpronounceable consonant clusters.** They read as parody and echo famous published entities. A plain phrase is more frightening than an invented word.
- **Places are mundane, with one telling word:** Ebbing Cross, Marrow Fen, the Ninth Mile Inn, Low Chapel, Hollins Quarry.
- **Institutions sound respectable and dull:** the Cartographers' Hall of Brannock, the Board of Sanitary Works, the Society for Antique Survey.
- **Cults name themselves after something kind:** the Ninefold Choir, the Common Table, the Neighbors, the Keepers of the Small Light.
- **Rites and texts are named by their function or their author:** the Varrow Marginalia, the Last Survey, the Ninth Verse, the Brannock Rule.
- Test a name: if it would appear on a shop sign or a government form without anyone blinking, it is right.

## In-world documents

Each of these is a `stage_secret`, aimed at the character most likely to find or understand it:
- **Field notes:** careful, dated, with measurements. The last entries keep the format and lose the sense ("Depth of pool: 11 ft. Depth of pool from the other side: yes.").
- **A corrected map:** an old map with a newer hand adding a valley, a road or a lake, and a note: "verified".
- **A census or tithe roll:** one household listed every year for a century, always with the same names and ages.
- **Marginalia:** a scholar's notes in the margins of a dull book, gradually arguing with the main text and then with each other.
- **An engineer's report:** a calm, technical account of why a bridge cannot stand, which it has, for eighty years.
- **A child's drawing:** the house, the family, the sun, and the thing standing behind them, drawn with the same care as the dog.
- **A sermon:** a priest's last sermon, kind and sensible, with one paragraph that is not in his handwriting.
- **A letter that arrives late:** posted years ago by an investigator, describing the party by name.
- **An auction catalogue:** dry lot descriptions of terrible objects, with provenance notes that form a chain back to one site.

## Delivering lore

- One document per scene, and make the players work for the second half.
- Every document has a competent author. Their competence is what makes the impossible detail land.
- Let one document be wrong in good faith; the truth lies in comparing it to another.
- Never write the entity's perspective. The closest the party gets is a human who tried to describe it and failed.
- Use a `save_monitor_preset` for a reveal handout so the DM can put the corrected map on the table screen at the right moment.
