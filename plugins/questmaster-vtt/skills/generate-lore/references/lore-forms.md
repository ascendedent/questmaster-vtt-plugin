# Lore forms

Every piece has two layers: the in-world text (what players meet) and the DM truth layer
(what is actually so). Write the header, one form below, then the truth layer.

## Header

- **Subject**: the place, person, event, object, deity, creature or phenomenon.
- **Form**: history, myth, prophecy, rumor, document, doctrine.
- **Source**: who wrote or speaks it, and what they want people to believe.
- **Delivery**: book, NPC speech, inscription, dream, song, official record, fragment.
- **Era**: when this version was made or last revised.

The source's voice, bias and blind spots must show. A temple record sounds different from a
dockside rumor; a conqueror's chronicle sounds different from the conquered's lament.

## History or chronicle (2 to 5 paragraphs)

**[Title]**, attributed to [author or institution], circa [in-world date].

Bias is a feature: a victor's history omits the massacre, a monastery's record credits
divine help, a merchant's log counts the cost in grain. Leave gaps where the source would.

> In the ninth year of the Margrave's peace the river rose, as it had been warned it would,
> and the faithful of the Weir Chapel were spared, the flood parting at our threshold.

(The truth layer can note the chapel sits on the only high ground in the parish.)

## Myth or legend (1 to 3 paragraphs)

**The Tale of [subject]**, as told by [a people, a tradition, a grandmother].

Elevated or folk register, symbols, compressed time, gods or spirits acting directly. A myth
need not be false; it must be told mythically.

**Common variants** (two at least):
- In [region]: how the story changes there.
- Among [group]: their version, and what it says about them.

## Prophecy (4 to 8 lines)

**[Opening line as title]**. Source: [the seer or tradition]. First recorded: [when, where].

Archaic phrasing, concrete images, deliberate ambiguity. It must support more than one
reading, and none of those readings may decide what a player character does.

> When the bell is rung by no hand,
> and the ford runs the wrong way home,
> the one who counts the cages
> shall open the last of them.

**Known interpretations**:
- [Faction or scholar] reads it as: ...
- [Another] reads it as: ...
- **The dangerous reading**: the one that, believed, makes someone do something terrible.
  This is usually the thread.

## Rumor or saying (1 to 2 sentences)

*As heard in [location]*: "[colloquial, short, the kind of thing said over a cup]."
- Spread: widely known, known in certain circles, or rare.
- Who repeats it, and why they enjoy repeating it.
- What it gets right (the grain of truth) and what it gets wrong (the distortion).

Write three versions of one rumor from three mouths when the DM wants the party to compare
notes; each becomes its own staged secret.

## Document: inscription, letter, scroll, ledger (1 to 4 paragraphs)

**[Document type]: [title or opening words]**
*Physical description*: material, condition, handwriting, seals, stains, what's missing.

Match the medium: an inscription is short and formal, a letter is personal and dated, a
ledger is columns and initials, an official notice is all authority and no explanation.
- Legibility: complete, or which parts are damaged or missing (mark gaps as [illegible]).
- Language or script: what it takes to read it. A check on the party with `get_character`
  shows who knows the language, and that character is the staged secret's audience.

## Doctrine (a short creed plus 3 to 5 tenets)

What the faithful must believe and do, in the faith's own words, with one tenet that sounds
harmless and is not. The clergy's private reading goes in the faction's `realAgenda`.

## DM truth layer (never shown to players)

- **What is true**: the actual history or metaphysics, plainly.
- **What is distorted**: what the source got wrong, left out or falsified.
- **Why the distortion persists**: the institutional, personal or historical reason.
- **Campaign connections**: the active thread it touches; the NPC who knows the real
  version (or a third version); what the party finds if they dig.
- **Discovery**: the skill that knows of it (History, Arcana, Religion, Nature) and how hard
  (easy, moderate, hard; the 2024 rules' typical DCs are 10, 15 and 20, and the DM picks).
  How to find it: research, a contact, a place. What a great result adds.

## Delivery notes

- Read aloud: which lines verbatim, which to paraphrase.
- From an NPC: their voice, emotional register and what they leave out (`style-npc-voice`).
- As an object: what the DM hands over; whether to print it (`style-handouts`).
- Best moment: the story state that makes it land (after the party has seen the bell, not
  before).

## Generic vs specific

| Generic: rewrite it | Specific: aim here |
|---|---|
| "An ancient evil was sealed away long ago." | "The chapel's founding stone lists twelve donors; the thirteenth name has been chiseled out, and the chisel marks are newer than the stone." |
| "Legends say the forest is cursed." | "Woodcutters leave the first log of every load at the treeline, and nobody remembers who started it." |
| "The prophecy speaks of a chosen one." | "The prophecy names a job, not a person: 'the one who counts the cages'. Three people in town have held that job." |

The test: if the text could open any fantasy book, it is generic. Anchor it to a name, an
object or a place in this campaign.
