# Read-aloud: weak vs strong

Five pairs. Each weak version is a common first draft; each strong version fixes it without growing past four sentences.

## 1. Measurements before senses

**Weak**

> You enter a 40 by 30 foot room with four pillars and a door on the north wall. The room is dark and eerie, and you feel like you are being watched.

**Strong**

> Cold air moves out of the dark toward you, carrying the smell of lamp oil and old bread. Your light finds four pillars, each scored waist-high with rows of tally marks. On the far wall, a door has been barred from this side, and the bar is new, pale wood.

**Why:** It opens on senses, swaps "eerie" and "being watched" for their causes, and ends on a door that raises a question. (The 40 by 30 and the pillar positions live in the DM notes and on the map.)

## 2. Playing the characters for the players

**Weak**

> Terrified, you rush into the burning chapel and drag the priest out of the flames, coughing as smoke fills your lungs.

**Strong**

> Smoke pours from the chapel's open doors, and the heat reaches you from across the square. Inside, through the haze, a man in grey vestments is dragging a heavy chest toward the altar, away from the door. Above him a roof beam groans and sags.

**Why:** The weak box decides the characters' fear, courage and actions; the strong one shows danger and a strange choice, and lets the players decide whether to go in.

## 3. Giving away what a check should earn

**Weak**

> In the corner sits a treasure chest, which is actually a mimic waiting to eat whoever touches it.

**Strong**

> The room is bare except for an iron-banded chest in the corner, cleaner than anything else down here. The dust around it is swept in a wide ring, as if something had been dragged, or had dragged itself. Near it hangs a faint sweet smell, like pears left too long in the sun.

DM note: the chest is a mimic. The clean ring and the smell are fair tells for a Perception or Nature check.

**Why:** The box shows what anyone would see and plants fair tells; the creature's identity stays in the DM note for a check to earn.

## 4. A history lecture in the box

**Weak**

> You arrive at Calder's Rest, founded three centuries ago by the exiled knight Ser Halvard Calder after the War of Ashen Banners. The town is known for its wool trade, its two rival temples, and the legend of the drowned bell, which some say still rings on moonless nights, though the elders refuse to speak of it, and the young people...

**Strong**

> Sheep bells clatter all around as a flock is driven through the gate beside you, and the whole street smells of wet wool. Two temples face each other across the square, one freshly whitewashed, one with its door painted over in red. A boy perched on the well calls out an offer to tell you the true story of the bell, for a copper.

**Why:** History belongs to sources the party can question; the box gives three present details and ends with someone offering the lore at a price.

## 5. Ending on mood instead of a move

**Weak**

> The duchess enters the hall. She is beautiful, cold and clearly dangerous. Everyone is afraid of her. The atmosphere is very tense.

**Strong**

> The musicians stop mid-bar. A tall woman in plain black crosses the hall without hurrying, and the guests step out of her path like water from a dropped stone. She stops at your table, sets down a sealed letter with your names written across it, and waits.

**Why:** The room's reaction shows her status, and the last beat (a letter and a silence) hands the players the next move.

## Fast fixes

| The draft says | Do this instead |
|---|---|
| "you feel", "you sense", "a chill runs down your spine" | Describe the cause: the draft, the smell, the sound |
| "you notice" for something hidden | Move it to the DM notes; let a check or a question find it |
| eerie, ominous, creepy, mysterious | One concrete detail that produces the mood |
| "suddenly" | Cut it; let the event happen in a short sentence |
| dimensions and compass points first | Senses first; measurements to the DM notes or the map |
| a list of everything in the room | Pick two details, then end on a third that invites action |
| a monster's game name | What it looks like and what it is doing |
| a lore paragraph | An NPC, a sign or a document that offers the lore |
