# Read-aloud registers

The same scene in five registers. The facts never change; the register changes sentence length, word choice and which details earn a place. Take the register from the campaign's tone in `get_campaign_overview` and hold it for the whole session unless the DM asks for a shift.

## The fixed facts

- A watermill at dusk. The wheel has stopped; the millrace still runs loud.
- A lantern burns inside. Nobody answers.
- Flour on the floor, one line of bare footprints going in, none coming out.
- The door stands open a hand's width. Inside, something creaks in a slow rhythm. (A sack hoist swinging on its rope: the DM knows, the box does not say.)

## Terse OSR

> The millrace roars; the wheel does not turn. Lamplight through a door open a hand's width. Flour on the threshold, bare footprints going in, none coming out. Inside, something creaks, slow and regular.

- **Fits:** sandbox and dungeon-crawl campaigns, deadly games, tables that map and take notes and want information fast so they can decide what matters.
- **Moves:** nouns first, fragments allowed, no metaphors. Every detail is something a player can touch, follow or avoid.
- **Watch for:** going so dry nobody can picture it. Keep one sensory hit (the roar) even at your tersest.

## Lush literary

> Water still pours down the millrace, loud as rain on slate, but the great wheel hangs still and dripping. A lantern burns gold in the one window against the blue evening. Flour lies on the threshold like fine frost, printed with bare feet that go in and do not come out. Beyond the lamp, through a door left a hand's width open, something creaks, slow as breathing.

- **Fits:** atmospheric, gothic, mythic or slow-burn campaigns; tables that enjoy listening and lean in for description.
- **Moves:** at most one simile per sentence, color and light, longer sentences that still end on a concrete noun.
- **Watch for:** the box growing past 70 words. Lush means a precise image, not more of them.

## Pulp

> The big wheel has stopped dead, but the millrace thunders on. Lamplight knifes through a door left a hand's width open. Bare footprints march through the flour, in and never out! Then, from the dark inside: creak. Pause. Creak.

- **Fits:** swashbuckling, two-fisted adventure, heroic action, and tables that want momentum.
- **Moves:** strong verbs (knifes, march, thunders), one exclamation at most, a beat of silence before the hook, an ending on a sound or a motion.
- **Watch for:** exclamation spam and labels like "terrifying". Pulp sells action with verbs, not adjectives.

## Noir

> The wheel's quit, but nobody told the water; the millrace keeps talking. There's a lamp burning inside and a door left open a hand's width, the way people leave doors when they mean to come right back. Bare footprints in the flour, going in, none coming out. Somewhere past the lamp, something creaks, patient as a clock.

- **Fits:** intrigue, city and investigation campaigns, morally grey politics, heists.
- **Moves:** a narrator with an opinion about the world (never about the characters), dry understatement, a detail that implies human behavior (the door left for a return).
- **Watch for:** the narrator's weariness turning into the characters' feelings. "You've seen this before" invents a past; "people leave doors this way" does not.

## Storybook

> Once this mill sang all day long, but tonight its wheel stands still while the water runs on alone. A lantern glows in the window, and the door is open just a little, as if it were waiting for someone. In the flour on the floor are bare footprints, all going in and none coming out. And from inside comes a small sound: creak, and creak, and creak again.

- **Fits:** fairy-tale campaigns, family tables and younger players, first campaigns, whimsical settings that turn dark on purpose.
- **Moves:** gentle personification (the mill sang, the door waits), patterns of three, plain words with a rhythm you could say to a child.
- **Watch for:** cuteness undercutting danger. Storybook can frighten; the softness of the voice is what makes the footprints chilling.

## Register moves at a glance

| Register | Sentence length | Images per box | Typical last beat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Terse OSR | fragments, 3 to 8 words | none | an object or exit |
| Lush literary | long, flowing | two or three | a sound or a light |
| Pulp | short and punchy | one, kinetic | a motion or a threat |
| Noir | medium, wry | one, about people | a quiet wrongness |
| Storybook | medium, rhythmic | one, personified | a pattern of three |

## Choosing a register

| Signal in the tone or the DM's words | Register |
|---|---|
| sandbox, hexcrawl, old-school, deadly, resource tracking | Terse OSR |
| gothic, mythic, atmospheric, slow-burn, melancholy | Lush literary |
| swashbuckling, high adventure, action, cinematic | Pulp |
| intrigue, investigation, city, corruption, heist | Noir |
| fairy tale, family, whimsical, first campaign, younger players | Storybook |

If two registers fit, ask the DM one question with a one-line sample of each: "Should this read more like the first or the second?"

## Shifting register on purpose

- Hold one register per campaign. A shift is a tool; save it for a turn in the story.
- Go terser for the box that starts a fight, in any register. Nobody wants a simile when initiative is rolled.
- A storybook campaign can drop into terse OSR for one box when the wolf turns out to be real. The change of voice is the scare.
- A noir campaign can go lush for one sacred place, to show that it matters to someone.
- Never mix registers inside one box; it reads as a mistake, not a choice.
