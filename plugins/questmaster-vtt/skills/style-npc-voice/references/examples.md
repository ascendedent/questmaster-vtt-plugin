# NPC voice: weak vs strong

Five pairs. Each weak version is a common first draft; each strong one gives the DM something to do with their voice, face or hands.

## 1. Adjectives instead of levers

**Weak**

> Brother Ansel: kind, wise, a little mysterious. Speaks softly.

**Strong**

```
Brother Ansel (almoner of the river chapel)
Voice: slow and formal; a long pause before every answer, as if listening for permission.
Tell: turns the keys on his ring one at a time while thinking; stops turning when he lies.
Habits: answers with a proverb, then the plain version ("Hunger has long ears. People talk while they eat.")
```

**Why:** Kind, wise and mysterious cannot be performed; a pause, a key ring and a proverb habit can, and the tell is wired to the lie.

## 2. Three lines, one job

**Weak**

- "Welcome, welcome! Best stew in the valley!"
- "Travelers! Sit, sit, warm yourselves!"
- "Nothing like a fire on a night like this, eh?"

**Strong** (Marta Venn, innkeeper)

- First impression: "Sit anywhere that isn't wet. Stew's turnip again; the cart didn't come."
- Under pressure: "The cart comes when it comes. You eating or asking?"
- Useful (quietly, once the room empties): "Carter's horse came back on its own Tuesday, still in harness. Nobody went looking. Nobody's asked me why, either."

**Why:** The weak set is one greeting three times; the strong set shows who she is, where she flinches, and hands over a lead.

## 3. A pressure line that confesses

Secret (DM only): Reeve Ottilie Brand was paid to keep the bucket line away from the granary that burned.

**Weak**

> "I never took a single coin to keep the buckets away from that granary, and I resent the suggestion!"

**Strong**

> "The bucket line went where it was needed. Houses first, grain second. Anyone who says different wasn't holding a bucket."
> (Tell: squares the papers on her desk into a neat stack.)

**Why:** The weak line denies something nobody asked about, which is a confession; the strong one defends the choice with a rule the party can test against witnesses.

## 4. Phonetic accent instead of rhythm

**Weak**

> "Oi! Wot's all this then? Ye'll not be gettin' past me wivout a pass, not today ye won't."

**Strong**

> "Pass. No pass, no bridge. I don't make the rules, I stand on them. Bring me paper and I'll stand aside."

Voice note: short sentences, leans on the first word, treats the bridge as his own property.

**Why:** Phonetic spelling is hard to read aloud and slides toward caricature; rhythm and attitude carry the character and leave the accent, if any, to the DM.

## 5. A cast that sounds like one person

**Weak**

- Guard one: "Halt! State your business."
- Guard two: "Halt! What is your business here?"

**Strong**

- Sergeant Pell (clipped, procedural, never uses names): "Purpose of entry. Length of stay. Blades peace-tied inside the walls."
- Tobin (young, rambling, apologizes constantly): "Sorry, sorry, he has to ask, it's the new rules since the fire, it's not you. Here for the fair? Everyone's here for the fair."

**Why:** They differ on pace and register, so the DM can switch between them without saying who is talking, and the chatty one is a natural leak.

## Patterns across the pairs

- Every strong version gives the DM a physical action or a rhythm, not a description.
- Every strong pressure line defends something; none of them explains it.
- Every strong useful line names a concrete thing (a horse, a ford, a rule) the party can follow.
- Every strong voice could be performed by a DM who has never done voices: the levers are pace, words and hands, not acting talent.

## Bank of performable tells

Borrow these when a sheet is missing one; wire each to a trigger.

- Counts coins already counted (trigger: money is mentioned).
- Touches a ring, a scar or a holy symbol (trigger: the past comes up).
- Answers to the window instead of the speaker (trigger: a question they dislike).
- Laughs one beat late (trigger: a joke at someone else's expense).
- Straightens objects on the table (trigger: losing control of the conversation).
- Says the questioner's name back to them, slowly (trigger: a threat).
- Stops eating and holds the spoon still (trigger: a name they recognize).
