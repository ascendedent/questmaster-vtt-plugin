# NPC voice registers

One NPC, the same three lines, five registers. The campaign sets the register; the NPC keeps their own levers (pace, habit, tell) inside it. Watch Odile's habit survive every version: she puts a price on everything, danger included.

## The NPC (fixed in every register)

- **Odile Varrow**, ferry keeper at Tench Crossing.
- Habit: prices everything, danger included.
- Tell: glances at the second mooring rope, the one tied with a different knot, whenever night crossings come up.
- Secret (DM only): she rents the ferry to smugglers after dark and wants no witnesses.
- Never says: who uses the ferry at night. She talks about the river, the marsh and the weather, never about people, because naming them would cost her the arrangement and possibly her neck.
- Facts the lines carry: the ferry is shut until dawn; two coppers a head; lights on the water at night; a ford a mile upstream, knee-deep; the toll men at the ford wear no lord's colors.

## Terse OSR

- First impression: "Shut till dawn. Two coppers a head."
- Under pressure: "Lights? Marsh gas. Costs you a boat to find out. Go to bed."
- Useful: "Ford, a mile up. Knee-deep. Toll men there wear no colors. Cheaper than me, dearer in the end."

**Fits:** tables that want information and want to move on; NPCs as quick, functional encounters.
**Perform it:** flat voice, no eye contact, keep working (coil a rope) while talking.

## Lush literary

- First impression: "The river keeps its own hours, travelers, and it closed its door at sundown. Come back when the mist lifts; two coppers each, and the herons will see you over."
- Under pressure: "The marsh breathes out at night, and some of what it breathes burns. I wouldn't put a boat among those lights for all the silver in your purse."
- Useful: "If morning is too far, walk a mile upstream to where the alders lean together; the ford there is only knee-deep. But the men who take the toll wear no lord's colors I have ever seen, and they count travelers more carefully than coins."

**Fits:** atmospheric and mythic campaigns; scenes the DM wants to slow down.
**Perform it:** unhurried, half to herself, looking at the water rather than the party.

## Pulp

- First impression: "Whoa, whoa! Ferry's shut tight till sunup, friends. Two coppers a head, and not one stroke before dawn!"
- Under pressure: "Lights? Swamp fire! You want to row into that, you pay me for the boat first, because you won't be bringing it back!"
- Useful: "In a hurry? Ford's a mile upriver, knee-deep, wade it in your boots. But keep a hand on your purse: the toll men up there wear no colors at all, and they don't charge by the head."

**Fits:** swashbuckling and high-action campaigns; NPCs who must register in one exchange.
**Perform it:** loud, big gestures, point upriver with the whole arm.

## Noir

- First impression: "We're closed. River doesn't work nights, and neither do I. Dawn. Two coppers."
- Under pressure: "Lights. Sure. Seeing things on the water is free. Looking closer costs extra."
- Useful: "You want across tonight, there's a ford a mile up. Knee-deep. The fellows taking the toll don't wear the duke's colors, so don't ask them whose they do wear. That question costs more than the crossing."

**Fits:** intrigue, investigation and city campaigns; NPCs who are always half lying.
**Perform it:** low and dry, a long pause before each answer, look past the speaker at the dark.

## Storybook

- First impression: "Oh, the ferry's fast asleep, my ducks, and so should you be! Come back when the sun is up, and it's two coppers each."
- Under pressure: "Lights on the water? Those are marsh-lanterns, and they belong to no one you'd want to meet. A night on this bank costs nothing; a night out there costs everything."
- Useful: "If you truly cannot wait, walk a mile upstream to the ford where the water only comes to your knees. But mind the toll-men there. They wear no lord's colors at all, and that is never a good sign."

**Fits:** fairy-tale, family and whimsical campaigns, and younger players.
**Perform it:** warm and sing-song, with a wagging finger on the warning.

## What changes and what does not

| Stays the same in every register | Changes with the register |
|---|---|
| The facts the lines carry | Sentence length and rhythm |
| The habit (pricing everything) | Vocabulary and imagery |
| The tell and its trigger | How much she says before the useful part |
| What she never says | How openly she hints at danger |

- If a register pushes a line past one breath, cut imagery, never facts.
- When the DM asks to hear one NPC in another register, keep the levers and redo only the words. A gruff miller in a storybook campaign is still gruff, just gruff the way a fairy-tale miller is.
- The tell never changes with register. It is the DM's anchor when they lose the voice mid-scene.

## Choosing a register

| Campaign signal | Register |
|---|---|
| sandbox, old-school, deadly, hexcrawl | Terse OSR |
| gothic, mythic, melancholy, slow-burn | Lush literary |
| swashbuckling, action, cinematic | Pulp |
| intrigue, city, investigation, heist | Noir |
| fairy tale, family, whimsical | Storybook |

An NPC can sit one step off the campaign's register on purpose: a noir-voiced fence in a pulp campaign is a character, not a mistake. Do it for one or two NPCs at most, or the off-register voice stops standing out.
