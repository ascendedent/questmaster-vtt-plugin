# Heist: lore and texture

A heist world is a world of thresholds: doors, walls, ledgers, passwords, routines. Give the DM concrete, physical details the crew can exploit, and let every description quietly answer the question "how would we get past that?"

## Lore textures

- **Routine.** The heartbeat of any target: shift changes, deliveries, the night bell, the owner's Thursday supper. Routines are what plans are built on and what complications break.
- **The trade.** Thieves have customs: the safe house, the split, the rule against working on holy days, the debt owed to whoever taught you. Give the crew a code they can keep or break.
- **Tools.** Picks, wax for key impressions, a hooded lantern, chalk for marking routes, a weighted cord, a forged seal, a stolen uniform. A named tool is a promise it will be used.
- **Architecture.** Old buildings have bricked-up doors, coal chutes, servants' stairs, priest holes, drains. New buildings have none of these, which is why owners build new vaults.
- **Magic security.** In a world with spells, owners plan for them. Flour on floors, bells keyed to teleportation, lead linings, decoy items, guard dogs trained to smell the invisible.
- **Value.** Why does the prize matter? Rarity, provenance, function, or what it proves. A prize with a story sells for more and is harder to sell at all.

## Sensory palette

**Sights**
- A brass keyhole polished bright by use, on a door that should be rarely opened.
- Chalk marks on a back alley wall: another crew's route, or a warning.
- A guard's lantern swinging on a regular beat across a high window.
- Flour dusting the strongroom floor, undisturbed except for one small set of paw prints.
- The prize itself, smaller than anyone expected.

**Sounds**
- Tumblers dropping into place, one at a time, in a silent room.
- A bell-wire humming faintly when someone touches the gallery rail.
- Boots on gravel outside, slowing, stopping, moving on.
- A dog's chain dragging across stone, then going quiet.
- The night bell from the temple across the square: the crew's clock.

**Smells**
- Lamp oil and cold stone in a cellar.
- Fresh paint over an old door, recently bricked up.
- Hot wax from a key impression, sharp and sweet.
- Perfume and roast meat drifting up the service stair during the gala.
- River water and tar in the getaway boat.

**Textures**
- The give of a floorboard that creaks only under a full-grown person.
- Greasy rope, cold iron rungs, damp brick under fingertips.
- The weight of a stolen uniform coat, cut for somebody taller.
- A forged invitation, the card stock just slightly too smooth.

## Naming guidance

- **The job gets a name, chosen by the crew.** Offer the players a few to pick from or let them invent one. Examples: the Long Supper, the Quiet Bell, the Candle Swap, the Second Funeral.
- **Crews name themselves after a place or a joke.** Examples: the Gutter Choir, the Thursday Club, Mother Halloway's Children.
- **Aliases** are short, forgettable, and tied to a habit. Examples: Tuesday, Thimble, the Accountant, Dry Jon.
- **Targets** carry the owner's name and a grand noun. Examples: the Vantreyne Collection, the Assay Hall of the Mintwardens, Coldharrow Strongroom.
- **Slang** sparingly, three or four words at most per campaign, defined by use. Examples: "a long walk" (a job with no way back), "soft" (a guard who can be bribed), "the bell" (the alarm, whatever form it takes).

## In-world documents

Stage each one with `stage_secret`, aimed at the character whose legwork found it.

- **A floor plan** with a wine-ring stain over the east stair, and a pencilled note in a different hand: "bricked up, 3 yrs".
- **A guard rota** for the coming week, with one name crossed out on Thursday and no replacement written in.
- **An invitation** to the unveiling, on heavy card, with a wax seal and a coded guest number.
- **A delivery manifest** for the service gate: "4 casks, 2 crates linens, 1 crate glassware (fragile, to be signed for by Steward Imbry only)".
- **A fence's price board**, chalked on slate: what she pays this month for silver plate, gemstones, seals, and "anything from the Vantreyne house: ask".
- **A forged writ of passage**, nearly perfect, with the wrong year's seal.
- **The client's commission letter**, written in a careful hand that does not match her signature.
- **A broadsheet the morning after:** "DARING THEFT AT THE ASSAY HALL. Wardens baffled. Reward offered." With a sketch of a suspect who looks like no one in particular, or exactly like a crew member.

## Location seeds

1. A counting-house with a vault under a public fountain; the vault floods if the fountain's sluice is opened.
2. A cathedral treasury opened once a year, on a feast day, in front of the whole city.
3. A prison where the prize is a prisoner, and the crew must leave with one more person than came in.
4. A moving target: a barge, a coach, a caravan, guarded by people who never stop.
5. A wizard's tower with no doors on the ground floor and a resident apprentice who is lonely.
6. A bank of memories, where the prize is a single recollection held in a glass bead.
