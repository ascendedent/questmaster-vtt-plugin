# Heist: beats

The signature structure is five words: **target, crew, plan, complication, twist.** Every heist arc, session and scene is some arrangement of those five, with legwork feeding the plan and heat following the getaway.

## The five pieces

- **Target.** A thing, the reason it matters, and the owner who loses it. Also: where it is, how it is protected, and when it will be somewhere else.
- **Crew.** The player characters plus any hired help. Each member should have a job only they can do on the night.
- **Plan.** The players' own. The assistant never writes it for them; it writes the obstacles and the assets they can build it from.
- **Complication.** Something that changes on its own and breaks one assumption in the plan. One per phase.
- **Twist.** A truth hidden from the start that reframes the job. Seeded in legwork, revealed during the job or the getaway.

## Arc structure: the Job

Usually 3 to 5 sessions. A one-shot compresses legwork and plan into the first hour.

1. **The offer.** A client, a debt, or a need brings the target to the crew. State the prize, the deadline, and the payment. Hint at the twist in one detail the crew will not understand yet.
2. **Legwork.** One or two sessions of casing, contacts, purchases and favours (structure below).
3. **The plan.** The players plan at the table, with a time limit. The DM notes the plan's assumptions; those are where complications aim.
4. **The job.** The execution session (structure below).
5. **Fallout.** Fence the prize, deal with the client, shed the heat. Seed the next job from whoever lost the most.

## Session structure: the legwork session

- **Days on the clock.** Start with a fixed number of days until the job (often 3 to 5). Each legwork action takes half a day or a day.
- **A menu, not a corridor.** Offer 6 to 8 legwork options and let the players choose 3 or 4. Examples: tour the building as visitors, buy drinks for off-duty guards, steal the architect's plans, recruit the inside man, acquire a uniform, test the alarm, learn the owner's schedule, find a fence.
- **Every option yields an asset.** An asset is a noun the crew can use later: a uniform, a key impression, a rota, a forged invitation, a favour from a coachman.
- **Every option has a cost.** Time, coin, or heat. Heat is noticed attention: the owner adds a guard, the watch asks questions.
- **One legwork scene should seed the twist.** Something that does not fit: the guard rota has a gap nobody explains, the client's description of the vault is too accurate.
- **End with planning.** The last 20 to 30 minutes is the players at the blueprint.

## Session structure: the execution session

1. **The briefing.** The players state the plan aloud. Write down every assumption ("the guard changes at midnight", "the inside man will open the side door").
2. **Entry.** The first layer. Let it go mostly to plan, to reward preparation.
3. **The layers.** Two or three security layers, each a scene (structure below).
4. **The complication.** Arrives in the middle layer. It breaks one written assumption. Always leave two ways through.
5. **The prize.** It is here. Usually. This is where the twist can land.
6. **The getaway.** A chase, a disguise, a quiet walk past a suspicious guard, a boat in the fog. Keep the clock running.
7. **The split.** Back at the safe house: what they got, what it cost, who saw them.

## Scene structure: the security layer

- **The obstacle.** One clear barrier: a locked door, a ward, a patrol, a dog, a bell-wire, a clerk who checks names.
- **Three approaches.** Quiet (stealth, timing), clever (deception, tools, assets), loud (force, distraction). Each has a different cost.
- **The alert change.** Say in advance what moves the alert level up a step (see encounters.md).
- **The asset hook.** Note which legwork asset would bypass or soften this layer, so the DM can reward it.

## Flashbacks, kept light

- A player may call a flashback during the job: "Last week, I swapped the bell-clapper for a wooden one."
- If the flashback uses an asset the crew already earned, it simply happened.
- If not, the player makes a fitting check now. On a success it happened. On a failure it happened with a cost: heat, a debt to someone, a witness who remembers.
- One flashback per character per job keeps it special. The DM can allow more.

## Worked example: the Die at Dunmarrow

**Target.** The master die used to strike the city's silver crowns, kept in the strongroom of the Mintwardens' Assay Hall. Whoever holds it can strike perfect coin.

**Client.** Corisande Hale, a widowed merchant, offers 600 crowns to steal it before the quarterly assay in four days. She says she wants it to counterfeit.

**Inside man.** Pell Ashby, an assayer's apprentice with gambling debts to a fence. He loves the work and hates what he suspects the guild is doing.

**Layers.**
1. The public hall: clerks, a weighing queue, a bored guard. Easy with a delivery or a petition.
2. The weighing room: scales sensitive enough to notice extra metal on anyone passing through. Crew members carrying tools ring the bell unless they bypass it.
3. The strongroom: two keys held by two wardens who never stand together.
4. The die chest: sealed with a glyph that marks whoever opens it with a faint silver stain.

**Legwork menu.** Visit as merchants; drink with off-duty wardens; buy Pell's debt from the fence; steal the builder's plans from the guild archive; observe the night shift from the bell tower; procure a warden's coat; learn the glyph from a retired assayer; find a buyer.

**Twist seeds.** The night shift works later than the hall's hours require. Pell mentions the furnace being warm in the mornings. Corisande knows the weight of a crown to the grain.

**Complication.** The assay is moved forward a day, and a guild inspector is on site.

**Twist.** The die in the strongroom is a copy. The guildmaster takes the real die home and strikes light coin at night. Corisande wants proof, not the die: she was ruined when her ships were paid in light silver.

**Options for the DM at the reveal.**
- Take the copy and walk: the job pays, the guild never learns, the light coin keeps flowing.
- Go to the guildmaster's house the same night: a second, unplanned job with no legwork.
- Bring the copy and Pell's testimony to the city magistrate: the crew becomes witnesses, and the guild becomes their enemy.
