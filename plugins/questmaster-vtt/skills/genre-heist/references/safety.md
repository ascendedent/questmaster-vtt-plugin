# Heist: table safety

Heists are usually light on gore, but they lean on tools that can hurt: threats against families, hostages, tight spaces, betrayal inside the party. Handle each one the way the table wants it handled.

## Always check first

- Call `get_table_safety` before drafting any of the themes below. If the owner shares limits, they bind every draft.
- **Lines** are absolute: the theme does not appear on-screen, off-screen, as a motive, in a rumour or in a staged secret.
- **Veils** happen off-screen: show the decision and the aftermath, never the act.
- **Soft limits** stay off-screen even if they are not marked as veils.
- If no limits are shared and a draft would include one of these themes, ask the DM one question ("Should X be on-screen, off-screen, or left out?") and follow the answer.

## Themes that commonly need a line or veil

### Hostages
- **Why it comes up:** It is the classic way a job "goes wrong", and the classic way an NPC forces the crew's hand.
- **Off-screen handling:** The rival crew holds the inside man's ledger, not his child. Leverage over property, reputation and debt works just as well.
- **If the table wants it:** Keep hostages adults, keep them unharmed on-screen, and resolve the situation with negotiation as well as force.

### Threats against the inside man's family
- **Why it comes up:** It is the cheapest way to make an NPC cooperate, and it turns player characters into villains.
- **Handling:** If a player proposes it, the inside man's loyalty breaks (npcs.md) and the job gets harder. Do not describe the threat in detail. Offer the DM alternative pressure: debts, a better offer, the inside man's own grievance.
- **Line in many tables:** threats against children. Check before using even as a rumour.

### Harm to bystanders
- **Why it comes up:** Servants, clerks, guests and guards who are just doing their jobs.
- **Handling:** Draft bystanders with names and small details so the crew sees them as people. Make harm to them generate heat and loyalty loss, not just a quick fight. Keep injuries off-screen unless the table wants them.

### Torture and interrogation
- **Why it comes up:** The crew catches a guard and wants the vault code; an owner catches a crew member.
- **Handling:** Offer leverage and deals instead. If a captured player character is questioned, cut to the aftermath and what the owner now knows. Never ask a player to describe or endure a torture scene.

### Confinement and tight spaces
- **Why it comes up:** Vents, crawlspaces, coffins, sealed vaults, sewers, being locked in at dawn.
- **Handling:** Some players find claustrophobia distressing. Describe tight spaces briefly and practically. If a player is uncomfortable, offer a different route at the same cost. Do not stack confinement with drowning or darkness without checking.

### Drowning and flooding
- **Why it comes up:** Flooding vaults, river getaways, sewers.
- **Handling:** Keep it as a clock and a hazard rather than a sensory description of suffocation.

### Betrayal inside the party
- **Why it comes up:** Heist stories love the crew member who was working for someone else.
- **Handling:** Never stage a secret that sets one player against another without the DM confirming the table enjoys player-versus-player scheming. If they do, make sure the betraying player agreed to the role before play.

### Addiction and gambling debt
- **Why it comes up:** It is a common reason an NPC needs money.
- **Handling:** Treat it with sympathy, not as a joke or a moral failing. A medical bill, a family debt or a lost business works equally well if the table prefers.

### Animals
- **Why it comes up:** Guard dogs.
- **Handling:** Give the crew non-harmful ways past them (scent, food, distraction). Some players are upset by harm to animals; keep it off-screen.

## On-screen versus off-screen, quickly

| Theme | Usually fine on-screen | Usually off-screen | Check first |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Breaking and entering | Yes | | |
| Fights with guards | Yes | Lasting injuries | Killing surrendered guards |
| Hostages | | Yes | Always |
| Family threats | | Yes | Always |
| Tight spaces | Briefly | Long descriptions | Players with claustrophobia |
| Party betrayal | | | Always |
| Harm to animals | | Yes | Yes |

## When the assistant drafts

- Never put a lined theme in any field, including DM-only notes and NPC secrets.
- For veiled themes, write records the DM can read aloud safely: what was decided and what changed, not the act.
- Flag sensitive items in the recap from `get_changeset` before `request_approval`.
- If the DM shares new limits later, offer to revise drafts that cross them.
