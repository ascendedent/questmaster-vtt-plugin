# Fey Fairytale: pitfalls

Each failure mode below is common at real tables. Each has the fix to apply in prep.

## Random whimsy
- **Symptom:** strange things happen for no reason (the tree sings, the river runs uphill), and the players stop trying to understand anything.
- **Fix:** every strangeness follows a rule, and the rule is findable. The tree sings because it holds a captured voice; the river runs uphill on the night the court rides. Whimsy with logic is wonder; without it, it is noise.

## The gotcha bargain
- **Symptom:** the fey's hidden clause wins every time, and players learn never to deal.
- **Fix:** every bargain has loopholes on both sides, and the wording is handed to the players. Let careful readers win. The fey are fair to the letter, which means the letter protects mortals too.

## Players refuse every offer
- **Symptom:** after one bad bargain, the party says no to everything and the genre stalls.
- **Fix:** make offers that are worth it and prices that are bearable. Show NPCs who thrive on good bargains. Put the party in situations where dealing is the least bad option, and let them negotiate the price.

## Fey as elves with wings
- **Symptom:** fey NPCs behave like noble humans with pointed ears, and the otherworld feels like a forest kingdom.
- **Fix:** give every fey binding rules and an inhuman want (to taste mortal grief, to collect every first word, to be thanked). Their logic should be consistent and alien.

## Prices that delete a character
- **Symptom:** a bargain takes a character's voice for the campaign, or makes them a puppet, and the player stops enjoying play.
- **Fix:** take something meaningful that still leaves the player in control: a memory, a sense for a season, a habit, a debt of service with a defined end. Pair every long-term price with a path to reclaim it.

## The DM's loophole always wins
- **Symptom:** the DM plans a clever twist on the wording and springs it, regardless of what the players argued.
- **Fix:** write the fey's loophole in advance and stick to it. When players find a better reading, honor it.

## Endless riddles
- **Symptom:** a session grinds while players guess the answer to a riddle the DM picked.
- **Fix:** riddles are answerable from what the party has seen; checks earn hints; a costly forfeit or bargain always lets play move on.

## Too many rules to track
- **Symptom:** every fey has six rules and every hall has ten customs; the players cannot remember any.
- **Fix:** 1 to 3 rules per fey, 1 or 2 per location. Repeat them in-world. Keep a short list in the dmNotes of each location and the secret of each NPC.

## Tone collapse
- **Symptom:** the campaign slides into slapstick (talking mushrooms, pun names) or into unrelieved horror (child-snatching every session).
- **Fix:** balance each session: one moment of wonder, one of danger, one of kindness. Match the ratio to the campaign's tone setting from `get_campaign_overview`.

## Overwritten contracts
- **Symptom:** bargains run to paragraphs of legalese, and the table loses interest.
- **Fix:** one or two sentences of wording. The danger lives in single words: "we", "ask", "the bell", "until".

## Mortals with no reason to deal
- **Symptom:** nobody would make these bargains, so the premise feels forced.
- **Fix:** show desperation first (drought, illness, a lost child, an unwinnable war) and the benefit second. A thriving village built on a bargain is more interesting than a ruined one.

## Iron solves everything
- **Symptom:** the party carries cold iron and every fey threat ends in one swing.
- **Fix:** wards are real but costly. Iron offends hosts and closes doors; it protects against harm but not against debt or a broken rule. Most fey dangers are social, not physical.

## Time slips with no consequence
- **Symptom:** "a year has passed" is said and nothing changes.
- **Fix:** show what time took: a rival who moved in, a debt that grew, a friend who married, a letter that arrived too late. Use time slips sparingly and make each one cost something.

## The fey as plain villains
- **Symptom:** every fey is an enemy to be outwitted or slain, and the otherworld becomes a dungeon with flowers.
- **Fix:** give some fey wants that line up with the party's, and at least one who is a genuine, if alarming, ally. The interesting question is not whether to trust them, but what they will ask in return.

## Prices that are forgotten
- **Symptom:** a bargain is struck, the benefit arrives, and the price never comes up again.
- **Fix:** record every bargain as a thread with its due date and raise its urgency each session. Bring at least one debt due in every arc. A price that is never paid teaches players that bargains are free.

## Review checklist before a fey session
- Does every fey the party might meet have 1 to 3 written rules and a want?
- Is every rule that could bite planted somewhere the players can find it first?
- Is every bargain's exact wording written down, with a loophole for each side?
- Has every price been checked against `get_table_safety`?
