# Fey Fairytale: safety

Fairytales are older and darker than their reputation. They lean on compelled behavior, stolen children, coercive bargains, transformation, lost identity and seduction. The genre's charm can make these feel lighter than they land, so decide what stays on-screen before the first bargain is offered.

## Always first

- Call `get_table_safety` before drafting fey NPCs, bargains or courts. If the owner has not shared limits, ask the DM once whether any topics should stay off the table.
- **Hard limits (lines) are absolute.** Remove them from bargains, prices, curses, documents and backstory hooks alike. A line cannot be a hidden clause either.
- **Soft limits (veils) stay off-screen.** A veiled event can be part of the world's past or a consequence reported afterward; it is never narrated in detail.
- Note any safety change in the NPC's notes or the location's dmNotes, so later sessions honor it.

## Themes that commonly need a line or veil

### Compelled behavior and mind control
- **Risk:** charms, enchantments and bargains that make a character act against their will can feel like the player losing their character.
- **On-screen:** compulsion as a pressure with a clear limit (one action, one hour) and a way to resist or break it.
- **Off-screen:** a character made to harm friends or do something degrading. Never make a player perform it.
- **Fix:** default to temptation over compulsion. Make the fey offer, not override.

### Coercion and consent in bargains
- **Risk:** bargains made under duress, threats dressed as choices, and fey who take what was not freely given.
- **Fix:** let players always refuse, at a cost they can see. A bargain the character could not refuse is not a bargain; it is a curse, and should be framed that way.

### Romance and seduction
- **Risk:** fey seduction is a fairytale staple and easily slides into sexual content or consent violations.
- **Default:** romantic and sexual bargains are a line unless the table has opted in. Never make a character's body or affection the price of a deal. Courtly flirtation that the player controls is fine where the table is comfortable.

### Stolen and swapped children
- **Risk:** changelings and abducted children are central to the genre and hit hard for parents and anyone with related grief.
- **On-screen:** the rescue, the return, the changeling as a person.
- **Off-screen:** harm to children, ever. Treat it as a default line. Taken children are safe and enchanted, not hurt.

### Loss of memory, name or identity
- **Risk:** prices that take memories or names can mirror dementia, dissociation or grief.
- **Fix:** ask before using it as a price for a player character. Keep it specific and recoverable (one memory, held in a locket the party can win back), not a slow erasure.

### Transformation
- **Risk:** being turned into an animal, a tree or a statue can touch body dysphoria or loss of control.
- **Fix:** keep transformations temporary with a clear key, and let the player keep agency (they can still act, speak in some way, choose).

### Time loss and returning to grief
- **Risk:** coming home to find decades gone and loved ones dead is a classic beat that can echo real loss.
- **Fix:** ask the DM before running it on a character whose player wrote family into their backstory. Smaller slips (a season, a year) carry the theme without devastation.

### Food, hunger and enchanted eating
- **Risk:** feasts where eating is dangerous and fasting is safe can touch disordered eating.
- **Fix:** if needed, move the rule from food to something else (drink, gifts, dancing, speaking first).

### Drowning and water spirits
- **Risk:** lake and river fey often lure victims into water.
- **Fix:** narrate from the bank, keep the struggle short, and give a clear rescue.

## Bargains and player backstories

- Backstories from `get_character` are player-written. If a character has a fey patron or an ancestral bargain, ask the DM before deciding what the patron wants or what the bargain costs. Never decide the terms of a player's existing deal yourself.
- When a bargain targets a specific character, aim the `stage_secret` that reveals it at that character only, and let the DM decide when it is pushed.

## The wonder is part of safety

- Fairytales are frightening because they are beautiful. Keep moments of real kindness, generosity and delight in every session, so the darker beats have contrast and the table has somewhere to rest.

## Checking a bargain against the table

- Before a bargain appears at the table, read its price and its breach against the table's limits. Both halves count: a harmless price with a breach that lands on a line is still a line.
- Prefer prices the player can describe and play (a lost color, a debt of service, a habit they cannot break) over prices that happen to the player's body or will.
- When unsure whether a price fits, offer the DM two versions, one gentler, and let them choose.
