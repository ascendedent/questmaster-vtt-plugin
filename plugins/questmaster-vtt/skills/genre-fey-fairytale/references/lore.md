# Fey Fairytale: lore

Fey lore lives in the mouths of ordinary people: rhymes, superstitions, things grandmothers say. Every warning a player hears should turn out to be a rule, and every rule should already exist somewhere in the folk lore before it bites.

## Lore textures

- **Rules hidden in rhymes.** Skipping songs, lullabies and counting games carry the real rules, garbled by time. The party has to work out what "seven stones, never the last" meant.
- **Superstitions that are true.** Turn your coat inside out when lost; leave milk on the step; never say thank you to a stranger on a bridge. Each one protects against something specific.
- **Time that runs strange.** A night under the hill is a month above; a year in the otherworld is an afternoon. Show it with a candle that never burns down or a beard grown overnight.
- **Names have power.** True names are never given freely. Fey go by titles and descriptions; mortals who deal with them learn to use nicknames.
- **Gifts flow one way.** Every gift creates a debt unless it is answered. Polite cultures near the woods have elaborate customs for returning gifts precisely.
- **Iron, salt, running water, bells, rowan.** Pick 2 or 3 wards that work in this campaign, and make them inconvenient: iron is heavy, salt is expensive, bells offend the hosts.

## Sensory palette

**Sights**
- Light that comes from nowhere, so nobody casts a shadow.
- Flowers of three seasons blooming on the same branch.
- A path of white pebbles that was not there a moment ago.
- Faces that are perfect until they smile, when there are slightly too many teeth.
- Dewdrops on a spider's web, each one reflecting a different sky.

**Sounds**
- Music from no visible source, always one bar ahead of where you expect.
- Your name, called in a voice you almost recognize.
- Bells, very small and very far off.
- Laughter that stops the instant you turn your head.
- Total silence in a wood that should be full of birds.

**Smells**
- Honey and crushed clover, too strong.
- Cold earth and mushrooms at the entrance to a hill.
- Apples in midwinter.
- Smoke from a fire that gives no heat.
- Rain on dry stone when the sky is clear.

**Tastes and textures**
- Bread that is warm all the way through, always, even days later.
- Wine that tastes like the best day of your childhood.
- Silk that feels like water and leaves the skin damp.
- Moss soft enough to sleep on, and the feeling of not wanting to get up.
- A hand that is pleasantly cool and leaves a print of frost.

## Naming guidance

- **Fey go by titles, not names:** the Lady of the Hollow Oak, the Gentleman in Grey, Mistress Orla of the Bramble Hall, the Knight of the Ninth Ford, Old Thrice-Asked.
- **True names are long, odd and secret.** Write one into the NPC's secret, and make it something that could be guessed from three clues (for example: Wren-Who-Sang-Down-The-Moon).
- **Place names warn:** Tithe Hill, the Thankless Bridge, Seven Stones, Lastlight Meadow, Weepwater Ford. Locals avoid saying them after dark.
- **Mortal nicknames for the fey are euphemisms:** the Good Neighbors, the Quiet Folk, Them Over the Hill, the Gentry. Pick one per region and have locals correct anyone who says "fey" aloud.
- **Courts named for times, not places:** the Court of Long Evening, the First-Frost Court, the Court of the Last Hour.

## In-world documents

- **A contract written on a leaf:** perfectly legible for a day, then fading. The party must copy it before it goes.
- **A skipping rhyme:** children sing it in the lane; each line is one rule of the crossing, in garbled order.
- **A parish register:** baptisms recorded every year except one, where the page is blank and smells of honey.
- **A witch's charm list:** "For a child who will not wake: salt at the door, iron in the cradle, and ask her name three times."
- **A will:** leaving "the bell and its debts" to the eldest grandchild.
- **A guest book in a fey hall:** names signed in many hands across centuries; some signed again later by older hands, some never signed out.
- **A letter from under the hill:** in a living person's handwriting, dated forty years ago, describing a party they attended "last night".

## Making rules findable

- Plant each important rule in at least two places: a rhyme and a warning, a document and a custom.
- Stage each as a `stage_secret` aimed at the character most likely to know it (the one raised near the woods, the bard, the scholar of old customs).
- When players break a rule they could have known, the consequence lands fully; when they break one they could not have known, give a warning first.
