# Fey Fairytale: NPCs

Fey NPCs are defined by their rules as much as their wants. Give each one 1 to 3 binding rules, a want that mortals can serve, and a tell that shows they are not quite human. Mortals in this genre are defined by the deals they have already made.

## How to use these

- For each fey, write its rules in the NPC's `secret` alongside its true name, and its tell in notes. Put the beautiful appearance and its one wrong detail in appearance, so players see it.
- At least one fey should be honestly generous, and that generosity should still bind.
- At least one mortal should be thriving because of a bargain, so the players see why anyone deals.

## Archetypes

### The gracious host
- **Twist:** cannot refuse a guest who asks properly, and cannot let one leave unfed. The danger is not malice; the danger is that guests who eat must stay until the host is thanked correctly, and nobody remembers how.
- **Want:** guests, endlessly. Conversation is food to her.
- **Fear:** an empty table.
- **Rules:** must grant any request made in rhyme; cannot touch iron.
- **Tell:** refills every cup the instant it is set down.
- Example: Mistress Orla of the Bramble Hall, whose sleeves are always damp with dew.

### The trickster who keeps score
- **Twist:** every prank is repayment for a slight the victim has forgotten. He keeps a ledger and is scrupulously fair by it.
- **Want:** to be acknowledged as clever, out loud, by name.
- **Fear:** being ignored.
- **Rules:** must answer a question asked three times; cannot pass an unsolved knot.
- **Tell:** laughs one beat before the joke lands.

### The debt collector
- **Twist:** collects for the courts, but privately believes every mortal debtor deserves one chance to renegotiate, and will help the party find a loophole if asked courteously.
- **Want:** a clean ledger and a good argument.
- **Fear:** his own debt, which is larger than any he collects.
- **Rules:** cannot lie about a contract's wording; must accept fair payment in any currency named in the original terms.
- **Tell:** reads every document aloud in full before discussing it, including the margins.
- Example: Master Pell Gloaming, with a coat of moth wings and a pen that writes in sap.

### The changeling
- **Twist:** swapped into a human family as a baby, now grown, and loves the family more than the fey child they replaced ever could. Exposure means being taken back.
- **Want:** to stay, and to know the other child is well.
- **Fear:** the twelfth birthday of the family's youngest, when the old bargain falls due again.
- **Rules:** cannot cross a threshold strewn with salt; cannot say the name of the family's patron saint.
- **Tell:** flinches at church bells, and covers it with a joke.

### The mortal who stayed
- **Twist:** taken under the hill decades ago and does not want rescue. Up above, everyone they loved is old or dead; here, they are a person of rank.
- **Want:** the party to carry one letter home and come back with an answer.
- **Fear:** learning what has happened to the people above.
- **Tell:** dresses in fashions forty years out of date and speaks with old courtesy.

### The sworn knight
- **Twist:** bound by a vow to guard a crossing until defeated, and desperate to be defeated so the vow ends. Cannot throw the fight; the vow forbids it.
- **Want:** a worthy opponent, or a clever one who finds the vow's gap.
- **Fear:** guarding forever.
- **Rules:** must accept every challenge; must fight with the weapon the challenger names.
- **Tell:** always offers the challenger first choice of ground, wistfully.

### The border witch
- **Twist:** a mortal who trades with the fey for her village, and keeps the worst bargains from being made by making them herself. She has paid more prices than anyone knows.
- **Want:** an apprentice who will not make her mistakes.
- **Fear:** the debt she owes for her own name.
- **Tell:** never says her own name; introduces herself by what she sells.

### The servant who bargains on the side
- **Twist:** a lesser fey bound to a noble, selling small favors to mortals behind its master's back to save up the price of its own freedom.
- **Want:** freedom, priced in something odd: a hundred true compliments, a lock of hair from every guest.
- **Fear:** its master noticing the ledger.
- **Tell:** finishes its master's sentences in a whisper.

### The court noble
- **Twist:** cannot lie, so has mastered the true statement that misleads. Is losing a long feud and needs a mortal agent who is not bound by fey rules.
- **Want:** to win the feud without breaking a rule.
- **Fear:** the rival's champion, who is also mortal.
- **Rules:** cannot lie; cannot break hospitality; must repay every gift threefold.
- **Tell:** answers questions with questions until asked a third time.

### The fey child
- **Twist:** older than the village and as curious as a child. Asks questions whose answers become true.
- **Want:** to learn why mortals cry.
- **Fear:** being bored.
- **Tell:** repeats the last word anyone says, delighted.

### The one who traded their memories
- **Twist:** a mortal hero who paid for each great deed with a memory. Now a legend who remembers none of it, guided by notes in their own hand.
- **Want:** to know if they were a good person.
- **Fear:** finding the note that explains the last price.
- **Tell:** checks a battered notebook before answering anything personal.
