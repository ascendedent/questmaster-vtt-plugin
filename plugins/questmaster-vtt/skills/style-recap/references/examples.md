# Recaps: weak vs strong

Five pairs, each from a different session. The weak versions are the common failures; the strong ones are built only from what the notes say. The prose samples are excerpts; a full recap runs 4 to 8 sentences.

## 1. Generic instead of specific

Notes: Mirela forged a guild seal to get past the counting-house desk. Clerk recognized Tam from the docks; Tam claimed to be a debt collector, clerk bought it. Got the ledger page. Clerk: "see you next quarter day".

**Weak**

> Last session the party went to the counting house, had some trouble, and eventually got what they came for.

**Strong**

> Last session, Mirela forged a guild seal good enough to get the party past the counting-house front desk. When the clerk recognized Tam from the docks, Tam claimed to be a debt collector, and the clerk believed him. They walked out with the ledger page they came for, and with the clerk's promise to see them again next quarter day.

**Why:** Specific choices, credited by name, are what players remember and what the DM must honor; "had some trouble" honors nothing.

## 2. Invented feelings, dialogue and scenes

Notes: Party skipped the shrine. Kesh argued with the priest about the tithe. Went straight for the pass. Avalanche, Joss hurt.

**Weak**

> Overcome with grief, Kesh shouted "Your god has abandoned us!" before the party paid their respects at the mountain shrine and set out for the pass, where a terrible avalanche nearly claimed brave Joss's life.

**Strong**

> Kesh argued with the priest over the tithe, and the party left without visiting the shrine. They went straight for the pass, where an avalanche caught them on the switchbacks and Joss was hurt.

Behind the screen: [unclear: how badly Joss was hurt; whether the argument ended on bad terms].

**Why:** The weak version invents a feeling, a line and a shrine visit that was planned but skipped; the strong one says only what the notes say and flags the gaps.

## 3. Leaking a secret

Notes: Abel Strand ferried the party; asked lots of questions about where they were going. (DM: Abel is the Baroness's informer, sent word that night.)

**Weak**

> At the crossing the party met Abel Strand, a friendly ferryman who is secretly a spy for the Baroness, and who reported their arrival as soon as they left.

**Strong**

> At the crossing, Abel Strand ferried the party over and asked a great many friendly questions about where they were headed.

Behind the screen: Abel is the Baroness's informer; he sent word of the party's arrival that night.

**Why:** The prose keeps what the party saw; the betrayal stays behind the screen until the players earn it.

## 4. A decisions table that records nothing

**Weak**

| Decision | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Fought the wolves | Won |
| Talked to the mayor | Got the quest |

**Strong**

| Who | What they chose | What happened | What it may set in motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sefa | Spared the wounded wolf and let it run | The pack withdrew | The pack may remember the party, for better or worse |
| Orrin | Told the mayor the reward was too low | The mayor doubled it from the shrine fund | The shrine-keepers may want to know where their fund went |
| The party | Did not report the poacher's traps they found | The traps are still set | More wolves may be hurt, and the pack may come back angrier |

**Why:** The strong table says who chose what, includes a choice made by doing nothing, and phrases the future as possibilities the DM can use or drop.

## 5. Tone that fights the campaign

Campaign tone: grim, a plague-struck city. Notes: Party burned the infected granary; it held the quarter's winter grain. Healer Nell wept. Big table laugh at a player's "roasted rats" joke.

**Weak**

> In a hilarious turn of events, the gang torched the granary and enjoyed some roasted rats! Even Nell got a little teary-eyed, poor thing.

**Strong**

> The party burned the infected granary to the ground, and the quarter's winter grain burned with it. Healer Nell wept as she watched. Now the quarter faces the winter with empty stores.

**Why:** The joke was real, but it belongs to the table, not the story; a grim campaign's recap keeps the weight the players will walk back into. (In a light campaign, the joke could stay.)

## Patterns across the pairs

- Every strong recap uses names and verbs from the notes, and nothing else.
- Every strong recap ends on what is still open, not on what was resolved.
- Every secret, motive and off-screen event sits behind the screen.
- Every strong table includes at least one choice made by inaction.
