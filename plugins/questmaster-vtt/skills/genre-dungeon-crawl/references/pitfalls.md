# Dungeon Crawl: pitfalls

Each failure mode below shows up at real tables. Each has the fix to apply in prep.

## The corridor dungeon
- **Symptom:** rooms in a single chain; the only choice is whether to open the next door.
- **Fix:** add loops (two per level), a second entrance, and a vertical link. Run the layout check from beats.md: could the party flee by a different route than they came in? If not, cut a new passage.

## The secret door gate
- **Symptom:** the only way forward is behind a secret door, the party misses it, and the session stalls.
- **Fix:** secret doors are shortcuts and treasure, never the critical path. Make the long way open and costly; make the secret way fast.

## The monster closet
- **Symptom:** each room holds monsters standing still, waiting for the party to open the door.
- **Fix:** give every inhabitant an activity and a reason to be in that room. Let them hear the fight next door and react: flee, reinforce, barricade, or loot the losers.

## The trap gotcha
- **Symptom:** a save-or-suffer trap with no warning, and the players learn only to search every square.
- **Fix:** telegraph every trap (scorch marks, bones, worn grooves, a dead delver) and give it an interaction. A good trap is a puzzle the players can see.

## Combat in every room
- **Symptom:** twenty rooms, twenty fights, a party exhausted by level 2.
- **Fix:** aim for roughly a third of keyed rooms with a fight possible, a third with a hazard, puzzle or parley, and a third with something to find or understand. Let factions talk.

## Resources nobody counts
- **Symptom:** light, rations and spells never run low, so there is no reason to hurry or turn back.
- **Fix:** count torches out loud and check wanderers on a fixed rhythm. Make the turn-back moment a scene. If the table hates bookkeeping, keep only the light count and the alarm clock.

## Too many rolls
- **Symptom:** every ten feet triggers a check; the session is spreadsheets.
- **Fix:** roll for wanderers on a schedule, not per action. Let searching a room take one turn and find what is there if the players look in the right place; a roll is for when time pressure matters.

## Empty rooms that feel like filler
- **Symptom:** "This room is empty." Repeated.
- **Fix:** empty of monsters is not empty of story. Give each one a trace of use: a bedroll, a shrine, a tally wall, a warning. Rooms with nothing at all get merged into corridors.

## The one-solution puzzle
- **Symptom:** the players do not think of the designer's answer, and the dungeon stops.
- **Fix:** write three solutions, accept any clever fourth, and keep a costly brute-force option (loud, slow, dangerous) so the puzzle never ends the session.

## The static dungeon
- **Symptom:** the party leaves and returns to find everything exactly as they left it.
- **Fix:** restock with consequences every visit (beats.md has the list). The dungeon remembers the party, and the factions move.

## Mapping by dictation
- **Symptom:** the DM spends half the session describing corridor lengths for a player-mapper.
- **Fix:** describe rooms by features and exits, show the map on the table screen as rooms are revealed, and let annotations reveal as the party sees them. Spend the words on what is interesting.

## The unreachable boss
- **Symptom:** the climax requires the party to fight through every room on the way.
- **Fix:** the heart should have at least two approaches, one of them bypassing most of the fights at a different cost (a swim, a bargain, a climb).

## Writing numbers by hand
- **Symptom:** a hand-tallied encounter turns out to be lethal or trivial at the table.
- **Fix:** let QuestMaster rate it. `plan_encounter` returns a rating; `rate_encounter` checks a mix; adjust the situation, not just the head count.

## Factions that only fight
- **Symptom:** every group inside attacks on sight, so the factions are just monster types with names.
- **Fix:** give each faction a want the party can serve and a grievance against a neighbor. At least one faction should open with talk, a toll or an offer of work.

## Treasure that means nothing
- **Symptom:** piles of coin and a few magic items with no history; the players stop reading the room descriptions.
- **Fix:** every hoard tells part of the dungeon's story (whose coin, why it was hidden, who wants it back). On every level, at least one find should be information: a map, a password, a confession.

## Review checklist before a delve
- Does each level have two loops, two ways in and a vertical link?
- Does each keyed room say what it was built for, what uses it now and what changed?
- Are the light count, the wanderer rhythm and the alarm clock written down?
- Is every lethal trap or lair telegraphed at least one room ahead?
- Has every planned fight been rated by QuestMaster rather than by hand?
