# Dungeon Crawl: beats

The signature structure is the expedition: prepare, go in, spend resources to learn and take, decide when to leave, get out, and come back to a dungeon that has changed. The layout is part of the beat structure, because loops and alternate routes are what turn rooms into decisions.

## Arc structure: the delve (3 to 6 sessions)

1. **The rumor.** A reason to go in that is specific and slightly wrong: a lost payroll, a sealed archive, a missing apprentice, a cure grown only in the dark.
2. **The threshold.** The entrance tells the party who built the place and who lives there now. Give at least two ways in, one obvious and one earned.
3. **The first foray.** Learn the layout, meet a faction, take something small, leave with questions. The party should exit by choice, not by rout.
4. **The lever.** The party learns what the inside factions want from each other and picks a side, a bargain or a betrayal.
5. **The deep.** Lower levels where the original purpose of the place shows through and the resources run thin.
6. **The heart.** The thing the rumor pointed at, and the truth about why it was sealed.
7. **The aftermath.** What leaving changes: a faction rules the upper halls, the thing in the deep is loose, or the treasure has a rightful owner who now wants it back.

## Campaign spine: the returning dungeon

- One large site, a town at its door, and a reason to go back often (it is a mine, a library, a prison, a border).
- Each level answers a different question about the builders. The deepest level answers the last one.
- Between sessions, restock with consequences (see the restock list below), and let town politics shift with what comes out.

## Session structure: one expedition

- **Town (15 minutes):** buy light and rope, hear one new rumor, settle one debt. End by asking which way in.
- **Approach:** the route to the entrance; spend one beat here only if it changes what they bring inside.
- **Forays (most of the session):** room by room, with the clocks ticking aloud. Aim for one faction contact, one hazard, one find and one hard route choice.
- **The turn-back moment:** when light, spells or health hit a threshold, put the question on the table: press on or get out?
- **Extraction:** getting out is a scene, especially when the alarm is up. Close on what they carried out and what followed them.

## Scene structure: the room

1. **Senses first:** one sound, one smell, one light condition, before any furniture.
2. **Obvious features:** two or three things anyone would notice, at least one interactable.
3. **Exits:** name every way out, including up and down. Note which are open, shut, barred or suspicious.
4. **Hand it over:** ask what they do, then answer with what changes. Searching takes time, so it ticks the clock.
5. **Leave a trace:** what the party leaves behind (blood, a spiked door, an open chest) is what the dungeon reacts to later.

## The turn procedure (kept light)

- One exploration turn is about ten minutes: move a stretch of corridor, search one room, pick a lock, rest a breath.
- A torch lasts about six turns. Count it out loud.
- Check for wanderers every two turns, and every turn while the party is noisy.
- Advance the alarm clock one step for each fight, shout, forced door or alarm triggered. At the top of the clock, the dungeon responds as a whole.

## Laying out loops: a fast method

1. Draw the entrance and a hub room two or three rooms in.
2. Draw three spokes from the hub, each ending in a faction's territory or a key room.
3. Connect two spokes to each other with a side passage. That is loop one.
4. Connect the third spoke to the entrance by a different route (a collapsed wall, a flooded tunnel). That is loop two.
5. Add one vertical link to the next level from somewhere other than the main stair.
6. Add one optional secret shortcut, never on the critical path.
7. Check: could the party reach the heart without the secret door? Could they flee by a different route than they entered? If not, add a route.

## Worked example: the Nine Weights

**The site.** An assay house and mint cut into a hillside, sealed thirty years ago after a coin scandal. Two entrances: the barred front gate, and the collapsed furnace chimney on the hilltop (a climb straight into level 2).

**Level 1, the Weighhouse.** Counting rooms, great scales, a vault door with nine keyholes. Faction: the Tallymen, a goblin clan who believe they are employees. They mint their own tin tokens, pay themselves wages and keep perfect books. Want: a real overseer to sign their ledger. Grievance: the Kiln Sisters below stole their furnace.

**Level 2, the Furnace Floor.** Crucibles, a stamping press, slag chutes. Faction: the Kiln Sisters, three cultists who tend a bound fire spirit in the great furnace and call it a saint. Want: fuel and silence. Grievance: the Tallymen keep blocking the coal stair.

**Level 3, the Under-Cistern.** Flooded cooling tanks and a records room above the waterline. Threat: a colony of metal-eating creatures grown huge on runoff. Heart: the mintmaster's ledger proving the coin was debased on royal orders, which is why the house was sealed with people still inside.

**Loops.** The main stair links 1 and 2. The bell-pull shaft (a tight climb) links the mintmaster's office on 1 to the foreman's booth on 2. The slag chute is one-way from 2 down into the cistern. The flooded stair (a swim) links the cistern back to the Weighhouse cellar. A party can reach the heart without ever passing the Sisters.

**Session 1.** In by the front gate. The Tallymen demand to see work papers and offer a job: fix the vault door. First foray ends with a choice: help the Tallymen take back the furnace, or trade with the Sisters for the chimney route.

**Session 2.** The alarm is up on level 2 after a fight at the coal stair. The party learns the chute goes down but not back. Turn-back moment at the chute's edge.

**Session 3.** The cistern and the ledger. Extraction by the flooded stair with the creatures following. Aftermath: whoever holds the ledger can embarrass the crown, and a royal agent is already in town asking about the assay house.

## Restocking between visits

- Doors the party forced are barricaded or trapped by whoever lives nearest.
- A cleared room is claimed by the faction that benefited most.
- Bodies are stripped, moved or eaten; their gear turns up on wanderers.
- One faction sends an envoy, a threat or a bribe.
- One new thing arrives from outside, drawn by the noise the party made.
