# Gothic Horror: Pitfalls

Each failure mode below is common in gothic play. Each has a fix you can apply to a draft before the DM ever sees it.

## Structure pitfalls

### Skipping the ladder
- **Symptom:** the vampire attacks in the first scene; by session two nobody is scared because they have already seen it.
- **Fix:** hold the monster back until the revelation rung. If the DM wants early action, give the party a fight with something the monster sent (hounds, a thrall, a possessed servant), never the monster itself.

### Revelation that only confirms
- **Symptom:** the diary says what the players already guessed, and the session sags.
- **Fix:** every revelation must change a want. Add one fact that implicates someone the party likes, or that makes the obvious solution cost more.

### The curse with no origin
- **Symptom:** "the house is just haunted". There is nothing to investigate and nothing to break.
- **Fix:** write the origin act in one sentence (who, what they traded, who benefits now) before drafting anything else.

### The party has no reason to stay
- **Symptom:** sensible players leave at the first ghost. The DM is tempted to lock the doors.
- **Fix:** never lock them in by force. Give them reasons they choose: someone to protect, money owed, a promise to the dead, a clue that points to their own past. Ask the DM once which hook fits this party.

### Free mercy
- **Symptom:** the party spares the monster and everything is fine. Mercy becomes the obviously correct button.
- **Fix:** price it before it is offered (see the mercy costs menu in beats.md). Mercy should still often be right; it should never be cheap.

## Atmosphere pitfalls

### Jump scares
- **Symptom:** "a skeleton leaps out!" The table laughs.
- **Fix:** trade surprise for anticipation. Tell the players the closet door is ajar an inch and it was closed. Let them choose to open it.

### Fog, ravens and thunder on every page
- **Symptom:** stock gothic props stacked until they are wallpaper.
- **Fix:** one specific, slightly wrong detail beats five genre props. Not "a raven watches", but "the rooks have all left the rookery, and their nests are full of the family's missing spoons".

### All rot, no beauty
- **Symptom:** everything is grim, so nothing stands out.
- **Fix:** make the house genuinely lovely in places. A perfect library, a warm kitchen, a beautiful song. The horror needs something worth losing.

### Narrated dread
- **Symptom:** "you feel a sense of creeping unease". Players are told what to feel.
- **Fix:** describe facts and let players supply the feeling. "The bed is made. You did not make it. It is made the way your mother made it."

## NPC pitfalls

### Everyone is sinister
- **Symptom:** every NPC whispers and leers, so the players trust nobody and engage with nobody.
- **Fix:** at least one NPC is warm, funny and on the party's side. Make losing them hurt.

### The monster as a stat block
- **Symptom:** the climax is a damage race and the monster has no voice.
- **Fix:** give it a want, a memory and a line it will not cross. Let it talk during the fight. Let it ask for something.

### Seductive monster without consent awareness
- **Symptom:** a charm or seduction power is used on a player character in a way the player did not sign up for.
- **Fix:** see safety.md. Frame supernatural influence as pressure the player narrates, not something done to them, and check the table's limits first.

## Player experience pitfalls

### Helplessness
- **Symptom:** nothing the party does changes anything until the finale.
- **Fix:** each session, one thing the party can affect: ward a room, save one servant, steal one key, learn one name. Small wins keep dread from becoming despair.

### Lore dump
- **Symptom:** an NPC explains the entire curse in a monologue.
- **Fix:** split the truth across at least three sources, each partial and each a little wrong.

### Splitting the party against their will
- **Symptom:** the DM separates players for atmosphere and half the table sits idle.
- **Fix:** split only for short scenes, cut between groups every few minutes, and give each group one decision so nobody is just watching.

### Ignoring the backstories
- **Symptom:** the curse belongs to NPCs only; the players are tourists.
- **Fix:** read `get_party` and tie one thread of the curse to a character's past (a lost relative, a debt, a resemblance in a portrait), as a hook the player can pull or leave. Never rewrite a player's backstory to make it fit.
