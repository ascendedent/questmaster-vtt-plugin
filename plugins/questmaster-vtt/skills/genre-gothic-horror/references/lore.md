# Gothic Horror: Lore

Gothic lore is personal and domestic. It lives in wills, letters, portraits, household ledgers and the rules a family follows without remembering why. The history that matters is two or three generations deep, and someone in the house was alive for it.

## Lore textures

- **Inheritance.** Wills with codicils, entailed land, heirlooms that must not leave the house, a family motto that reads differently once you know the curse.
- **Mourning.** Invent customs: mirrors turned to the wall for forty days, a widow's ring worn on the thumb, the dead's chair left at table until the next death, bread left on the grave on the anniversary.
- **Household law.** Rules with forgotten reasons: no one sleeps in the blue room, the clocks in the west wing are never wound, the youngest child lights the first candle.
- **Portraits.** Every generation painted. The portraits are a record: who was left out, who was painted over, whose eyes the artist refused to finish.
- **Land.** The estate's fortune comes from one thing (orchards, slate, wool, a spring) and that thing is tied to the curse.
- **The origin act.** One night, one choice, one person. Lore should circle it from many sides before anyone names it.

## Sensory palette

### Sights
- Dust sheets over furniture in rooms that are still dusted underneath.
- Candle wax pooled in a shape, as if someone stood holding the candle for hours.
- A garden gone to seed except for one bed, perfectly kept.
- Wallpaper with a pattern of vines, and in one room the vines are real.
- A pale rectangle on the wall where a portrait used to hang.

### Sounds
- A clock in an empty room that strikes thirteen once a year.
- Footsteps on the floor above, where the floor above is the roof.
- A bell-rope that sways with no draught, and the faint answering ring from somewhere below.
- Rain in the chimney; a child counting in the rain.
- The family laughing together, very suddenly stopping.

### Smells
- Lilies and something under the lilies.
- Beeswax, lavender, damp plaster.
- Apples in midwinter, too sweet, slightly fermented.
- Cold ash in a grate that was lit an hour ago.
- Old paper, mouse, and iron.

### Textures and temperature
- Banisters worn smooth by generations of the same hand.
- A bed that is warm when the party returns to their room.
- Velvet gone stiff with damp; lace that tears like wet paper.
- A key that is always cold, even in a closed fist.

## Naming guidance

- **Family names** sound old, landed and slightly heavy: two syllables, a soft consonant and a hard one. Examples: Corvel, Ashlade, Morrant, Vellacott, Greythorne, Haldane, Sorrell.
- **Estates** are named for a feature or an old use, plus "House", "Hall" or "Court": Harrowmoor Hall, Sallow Court, Cinderwick House, the Lantry Grange.
- **Given names** in the family repeat across generations (the third Abelard, the fourth Imsa), which lets ghosts and the living share a name.
- **Servants** have short, plain names or are known by role: Mother Tamsel, Old Pike, the Lamp Boy.
- **Villages** are practical: Cauldwell, Fennick Cross, Lower Harl, Nettlebridge.
- **The thing** is never named by the family. They use euphemisms: "the arrangement", "Grandmother's tree", "our friend in the orchard". A true name, if it has one, is a revelation-rung reward.
- Avoid names that sound like a famous vampire or a published setting. Test a name by saying it in a sentence the butler would say.

## In-world documents

Each of these is a `stage_secret` waiting to be aimed at the right character:
- **A codicil to the will:** one sentence that makes no sense until the revelation rung ("I leave my youngest granddaughter to the orchard, as was promised").
- **A household ledger:** entries for black crepe, a coffin, and a dowry, all in the same week, every sixteen years.
- **A letter never sent:** found in a writing desk, addressed to someone who died before it was written.
- **A child's primer:** the alphabet with one letter for each family rule ("E is for East Stair, which we do not climb").
- **A recipe book:** a preserve recipe with one ingredient scratched out and replaced, in a different hand, with a name.
- **The parish register:** births and deaths, with one recurring note in the margin.
- **A painter's commission:** the instructions for a family portrait, specifying who must not be included.
- **Pressed flowers in a diary:** each labelled with a date and the words "she asked me to keep it".
- **A servant's reference letter:** glowing, written for a maid who vanished, by the family who made her vanish.

## Delivering lore

- One document per scene, never a stack. The party should want the next one.
- Damage the document deliberately: a burnt corner, a missing page, a smudged name. The gap is a clue.
- Let at least one document be a lie, written by someone protecting the family. The truth is in what the lie had to explain away.
