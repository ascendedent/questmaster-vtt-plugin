# Prep note registers

Prep notes are always terse; that is their job. The register lives in the cues: the sensory details you jot down, the line you give an NPC, and the flavor of each consequence. Below, the same scene in five registers. The purpose, the situation and the decision never change.

## The fixed scene (written out in full, terse OSR)

```
### Gannet's Cross toll bridge
**Purpose:** the party learns the bridge warden is paid to look away at night.
Situation:
- Cobb Ashby, bridge warden, wants a quiet life and his cut. Two bored guards.
- Toll 1 sp a head. Under the bridge: a barge, no lantern, four crew loading sacks.
Cues: wet rope, a gull at a fish head, a brand-new padlock on the coin box.
Cobb: "Silver a head. Read the board."
**Decision: how they cross**
- If the party pays: waved through; Cobb watches them all the way over.
- If the party threatens Cobb: the guards run for the barge, not for town.
- If the party goes down to the barge: crew wary (reaction roll); salt sacks over crossbow crates.
- If they do something else: the barge casts off at the next bell either way.
Clues: the warden is bought (also: the padlock key on the barge captain's belt; the town's toll tally runs short).
Leads out: the barge, Gannet's Cross town, Cobb's shack.
```

For the other four registers only the cue line, Cobb's line and the flavor of the branches change. Everything else is copied as is.

## Lush literary

- Cues: river mist pooled under the arches, lantern light that stops at the bridge's middle, tar and cold stone, the arm of Cobb's chair worn pale where his hand rests.
- Cobb: "The river asks a silver of everyone, travelers. I only hold its purse."
- Branch flavor: consequences arrive slowly and land as images. If they pay, Cobb's eyes follow them into the mist, and that night they hear oars where no boat should be.
- Pacing note: let the scene breathe; describe the barge before anyone acts.

## Pulp

- Cues: the barge crew already shoving off, a rope snapping taut, a crate lid sliding, the glint of a crossbow stock.
- Cobb: "Silver a head, and quick about it! I've a supper going cold!"
- Branch flavor: every branch ends in motion. If they threaten Cobb, a guard leaps for the barge as it lurches into the current: a chase on the water.
- Pacing note: start a timer the moment they see the barge; three rounds before it reaches the far bank.

## Noir

- Cues: Cobb's coat is better than a warden's wage; the coin box padlock is new and its key is not on him; the guards are careful not to look at the barge.
- Cobb: "Toll's a silver. What you see from my bridge is free. What you say about it isn't."
- Branch flavor: consequences are favors and leverage. If they pay without a fuss, Cobb remembers them as people who keep quiet, and someone offers them work by the end of the week.
- Pacing note: the scene is a negotiation; a fight is the failure state.

## Storybook

- Cues: three arches, a painted sign with a smiling fish, a bell to ring for the warden, the barge below as dark as a shadow with no owner.
- Cobb: "One silver for each of you, and a polite word for the river, if you please."
- Branch flavor: patterns of three, and rules that matter. If they are rude to Cobb, the toll doubles; if they are kind, he lets slip that the barge only comes on nights with no moon.
- Pacing note: three travelers ahead of the party pay three different ways; the players can copy any of them.

## What stays fixed in every register

- The purpose line and the decision are register-neutral. Never dress them up.
- Bold stays on decision points only.
- Mechanics (reaction rolls, timers, DCs) are written plainly. Lush prose around a DC makes it harder to find.
- If a register adds a consequence (the pulp chase, the noir job offer), it is still a consequence of a player choice, never a scene the party is pushed into.
- Read-aloud for the scene follows `style-read-aloud` in the same register, and sits above the purpose line or in the encounter, not mixed into the bullets.

## Choosing at a glance

| Campaign tone | What the cues should hold |
|---|---|
| Terse OSR | exits, objects, numbers, reaction rolls |
| Lush literary | light, weather, texture, one striking image per scene |
| Pulp | motion, timers, things about to fall, snap or sail away |
| Noir | money, who is watching, what people pretend not to see |
| Storybook | patterns of three, rules, small kindnesses and their rewards |

## Cue line habits that work in any register

- Three cues per scene: one sound or smell, one object the party can use, one sign of what an NPC wants.
- Write cues as nouns with one modifier ("new padlock", "worn chair arm"). The DM builds the sentence live.
- Put one line of NPC speech in quotation marks so the DM can find the voice instantly. Longer voice work belongs on the NPC record (`style-npc-voice`).
