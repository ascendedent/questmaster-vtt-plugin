# Prep notes: weak vs strong

Five pairs. Each weak version reads fine at home and fails at the table; each strong version can be run with one glance.

## 1. The wall of prose

**Weak**

> When the party reaches the Wren and Barrel, the innkeeper Marta is nervous because the smugglers have threatened her, and she will try to get the party to leave, but if they are friendly she might eventually tell them about the cellar, where the smugglers have hidden the stolen bell, unless the party already spoke to the smith, in which case she will assume they know and react with anger, and the smugglers will arrive at midnight.

**Strong**

```
### The Wren and Barrel
**Purpose:** the party learns where the stolen bell is hidden.
Situation:
- Marta Venn, innkeeper, threatened by smugglers; wants the party gone before midnight.
- The bell is in the cellar, behind the cider barrels.
- Smugglers arrive at midnight. Clock: 3 hours.
**Decision: how they approach Marta**
- If the party is friendly: she warns them off, then hints at the cellar if they stay past ten.
- If the party already spoke to the smith: she assumes they know; anger, then a plea.
- If they do something else: the smugglers arrive at midnight regardless.
Leads out: the cellar, the smith, the quarry road.
```

**Why:** The same information, split into findable lines; the clock and the decision are visible at a glance.

## 2. The railroad

**Weak**

> The party will go to the crypt, where they must find the second key. Then they travel to the abbey.

**Strong**

```
**Decision: where they go after the funeral**
- If the party goes to the crypt: the second key is gone; the sexton's muddy footprints lead out.
- If the party follows the sexton: he walks to the abbey, the key in his coat.
- If the party questions the widow: she names the sexton and begs them to leave the crypt sealed.
- If they do something else: the sexton reaches the abbey at dawn and the key passes to the abbot.
```

**Why:** Every route reaches the key or loses it on a clock; nothing says the party must, and doing nothing has a consequence.

## 3. Bold everywhere

**Weak**

- **Mother Gaskett** is the **herbalist**, very **suspicious** of **outsiders**.
- She has the **antidote** but will only trade for **news of her son**.
- **Important:** the **antidote** works only **before sundown**.

**Strong**

- Mother Gaskett (GAS-ket), herbalist, distrusts outsiders.
- Has the antidote; trades it only for news of her son.
- Antidote works only before sundown. Clock: 5 hours.
- **Decision: what they offer her (news, coin, or a threat)**

**Why:** When everything is bold the eye has nowhere to land; bolding only the decision puts the DM's attention where the table's will be.

## 4. One clue, one door

**Weak**

> The party discovers that the reeve is the thief by finding the ledger in his office.

**Strong**

```
Clue: the reeve is the thief.
- The ledger in his office (search it, or bribe his clerk).
- His wife's new silver brooch, worn at market (anyone who looks).
- The fence in Cobble Row, who describes him (if the party asks around the docks).
```

**Why:** If the one search fails, the session stalls; three paths to the same conclusion mean someone at the table will find it.

## 5. No purpose, no exit

**Weak**

> The old mill. A ruined mill by the river. Rats. A broken wheel. Some old sacks.

**Strong**

```
### The old mill
**Purpose:** the party learns the missing apprentice was here two days ago, alive.
Situation:
- Ruined mill, wheel broken, rats in the sacks.
- Her satchel under the hopper; inside, bread two days old and a half-written letter.
Leads out: the footpath upriver (fresh prints), the miller's widow (rents the loft), back to town.
```

**Why:** A location with no purpose is set dressing; a purpose line and leads out turn it into a scene that moves the session.

## Patterns across the pairs

- Strong notes have more lines and fewer words.
- Every strong decision has a catch-all branch, and the world moves in it.
- Every strong scene says what it is for and where it leads.
- Clocks appear as numbers on their own line, never buried in a sentence.

## Converting a DM's prose in four passes

1. Underline every sentence with "if", "unless" or "when". Those are decision points; bold them and branch them.
2. Pull every name into a one-line reminder; pull every number onto a mechanics line.
3. Ask of each remaining sentence: does the DM need this mid-scene? If not, move it to the NPC or location record.
4. Add the purpose line and "Leads out" last, once you can see what the scene does.
