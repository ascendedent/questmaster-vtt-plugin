# Swashbuckling: Pitfalls

The ways this genre goes wrong, how to spot each one in a draft or at the table, and the fix.

## The parody voice
- **Spot:** every NPC growls "arr" and the players start doing bits instead of playing.
- **Fix:** give each NPC one verbal flourish (a toast, a curse, a pet name) and otherwise let them speak plainly. Wit beats dialect.

## The static slugfest
- **Spot:** three rounds in, nobody has moved, and the fight is a damage race.
- **Fix:** stage changes on triggers, a prize that moves, enemies who stunt first. If a fight stalls in play, the DM changes the footing now: a lantern falls, the deck tilts, the chandelier rope frays.

## Daring punished by death
- **Spot:** a player swings from the rigging, fails one roll, and drops out of the fight or dies. Nobody tries a stunt again.
- **Fix:** failed stunts cost position, gear, dignity or freedom. Keep lethal outcomes for risks the players chose knowing the stakes.

## Rivals who are only bosses
- **Spot:** the rival appears only to be fought, and dies at the end of the arc.
- **Fix:** write the honor code, climb the rivalry ladder one rung per meeting, and give the rival something they want from the party other than defeat.

## The capture railroad
- **Spot:** the plot needs the party in the brig, so the fight is made unwinnable.
- **Fix:** capture is an out the players can choose or the fair result of a lost fight. If the story needs a jail, give them a reason to break into one (a friend inside) instead of forcing them into it.

## Planning paralysis
- **Spot:** forty minutes of planning a boarding, and the table's energy is gone.
- **Fix:** give a reason to go now (the ship sails on the tide, the ball starts at the bell). Let the DM offer a "we prepared for that" flashback paid for with something gathered earlier. Make waiting cost something visible.

## One swashbuckler, four spectators
- **Spot:** the fencer gets every spotlight while the wizard and the cleric hold coats.
- **Fix:** build each stage of a set piece for a different character (`get_party`). A stage can be a lock, a crowd, a sea creature, a lie, a burning rope that needs a spell.

## Consequences that evaporate
- **Spot:** the party burns the governor's yacht and nothing changes next session.
- **Fix:** a reputation thread, broadsheet headlines, prices that change, a new rival drawn by the fame, a door that now opens because of what they did.

## Chandelier arguments
- **Spot:** a rules dispute over whether a stunt is even possible.
- **Fix:** settle stunt resolution before the first set piece and write it in the session notes. The DM says "yes, and here is the cost" far more often than "no".

## Villains who monologue
- **Spot:** the villain stops the scene to explain the plan.
- **Fix:** villains talk while doing something (fencing, dressing for the ball, loading a pistol), and the speech reveals a want, not a plan.

## Forced romance
- **Spot:** an NPC flirts relentlessly with a character whose player is not interested.
- **Fix:** romance is a door the player can open. If they do not, the NPC becomes a friend, a rival or an ally with a crush they never act on. Check `get_table_safety`.

## Nothing ever matters
- **Spot:** everything is a lark; no loss sticks; the players stop caring about outcomes.
- **Fix:** one real loss per arc (the ship, a mentor's reputation, a friend's freedom) that the party can fight to reverse. Lightness lands harder against something at risk.

## Too many factions in one scene
- **Spot:** six factions in the masquerade and the players cannot tell who wants what.
- **Fix:** two factions per set piece, three at most for a finale. Every other faction is a rumor.

## Banter that stalls the fight
- **Spot:** quips fly back and forth for ten minutes and the blades stop moving.
- **Fix:** every taunt is an action with an effect (an opening, a distraction, the crowd swinging to one side). One line, then the steel moves.

## The rival who always escapes
- **Spot:** the rival slips away by DM fiat every time, and the players feel cheated.
- **Fix:** the rival escapes only through outs the players could have seen and closed (a rope, a waiting boat, a bribed guard). If they close every out, the rival yields, which is a better scene.

## Anachronistic winks
- **Spot:** modern jokes and slang flatten the world into a sketch.
- **Fix:** humor comes from character and situation (a rival's vanity, a terrible disguise), not from references outside the world.
