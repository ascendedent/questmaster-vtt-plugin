# Swashbuckling: Beats

The signature structure is the set piece: a scene where the ground itself is a character and every stage is more precarious than the last. Arcs are built from rivalries; sessions start mid-swing.

## The set piece (scene scale)

A set piece runs in 3 to 4 stages. Each stage changes three things at once: the terrain, the stakes and the clock. Copy this card per stage:

- **Ground:** where everyone stands and what is moving under them.
- **Props:** 2 or 3 new things to use, each with what it does.
- **Problem:** the new danger or demand.
- **Stake:** what is lost if the party ignores it.
- **Trigger:** the event, round or party action that moves to the next stage.
- **Spotlight:** which character this stage is built for (check `get_party`).
- **Outs:** how someone leaves this stage early, and what they give up.

### Escalation levers (use a different one each stage)

- **Altitude:** deck, rigging, crow's nest, the spar over open water.
- **Footing:** dry, wet, tilting, burning, collapsing.
- **Crowd:** empty, onlookers, a panicking crowd, a crowd that takes sides.
- **Time:** a fuse, a tide, a closing gate, a bell counting the hour.
- **Exposure:** masks on, a mask slipping, a name shouted across the room.
- **Stakes:** money, then a ship, then a person, then a reputation.
- **Light:** lanterns, fireworks, smoke, dark broken by cannon flashes.

### Rules of thumb

- Never repeat a lever in consecutive stages.
- The final stage is narrow: a one-on-one on the worst footing in the scene, where the audience can see.
- Write the exit as carefully as the entrance: the bow, the leap, the last line, the cost paid.
- If the players find a clever way to skip a stage, let them. The skipped stage's props can return later.

## The rivalry arc (3 to 5 sessions)

1. **The slight.** The rival enters by besting the party at something small and public. They do not kill; they win and leave a calling card.
2. **The race.** Both sides want the same prize. Two or three set pieces where the lead changes hands; each reveals one clause of the rival's code.
3. **The turn.** A third party (the patron, the governor, the real villain) breaks the rules of the game. The rival is betrayed too, or pushed to break their own code. The party loses something real: a ship, the prize, a friend to capture.
4. **The bargain.** Offer the DM at least two shapes: a truce held by both codes, a duel for terms, or the party turning the rival's code against them. The players choose.
5. **The grand set piece.** Every thread in one place (the gala, the regatta, the burning flagship). Four stages, each resolving one thread. Write what each possible duel result changes; the dice and the players decide which one happens.

## The session: cold open to cliffhanger

- **Cold open (10 to 15 minutes):** start mid-action. The party is already on the roof, already masked, already cutting the anchor cable. No travel, no shopping.
- **The breath:** a short social scene to land consequences, plan, banter and plant a hook (a tavern, a patron's salon, the brig).
- **The set piece:** 3 or 4 stages; the bulk of the session.
- **The twist:** something the set piece reveals (the prize is a forgery, the rival's second is the rival's master, the patron paid both sides).
- **The exit beat:** end on a decision, a sail on the horizon, or a challenge card left on a pillow.

## The duel (beat by beat)

- **Salute:** terms are stated (first blood, yield, disarm) and the audience leans in.
- **Probe:** an exchange that shows each side's style; the rival names a weakness they have spotted.
- **Shift:** the ground or the stakes change (pick a lever).
- **Gambit:** someone tries something reckless and the dice decide.
- **Verdict:** the terms are honored or broken in front of witnesses, and the room reacts.

## Worked example: The Candleboat Regatta (one session)

**Setup.** Every midsummer the port of Calvesse races candle-lit boats across the harbor. The winner's purse is a harbor pilot's license, which the party needs to bring a stolen ship in legally. The rival, Captain Ondine Varrel of the *Brass Kestrel*, needs it to pay a ransom she has told no one about. Her code: never strike a back; no one drowns for her wager; pay every debt in the coin it was made in. Breaking point: her brother's life.

- **Cold open.** The party is aboard a borrowed racing cutter at the start line, and someone has sawed halfway through their boom.
- **Stage 1, the start (lever: crowd).** Forty boats jostle for the line. Props: boat hooks, a fish-seller's skiff, a lit candle in a glass on every prow. Problem: the sabotaged boom. Spotlight: whoever is handy with tools or can sweet-talk a neighboring boat. Trigger: the starting cannon.
- **Stage 2, under the Weavers' Bridge (lever: altitude).** The course passes under a bridge packed with spectators who drop flowers on boats they backed and rotten fruit on boats they bet against. Props: laundry lines, a bridge winch, a dangling guild banner. Problem: Varrel's crew drops a weighted net. Stake: the lead, or a snared crewmate. Trigger: the first boat clears the bridge.
- **Stage 3, the fireworks barge (lever: light and footing).** A stray rocket lights the barge mid-harbor with celebrants aboard. Props: water buckets, rocket racks, a tow line. Problem: rescue them or keep racing. Stake: the crowd's love against the lead. Varrel turns back too if the party does, because no one drowns for her wager. Trigger: the barge sinks or is towed clear.
- **Stage 4, the finish under the governor's balcony (lever: stakes).** Both boats are prow to prow; Varrel boards for a duel on the bowsprit as they cross the line. Outs: yield, a dive, cutting her boat loose. Stake: the license.
- **Twist.** Whoever wins, the governor announces the license comes with a favor owed to his household. Varrel's ransom note was written in the governor's own hand.
- **Exit.** A candle-boat bumps the party's hull with a folded note: "Next tide. Same terms. O.V."

Branches to offer the DM: if the party skipped the barge rescue, the crowd turns, and stage 4 has hecklers pelting the party instead of Varrel. If they sabotaged Varrel in stage 2, she breaks her first clause in stage 4, and the party has a witness to it.
