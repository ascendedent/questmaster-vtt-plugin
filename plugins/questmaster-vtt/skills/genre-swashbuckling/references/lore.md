# Swashbuckling: Lore

Swashbuckling lore is gossip with a sword in it. Keep it light on history and heavy on who owes whom, what the song gets wrong and which tavern is safe tonight.

## Textures of the world

- **Law is local and for sale.** Every port has two sets of rules, the written and the real. The written rule bans dueling; the real rule bans dueling before noon.
- **Reputation outruns ships.** Broadsheets, ballads and tavern talk carry the party's deeds to the next port before they arrive, exaggerated and sometimes wrong in useful ways.
- **Everyone had another name once.** Captains, fencing masters, harbormasters and courtesans all came from somewhere else and left something behind.
- **Steel and powder, a pinch of magic.** Pistols misfire in the rain; blades are personal; magic is seasoning (luck charms, weather-callers, a cursed coin) unless the campaign says otherwise.
- **The sea is a road.** Lanes, currents and trade winds are routes everyone knows. The unknown begins where the charts stop being signed.

## Sensory palette

### The harbor
- **Sights:** a forest of masts, gulls on every bollard, a gallows by the customs house, laundry strung between hulls.
- **Sounds:** block-and-tackle squeal, hawkers, one ship's bell answering another, coins on a counting table.
- **Smells:** tar, fish guts, wet hemp, orange peel from the fruit ships.
- **Textures:** salt crust on railings, slick quay stones, rope burn.

### A ship at sea
- **Sights:** canvas cracking full, a horizon that tilts, a captain's cabin with too many maps.
- **Sounds:** timbers groaning, the bosun's whistle, the slap of a loose sheet.
- **Smells:** bilge, lamp oil, spray, a cook's pot of something that used to be a turtle.
- **Textures:** a deck that never stops moving, sticky pitch in the seams, wet wool.

### The ballroom
- **Sights:** a hundred candles in a chandelier hung on a single rope, masks of feathers and lacquer, a host's portrait that watches the room.
- **Sounds:** a string quartet half a beat behind the dancers, fans snapping shut, glass on glass.
- **Smells:** beeswax, perfume over sweat, roasted almonds.
- **Textures:** silk gloves, a cold balustrade, the weight of a borrowed sword under a cloak.

### Rooftops at night
- **Sights:** chimney stacks, washing lines, the moon in a rain barrel, one lit window.
- **Sounds:** cats, a watchman's rattle, a tile cracking underfoot.
- **Smells:** coal smoke, bread ovens starting before dawn.
- **Textures:** wet slate, a lead gutter, a weathervane that turns under a hand.

### The brig
- **Sights:** one barred square of light, a rat with a favorite corner, names scratched into the beam.
- **Sounds:** the guard's dice, water sloshing under the boards, the captain's boots overhead.
- **Smells:** old water, damp straw, the jailer's pipe.
- **Textures:** cold iron, a loose nail, a splinter that could become a lockpick.

## Naming

- **Ships:** a metal or color plus a bird or beast (the *Brass Kestrel*, the *Copper Heron*, the *Red Hart*); a virtue the ship lacks (the *Patience*, fastest hull in port); a lover's name painted over an older one that still shows through.
- **People:** a soft given name and a clipped family name: Ondine Varrel, Sevrin Aldocq, Mareth Coil, Teodric Lusse, Paz Ollander, Ines Brevard. Save joke names for comedic campaigns.
- **Nicknames earned by deeds:** Three-Bells (rang the alarm three times and was right each time), the Dry Captain (has never once fallen in), Halfsail, Velvet.
- **Places:** named for events (Hangman's Steps, the Quay of Second Thoughts), trades (Ropewalk, Tallow Row), or a saint nobody remembers.
- **Taverns:** two nouns, one unexpected: the Anchor and Violin, the Salted Bishop, the Honest Cutlass.
- **Blades:** named for what they did, not what they are: Apology, Last Word, the Governor's Regret.

## In-world documents

Each works as a `stage_secret` handout aimed at the character who would receive it.

- **Cartel of challenge:** "To the person who wore green at the Corlay Ball. You trod on my honor and on my foot. The Weavers' Bridge, first light, smallswords. Bring a second and a better excuse. S.A."
- **Letter of marque:** a commission to seize enemy shipping, the enemy's name left blank, the wax still soft.
- **Wanted broadsheet:** a drawing that looks nothing like the character, a reward insultingly small, and a list of crimes, half of which they did not commit.
- **Ship's articles:** the crew's written rules (shares, payment for a lost hand, no dice below decks), signed with marks, one mark crossed out in red.
- **Ransom note:** written on the back of a playbill, the price set in a currency no one in port uses.
- **Broadside ballad:** a song about the party's last escape with the facts wrong in a useful way: "and the captain leapt from the steeple bell" (they took the stairs).
- **Gambling chit:** an IOU for a ship, signed by someone who does not own it yet.
- **Coded love letter:** a fish-stew recipe where every third ingredient is a harbor berth and a time.
