# Swashbuckling: Encounters

Build every encounter as a situation: who wants what, what stands in the way, what makes it worse, and how anyone gets out. QuestMaster rates fight difficulty through `plan_encounter`, `rate_encounter` and `propose_encounter`; this file covers shape and pacing, not numbers.

## Design principles

- **Movement over attrition.** A fight where everyone stands still trading blows is a failed set piece. Give every round a reason to move: a prize changing hands, footing changing, a clock.
- **Stakes beyond hit points.** Something to grab, someone to save, a door closing, a crowd to win.
- **Agree on stunts once.** Before the first set piece, settle with the DM how stunts resolve (for example, one ability check; success grants an edge such as advantage or a free reposition, failure costs position, an item or dignity). A settled rule ends chandelier arguments.
- **Enemies stunt first.** Have a named enemy swing on a rope or kick a barrel in round one. Players copy what they see work.
- **Outs are written, not improvised.** Every encounter lists at least two ways it ends without anyone dying.

## Combat situations

### The duel of terms
- **Situation:** a formal duel with seconds and witnesses on stated terms (first blood, yield or disarm).
- **Stakes:** a debt, a deed, a reputation, someone's freedom.
- **Complications:** the opponent's second cheats; the watch arrives mid-bout; the ground is the rim of a frozen fountain.
- **Outs:** terms met, a yield, the seconds negotiate a draw, everyone scatters from the watch.
- **Escalate:** a hidden pistol in the crowd turns a duel into a skirmish, and the cheating side loses its honor in front of everyone.

### The boarding action
- **Situation:** two ships grappled together, crews fighting across a gap that keeps changing.
- **Stakes:** the ship, the cargo, the captives below decks.
- **Complications:** grapples parting, a falling yard, fire near the powder room, a squall.
- **Outs:** a captain strikes the colors, the lines are cut, a crew turns on its own captain.
- **Escalate:** the ships drift apart; anyone on the wrong deck must jump, swing or surrender.
- **Morale:** count the enemy crew's setbacks (captain down, flag taken, mast lost). On the third, they call for quarter.

### The rooftop chase
- **Situation:** the quarry has a head start across rooftops and alleys at night.
- **Stakes:** the letter they carry, the name they will sell by dawn.
- **Structure:** 4 to 6 obstacles. Each offers a safe route (slower, the quarry gains ground) and a daring route (a leap, a slide down an awning, a sprint through a rooftop wedding).
- **Complications:** loose tiles, a guard dog on a flat roof, a bell tower striking the hour.
- **Outs:** corner the quarry, lose them but find the dropped glove, bargain at the edge of a long fall.

### The masquerade brawl
- **Situation:** steel is drawn in a ballroom full of masked guests.
- **Stakes:** the guests, the party's covers, the host's goodwill.
- **Complications:** the orchestra keeps playing; a dozen guests wear the same mask; a guest faints into someone's sword arm.
- **Outs:** a balcony leap into the garden, the host's guard separates everyone, a public apology that saves face.

## Social situations

### The public challenge
- **Situation:** the rival issues a challenge before a crowd.
- **Stakes:** refusing costs reputation; accepting endangers the mission.
- **Options to offer:** counter-terms (time, place, weapon), naming a champion, a side wager, a refusal so witty the crowd sides with the refuser.

### The toast war
- **Situation:** a formal dinner where every toast is a veiled attack and every reply is scored by the room.
- **Stakes:** a patron's favor, an alliance, a seat at the next table.
- **Complications:** a guest answers on the party's behalf; a rumor of poisoned wine; a toast that exposes a secret.
- **Outs:** a graceful exit, a counter-toast that turns the room, a challenge that moves things outdoors.

### The bluff at the gate
- **Situation:** the party in borrowed uniforms, facing an officer who is almost sure.
- **Complications:** the officer's friend wore that uniform last week; the real messenger arrives behind them.
- **Outs:** bribe, bluster, distraction or a sprint, each leading to a different next scene.

### Parley under the black flag
- **Situation:** two crews meet on neutral ground (a sandbar at low tide, a chapel, a tavern with a no-steel rule).
- **Stakes:** a hostage exchange, a shared enemy, a split of treasure.
- **Complications:** a crew member with a private grudge; a navy sail on the horizon; a forged copy of the thing being traded.

## Exploration situations

### Rigging in a storm
- **Situation:** a sail must be cut, a lookout saved or a signal lit at the masthead in a squall.
- **Clock:** each lightning flash is a round; on the fourth, the mast cracks.
- **Outs:** lash yourself to the yard, cut the sail free, take a line and jump for the sea.

### The tide cave
- **Situation:** a smugglers' cave that floods at high tide, with the cargo still inside.
- **Clock:** barnacle lines on the walls show where the water will stand at each bell.
- **Complications:** smugglers already there, a stuck cart, something drawn by lamplight.
- **Outs:** ride the flood out through the blowhole, wait it out on a ledge, abandon the cargo.

## Puzzle situations

### The listing strongroom
- **Situation:** the captain's strongroom on a sinking ship; the lock opens to a sequence written in the ship's log as wind directions.
- **Stakes:** the charter, the ransom, the proof.
- **Clock:** the list worsens; loose cargo slides on each failed attempt.
- **Outs:** smash the door and lose the contents to the sea, take the whole strongbox, find the spare key on the captain's chain.

### The opera cipher
- **Situation:** tonight's performance carries a coded message in last-minute libretto changes; the lead singer is the courier and does not know it.
- **Stakes:** a plot against the governor.
- **Complications:** the party must be in the audience and backstage at once; the rival has the next box.

## Escalation ladder

When an encounter needs more heat, climb one rung at a time:
1. Add an audience.
2. Change the footing.
3. Start a clock.
4. Put someone the party likes in danger.
5. Threaten an identity (a mask slipping, a name shouted).
6. Bring in the rival, code on display.

## Writing the outs (per encounter)

- **Yield:** what the winner gains, what the loser keeps.
- **Capture:** where the captive wakes and who visits first.
- **Escape:** what is left behind (a hat, a blade, a letter).
- **Bargain:** what each side would accept, so the DM can play it straight at the table.
