# Swashbuckling: NPCs

Swashbuckling NPCs perform. Each archetype below has a twist that keeps it from the stock figure, a want, a fear and a tell the DM can play in one gesture or line. Swap genders, ancestries and ages freely; keep the twist.

## Writing an honor code

- **Three kept clauses,** in the NPC's own voice: "I never strike a back." "I pay my debts in the coin they were made in." "No one drowns for my wager."
- **One breaking point:** the specific pressure that makes them break a clause (a sibling threatened, a public humiliation, a sum large enough to free their crew).
- **One contempt:** the thing they despise in others, so the party can earn respect or scorn on purpose.
- Codes are known in the right circles. Let the party learn clauses from a former crewmate, a ballad or by watching a fight.
- In QuestMaster, the kept clauses go in the NPC's profile; the breaking point goes in the DM-only `secret`.

## The rivalry ladder

A rivalry is a thread that climbs one rung per meeting. Do not skip rungs unless the players force it.

1. **Slight:** the rival wins something small and public.
2. **Draw:** a third party interrupts before anyone wins.
3. **Defeat:** one side loses something real, and the winner spares them.
4. **Respect:** they warn each other, or fight back to back once.
5. **Resolution, chosen in play:** alliance, a final duel, one side giving up the chase, or romance only if the player opens that door.

## Archetypes

### The gentleman rival
- **Twist:** quietly funds the party's other enemies, not from malice, but to make the party strong enough to be worth beating.
- **Want:** one clean victory in front of the person whose opinion he craves.
- **Fear:** winning by someone else's cheat.
- **Tell:** wipes his blade on his own sleeve, never on the opponent's clothes.
- Example: Sevrin Aldocq, who returns every weapon he disarms with a calling card tucked in the guard.

### The dread captain
- **Twist:** cannot swim and is terrified of deep water; the fearsome reputation is armor.
- **Want:** a pardon and a house on a hill a day's ride from any coast.
- **Fear:** going overboard; being seen afraid.
- **Tell:** always has one hand on a line, a rail or a mast.
- Example: Captain Mareth Coil, who dangles captives over the side because it is the worst thing she can imagine.

### The fencing master
- **Twist:** has never fought a real duel; all theory, reputation and bluff.
- **Want:** a student brilliant enough to make the school's name before anyone tests the master.
- **Fear:** a challenge he cannot refuse.
- **Tell:** corrects strangers' grips mid-conversation, even on teacups.

### The ward who needs no rescue
- **Twist:** she is staging her own escape and has hired the party as the distraction without telling them.
- **Want:** her inheritance, in her own name.
- **Fear:** being rescued into another cage: a marriage, a convent, a grateful hero.
- **Tell:** knows the guard rotation better than the guards and finishes the party's plan for them.

### The masked hero
- **Twist:** two siblings share the mask on alternate nights; their styles differ and witnesses swear to different heroes.
- **Want:** to ruin the magistrate without either of them hanging for it.
- **Fear:** one of them unmasked, and the other forced to choose.
- **Tell:** the left-handed one hums while fighting; the right-handed one never speaks.

### The honest harbormaster
- **Twist:** every crooked permit she signs is under blackmail, and she is gathering evidence to burn the blackmailer.
- **Want:** an ally who can steal back her letters.
- **Fear:** her daughter learning what she did to get the post.
- **Tell:** stamps a document twice when she is lying about it.

### The duke's champion
- **Twist:** the duke holds his family's debt; he fights well and hopes to lose to someone who would look after them.
- **Want:** the debt cleared, or the duke gone without his hand on it.
- **Fear:** killing an innocent for a man he despises.
- **Tell:** offers every opponent the chance to walk away, exactly twice.

### The forger-cartographer
- **Twist:** the treasure map she sells is genuine, and she sells it to every crew in port to empty the harbor on one particular night.
- **Want:** that empty harbor, for a job of her own.
- **Fear:** anyone who reads maps well enough to spot her watermark.
- **Tell:** an ink-stained thumbnail that taps the compass rose while she haggles.

### The relentless hunter
- **Twist:** a by-the-book naval officer who admires the party and wants them alive, because a hanged pirate is a story and a living one is a trial that could change the law.
- **Want:** one capture that earns a seat at the admiralty table.
- **Fear:** being ordered to hang someone he believes innocent.
- **Tell:** polishes one particular button, a gift from his mother, when frustrated.

### The rival's second
- **Twist:** the better blade of the pair, losing on purpose to keep the principal alive, or to stay unnoticed.
- **Want:** release from a debt of honor to the principal's family.
- **Fear:** being forced to fight at full skill in public.
- **Tell:** lazy parries until the principal is threatened, then suddenly perfect ones.

### The quartermaster
- **Twist:** keeps two ledgers; the second tracks what it would cost to buy every crew member out of their articles.
- **Want:** one big score to close the second ledger.
- **Fear:** the captain finding it.
- **Tell:** counts under her breath when nervous: barrels, coins, heartbeats.

### The smuggler queen
- **Twist:** a disgraced navy captain who still runs naval discipline; her smugglers salute and keep a clean deck.
- **Want:** her commission restored, which she would trade the party for.
- **Fear:** the officer who disgraced her rising any higher.
- **Tell:** checks her pocket watch against every harbor bell.

## Performing them fast

- One verbal flourish each (a toast, an oath, a style of insult) and one prop (a glove, a flower, a pipe).
- Rivals compliment the party's best quality, not their worst: "You are faster than your captain deserves."
- Let every recurring NPC remember the last meeting out loud. Callbacks make a rivalry feel earned.
