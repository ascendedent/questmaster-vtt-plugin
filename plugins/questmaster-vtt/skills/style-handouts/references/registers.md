# Handout registers

One handout, five registers. The core facts never change. What changes is the object, the writer and the voice, and each writer may add one line of their own: that line is a free hook. Choosing the object is itself a clue about who cared enough to make it.

## The core facts

- Wenna Cole, seventeen, the bell-founder's daughter, missing three nights.
- Last seen at the Ropewalk at dusk, in a grey shawl with a red hem.
- Twenty silver for word that leads to her; fifty if she is brought home.
- Ask for Master Aldous Cole at the foundry on Tallow Lane.

## Terse OSR: a job-board slip

Object: a palm-sized slip of rag paper nailed among bounty slips; one corner torn off, where someone took a tally.
Written by: the board clerk, in square capitals, the same hand as every other slip on the board.

> MISSING. WENNA COLE, 17, FOUNDER'S DAUGHTER. GREY SHAWL, RED HEM. LAST SEEN ROPEWALK, DUSK, 3 NIGHTS BACK. WORD OF HER 20 SP. HOME SAFE 50 SP. A. COLE, FOUNDRY, TALLOW LANE.

- **Fits:** sandbox play where jobs come from a board and the party picks.
- **The free hook:** the torn corner says someone else has already taken this job.

## Lush literary: the father's printed appeal

Object: a sheet of good paper, printed at real expense, with a small engraving of a bell at the head; a dozen pasted along the Ropewalk.
Written by: a schoolmaster Aldous paid to find the words; the grief is his own.

> To any soul who walked the Ropewalk on the evening of the fourth: my daughter Wenna, seventeen years, went out at dusk in her grey shawl with the red hem and has not come home. She has her mother's laugh and my hands, burned at the knuckles from the moulds. Twenty silver to whoever brings me word of her. Fifty, and my gratitude for all my days, to whoever brings her home. Aldous Cole, at the foundry, Tallow Lane.

- **Fits:** atmospheric or melancholy campaigns; a hook about a family as much as a mystery.
- **The free hook:** her burned knuckles. She worked the moulds, which a foundry daughter is not supposed to do.

## Pulp: the broadsheet

Object: a cheap broadsheet, the ink still smudging, sold for a copper by a boy shouting the headline.
Written by: a printer who smells a story. Aldous gave him the facts; the printer added the rest.

> VANISHED! FOUNDER'S DAUGHTER SNATCHED FROM THE ROPEWALK! Wenna Cole, 17, last seen at dusk three nights past, grey shawl, red hem! TWENTY SILVER for word! FIFTY for her safe return! Apply to Master Cole, foundry, Tallow Lane!

- **Fits:** action campaigns; handouts players want to read aloud in a voice.
- **The free hook:** "snatched" is the printer's guess, not a fact. A sharp player will ask who said so.

## Noir: a card under the door

Object: a stiff card slid under the party's door at the inn, a sooty thumbprint on one edge.
Written by: Aldous himself, in an unsteady hand trying hard to look steady.

> I'm told you find people. My girl Wenna's been gone three nights. The Ropewalk at dusk, grey shawl with a red hem. That's all anyone saw, or all anyone is saying. Twenty silver for word of her. Fifty if she comes home. Foundry, Tallow Lane. Ask for me, not the foreman.

- **Fits:** intrigue and investigation campaigns; the job that arrives quietly.
- **The free hook:** "Ask for me, not the foreman." The DM can leave it unexplained, or make it matter.

## Storybook: the notice on the well

Object: a square of card tied to the well rope with blue ribbon; a little drawing of a bell in one corner.
Written by: Wenna's younger brother, in a neat, round, careful hand.

> HAVE YOU SEEN WENNA? She is seventeen and her laugh is the loudest in Tallow Lane. She went to the Ropewalk when the lamps were lit, three nights ago, in her grey shawl with the red hem, and she has not come back. Father will give twenty silver to anyone who tells us where she is, and fifty to anyone who brings her home. Please knock at the foundry.

- **Fits:** fairy-tale and family campaigns; a hook that makes the party care first.
- **The free hook:** the writer is a child, so there is a child at the foundry who may know more than the grown-ups.

## The DM note under all five (never shown to players)

- Truth: not yet canon. Offer the DM two options and ask which to keep, for example: she left by choice with a ropemaker's apprentice; or she was taken by someone who owes her father money.
- Planted lead: the red hem. Whoever later finds a grey shawl with a red hem has found where she went.
- Who notices what: a character from the town knows the Ropewalk empties at dusk except for the night crews; whoever reads the job board sees the torn corner.

## What the register changes

| Register | Object | Writer | How the facts are bent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Terse OSR | a board slip | a clerk | stripped to data; nothing personal |
| Lush literary | a printed appeal | a hired writer, a grieving father | wrapped in love and detail |
| Pulp | a broadsheet | a printer | sensationalized, with a guess sold as fact |
| Noir | a private card | the father, alone | guarded, suspicious, quietly desperate |
| Storybook | a ribbon-tied notice | a child | simple, warm, and more honest than intended |

## Choosing the object by register

| Register | Objects that sound right |
|---|---|
| Terse OSR | job slips, tallies, inventories, scrawled maps, bounty boards |
| Lush literary | printed appeals, letters on good paper, deeds, epitaphs |
| Pulp | broadsheets, wanted posters, playbills, ransom notes |
| Noir | cards under doors, receipts, pawn tickets, notes in a dead man's pocket |
| Storybook | notices on wells, letters with drawings, riddles on doors, recipe cards |

Two versions of the same handout in one campaign is a good trick: the father's card and the printer's broadsheet disagree on one word, and that word is the mystery.
