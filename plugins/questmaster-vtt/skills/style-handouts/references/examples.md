# Handouts: weak vs strong

Five pairs. The weak versions are text with no object; the strong ones are things a player could hold, and every one of them hides a lead.

## 1. A letter with no object

**Weak**

> A letter from the smuggler captain: "Meet me at the docks at midnight. Come alone. Bring the money."

**Strong**

Object: a strip torn from a ship's chart and folded small; the back still shows soundings and half a coastline. Smells of tar.
Written by: someone who writes like a sailor, in block letters with no wasted words, crossing the 7 in the foreign way.

> Pier 7. Second bell after midnight. You alone. The full sum, in Ardish coin, not your local tin. If I see a lantern I am gone.

DM note: the chart scrap matches the charts aboard the *Gull's Wager*. The crossed 7 is how Captain Rennick writes, as any of his old crew could tell.

**Why:** The torn chart, the crossed 7 and the coin demand are three leads the plain letter does not have.

## 2. A wanted poster that tells the truth

**Weak**

> WANTED: Sela Marr, a rebel who is actually innocent and was framed by the magistrate. 100 gold reward.

**Strong**

Object: a printed poster on yellow paper, a crude woodcut face, the magistrate's red stamp in the corner. Many copies; some torn down.
Written by: the magistrate's office, in the language of the court.

> WANTED for SEDITION and the BURNING of the Tithe Barn: SELA MARR, called Redhand. Tall, a scar through the left eyebrow, a smith's forearms. A reward of 100 gold for her delivery, alive, to the Magistrate's Court of Fenwick. Those who shelter her share her crime. By order of the Court, the 9th day of Harrow.

DM note: the tithe barn burned on the night of the 9th. The poster was issued that same day, so it was written before the fire. Anyone who knows when the barn burned can catch it.

**Why:** A wanted poster speaks for its issuer; the strong version carries the issuer's slant and a date that undoes it.

## 3. A map key that is only a list

**Weak**

> Key: 1. Village. 2. Forest. 3. Cave. 4. Tower. 5. River.

**Strong**

Object: the key inked in the corner of a surveyor's map on sheepskin, rolled so long it will not lie flat. A second hand has added notes in pencil.
Written by: the guild surveyor (neat, confident), and later an owner (cramped, hurried).

> 1. Bramley, 30 hearths, good well.
> 2. The Hollins, oak and ash, cut for the shipyards.
> 3. Old adit, worked out, sealed. [pencil: NOT sealed]
> 4. Signal tower, garrisoned. [pencil: empty since the fever]
> 5. Pell Ford, passable after midsummer.

DM note: the surveyor's key is twenty years old. The pencil notes belong to the previous owner, who went into the adit and did not come back out.

**Why:** A key in the mapmaker's voice, with a later owner's corrections, turns a map into a story and two of its entries into hooks.

## 4. A handout that leaks the DM note

**Weak**

> Dear Mother, the job at the manor is going well. Lord Ashcombe is actually the cult leader and keeps the ritual dagger in his study. Love, Pem.

**Strong**

Object: a single sheet folded twice, a floury thumbprint on the back; posted but never collected from the carrier's office.
Written by: Pem, a young maid, who writes small to save paper and spells carefully.

> Dear Mother, the work at the manor is steady and the food is better than ours. His lordship keeps odd hours. I am to stay out of the study after dark, which suits me, as I don't like the smell of it. The other girls say the last maid left without her wages. I would not leave without mine. Your loving Pem.

DM note: Lord Ashcombe leads the cult; the study holds the ritual dagger; the last maid did not leave.

**Why:** Pem cannot know the truth, so the letter carries only what she saw and heard; the players assemble the rest, and the DM note keeps the answer.

## 5. Paperwork with nothing in it

**Weak**

> A receipt from the general store for some rope and lamp oil.

**Strong**

Object: a shop receipt on a torn ledger leaf, spiked through on a nail, grease-stained.
Written by: Dobbs the shopkeeper, who itemizes everything.

> Sold to the account of Mr. Fenn, the 3rd of Thaw:
> Rope, hemp, 50 ft: 1 sp
> Lamp oil, 3 flasks: 3 cp
> Chalk, 1 box: 2 cp
> Iron spikes, 20: 2 sp
> Lime, quick, 2 sacks: 4 sp
> Total: 7 sp 5 cp. Paid in full. Thank you kindly.

DM note: quicklime is not climbing gear. Fenn bought it to deal with a body, and Dobbs will remember the sacks if asked.

**Why:** Ordinary paperwork is the best hiding place: one line in five is the clue, and players love finding it themselves.

## Patterns across the pairs

- Every strong handout says what it is made of and who made it.
- Every strong handout has one detail that does not fit, and the DM note says why.
- No strong handout knows more than its writer could.
- The planted lead is always something the players can check against another source.
