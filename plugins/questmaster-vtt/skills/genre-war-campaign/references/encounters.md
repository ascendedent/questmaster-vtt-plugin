# War Campaign: Encounters

The party never fights a whole battle. They fight a slice of one, and the slice decides something. Build every encounter as a situation with stakes, complications and outs, and decide before play which front's clock each outcome moves. QuestMaster rates fight difficulty through `plan_encounter`, `rate_encounter` and `propose_encounter`; this file covers shape.

## Design principles

- **The battle is weather.** The wider fighting is a backdrop that changes each round: a charge passes, smoke rolls in, a banner falls. Use the battlefield events list below.
- **Objectives, not body counts.** Hold, take, carry, destroy, rescue, signal. The enemy supply of soldiers is endless; the objective is not.
- **Clocks inside clocks.** An encounter can carry its own short clock (the relief column arrives in 5 rounds) that feeds a front's clock.
- **Surrender is real.** Both sides take prisoners. Every encounter offers outs: retreat, surrender, parley, a broken line everyone flees through.
- **Write the result as a lever.** Before play, note what each outcome does to which front.

## Combat situations

### Hold the line
- **Situation:** hold a breach, a bridge or a wall until relief arrives.
- **Stakes:** the regiment behind the party; the front's clock.
- **Complications:** the relief is late; the line breaks somewhere else; an enemy champion calls out a challenge.
- **Outs:** fall back in good order (front ticks 1), hold (front holds), break (front ticks 2 and the party must escape).

### Take the battery
- **Situation:** silence the siege engines or guns on the hill.
- **Stakes:** every round they fire, the wall loses a section.
- **Complications:** the crews are conscripts who would surrender gladly; the engine is loaded with something worse than stone.
- **Outs:** wreck the engines, turn them on the enemy, capture the crews.

### Rescue the pinned company
- **Situation:** a friendly unit is surrounded in a village.
- **Stakes:** forty soldiers, one of them a named NPC the party knows.
- **Complications:** many are wounded; the enemy expects a rescue and has set an ambush; villagers are sheltering in the same houses.
- **Outs:** break through, draw the enemy off, or negotiate the company's surrender on decent terms.

### The champion's challenge
- **Situation:** before the armies clash, an enemy champion demands single combat.
- **Stakes:** morale on both sides (a front's clock, by agreement of both commanders).
- **Complications:** the party's own commander forbids it; the champion is a person they have met.
- **Outs:** yield, first blood, a refused challenge that costs morale.

### Battlefield events (pick or roll one per round)
1. A riderless horse plows through the fight.
2. Smoke cuts sight to a few paces.
3. A friendly banner falls nearby; someone must raise it or the company wavers.
4. Enemy reinforcements crest the ridge.
5. Rain turns the field to mud.
6. A wounded messenger staggers in carrying orders meant for someone else.

## Social situations

### The war council
- **Situation:** commanders argue the plan; the party has a voice.
- **Stakes:** which front gets the reserve.
- **Complications:** each commander has a real agenda; one of them is feeding the enemy.
- **Outs:** the party's argument tips the vote; they are overruled and act alone; they leak the plan on purpose.
- Give each commander one hard fact the party could use to persuade them.

### Parley under truce
- **Situation:** meet the enemy between the lines to exchange prisoners or recover the dead.
- **Stakes:** the prisoners; a chance at a separate peace.
- **Complications:** a marksman on one side who has not heard about the truce; the enemy officer quietly offers terms her own command has not approved.
- **Outs:** the exchange, a secret channel opened, a truce broken by someone else.

### Raising the militia
- **Situation:** persuade a frightened village to fight, flee or hide.
- **Stakes:** the village, its grain, its bridge.
- **Complications:** a collaborator among the elders; the village already paid the enemy for protection.
- **Outs:** a militia, an evacuation, a village that stays neutral and is left alone (or is not).

### The prisoner who knows
- **Situation:** a captured soldier knows the attack plan.
- **Stakes:** the timing of the next assault.
- **Interesting options:** persuasion, a bargain (a letter home delivered, a guarantee of exchange), mercy that earns the truth. Cruelty is veiled by default; see safety.md.

## Exploration situations

### Crossing the dead ground
- **Situation:** cross the space between the lines at night.
- **Hazards:** craters, sharpened stakes, flares, the wounded calling for help.
- **The choice:** help the wounded (slower, more exposed) or pass them by. Either is remembered.

### The supply run
- **Situation:** get a convoy of food through winter roads.
- **Clock:** days and weather.
- **Complications:** a broken axle, a bridge washed out, a starving village on the route that sees the wagons.

### Behind the lines
- **Situation:** move through an occupied town.
- **Complications:** curfew, papers, a collaborator, someone who recognizes a character from before the war.

## Puzzle situations

### The dispatch cipher
- A captured dispatch in a regimental code. The key is a soldier's letter home: the first word of each line.

### Three wagons, one ferry
- Move the wounded, the powder and the grain across a river with one ferry before the enemy arrives. Whatever is left behind has a named cost.

### Where will they strike?
- Scattered intelligence (a large order for winter boots, a priest's travel pass, a moonless night in the almanac) points to the enemy's true target. The party chooses where to send the warning.

## Escalation

1. The wider battle reaches the party's position.
2. A named NPC is in danger.
3. The objective changes (the bridge must now be destroyed, not held).
4. The encounter clock speeds up.
5. The enemy offers terms.

## After the encounter

Record in the session notes which front moved, by how many segments, and the human-sized image that will show it next session.
