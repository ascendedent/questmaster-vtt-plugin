# War Campaign: Lore

War lore is paperwork, songs and mud. The history that matters is the last war and the grudges it left; the details that matter are who has boots and who has bread.

## Textures of the world

- **Paper runs the war.** Requisition forms, passes, muster rolls and ration chits decide who eats and who marches. A forged pass is worth more than a sword.
- **Rumor is faster than truth.** Both sides claim every victory; every defeat is blamed on a traitor.
- **Soldiers build their own culture.** Songs, superstitions, nicknames, a black market in tobacco and letters.
- **The land remembers.** Burned orchards, a field of graves, a ford renamed for the battle fought in it.
- **Magic at war is logistics (if magic exists).** A healer is worth a regiment, a weather-caller can win a siege, and a scrying glass is the most guarded object in camp.

## Sensory palette

### The camp
- **Sights:** tents in rows, laundry drying on pikes, a sergeant's chalkboard of duty rosters.
- **Sounds:** whetstones, a fiddle, coughing, the bugle at dawn.
- **Smells:** wet wool, woodsmoke, horses, latrine trenches.
- **Textures:** hard biscuit, frozen canvas, a ration of salt twisted in paper.

### The siege
- **Sights:** a wall with a new gap every morning, rubble patched with furniture, crows on the battlements.
- **Sounds:** the thud of the engines on a slow rhythm, digging beneath the walls, a bell rung for each breach.
- **Smells:** lime, wet ash, boiled leather (someone is eating their belts).
- **Textures:** grit in every meal, a cold stone parapet, rope worn smooth by buckets.

### After the battle
- **Sights:** scavengers, a broken standard, boots without owners. Keep the dead as shapes and absences unless the table has agreed to more.
- **Sounds:** wind, a horse calling, someone looking for a name.
- **Smells:** smoke, churned earth, iron.
- **Textures:** mud to the knee, a dropped locket, a letter half-written.

### The occupied town
- **Sights:** the invader's notices nailed over the old ones, shutters closed at dusk, a tavern full of officers.
- **Sounds:** foreign marching songs, conversations that stop when a uniform passes.
- **Smells:** bread only the officers can afford, horse dung on the square.
- **Textures:** a stamped pass, a curfew bell's echo, coins from two realms in one purse.

### The field hospital
- **Sights:** lanterns swinging, sheets that were once curtains, a line of stretchers.
- **Sounds:** a saw, a prayer, someone singing to keep someone else awake.
- **Smells:** vinegar, blood, burning herbs.
- **Textures:** wet bandage linen, a cot frame, a hand held too tight.

### The war council
- **Sights:** a map pinned with knives, candle stubs, a general who has not slept.
- **Sounds:** arguing, a dispatch rider's boots, a chair scraped back.
- **Smells:** ink, wax, cold coffee.
- **Textures:** the pins on the map, a creased order, a signet ring on the table.

## Naming

- **Units:** a number plus an earned nickname (the 4th of Foot, called the Turnips after the field they held); companies named for captains (Halloran's Company, the Widow's Lances).
- **Battles:** named for the most mundane landmark nearby (the Battle of the Tanner's Wall, the Cabbage Ford). Soldiers name battles; generals rename them.
- **Operations:** code names chosen by officers who think they are clever (Operation Lark, the Long Spoon).
- **People:** rank, surname and a camp nickname (Corporal Wenna Tarrow, called Kettle).
- **Places in two tongues:** every contested town has the invader's name and the locals' name, and which one a speaker uses tells you their side.
- **Enemy slang:** soldiers invent nicknames for the enemy. Keep them invented and never echo a real-world slur.

## In-world documents

Each works as a `stage_secret` handout aimed at the character who would find it.

- **Dispatch:** "To Colonel Ashgrave. Hold the mill until relieved. Relief departs first light Thursday. Burn this." It is Friday, and the dispatch was not burned.
- **Casualty list:** nailed to a chapel door; one name has been scratched out and rewritten, spelled correctly.
- **Requisition order:** for "all draft animals and one (1) blacksmith", signed by an officer who died last week.
- **Two broadsheets:** each side's account of the same battle; both claim victory, and both are partly right.
- **Letter home:** found on a fallen enemy, asking his mother to sell the cow before winter.
- **Deserter notice:** a reward for a soldier the party knows by a different name.
- **Treaty draft:** clauses crossed out in three different inks, one clause added in a fourth.
- **Marching song:** "We dug the ditch at Harrow, and we dug it deep and wide; the captain said we'd hold it, so we held it, and we died." Soldiers sing it cheerfully.
- **Pay ledger:** the company has been paid for forty soldiers for six months; twenty-two are alive.
