# War Campaign: Beats

The signature structure is the front: one place or pressure where the war can be won or lost, with a clock that fills whether the party acts or not. Arcs are seasons of war; sessions are missions; scenes bring the scale down to one person.

## The front card

Write each front on a card:

- **Name:** a place or a pressure (the Brandle Ford, the Hungry Winter).
- **Objective:** what the side pushing this front wants here, in one sentence.
- **Clock:** 4 to 8 segments. Each segment is a change someone in the world would notice, not an abstract number.
- **Tick triggers:** what advances it (time, a party failure, an ally's betrayal, an enemy mission succeeding).
- **Levers:** 2 or 3 missions that move it, and by how much (back 2, freeze for a session, forward 1 as a side effect).
- **When it fills:** the consequence, written as a new situation, never just "you lose".
- **Rollback:** what it takes to push it back, and what that costs.

### Front types (mix at least one that is not a battle line)

- **Military:** a crossing, a siege, a mountain pass, a fleet.
- **Supply:** food, salt, arrows, horses, the harvest.
- **Political:** an ally wavering, a council vote, a succession.
- **Morale:** desertion, fever in the camps, a prophet preaching to the troops.
- **Shadow:** spies, an assassin, a traitor in high command.

## The war turn (between sessions, in prep)

1. Tick each front for time (usually one segment; none if the season stalls it).
2. Apply the levers the party pulled, as written on the cards.
3. Apply one enemy lever, chosen for what the enemy would sensibly do next, never to punish the party.
4. For each front that moved, pick one human-sized image to show next session.
5. Refresh the mission board: retire missions whose window closed, add missions the movement created.
6. Check for full clocks: a full clock becomes next session's opening situation.

Offer the DM the war turn as a short list of proposed ticks with reasons; the DM confirms each one before anything is drafted.

## Arc: a season of war (6 to 12 sessions)

1. **Mobilization.** The war arrives in the party's lives. Show the ordinary world bending: requisition officers, a fair cancelled, a sibling enlisting.
2. **First contact.** A small, sharp encounter that shows the enemy's competence and humanity. The party watches a front move before they ever move one.
3. **The long middle.** Two to five sessions of lever missions with triage. Fronts tick; the party chooses what to save.
4. **The turn.** A decisive operation that the earlier levers set up. Its odds depend on which clocks the party held back.
5. **Aftermath.** The costs come due, the peace creates new fronts, and a promise made in the middle has to be kept.

## Session: the mission

- **Briefing:** a war council, a dispatch or a desperate villager. State the lever plainly: what moves if they succeed.
- **Approach:** getting there through the war (checkpoints, refugees, an emptied village). One choice that costs time or exposure.
- **The lever:** the core of the mission (burn the pontoons, carry the message, steal the paymaster's chest). Offer two shapes: a fast, cruel way and a slow, costly way.
- **The war intrudes:** something bigger than the mission passes through (a cavalry sweep, a barrage, a battle the party is not part of).
- **Exfil:** getting out, with what they carry and whoever they brought.
- **Ripple:** a short closing scene that shows a front moving because of what they did, or did not do.

## Scene: the human-sized consequence

- **The person:** one named individual whose life the front just changed.
- **The object:** a thing that carries it (a regimental button, a burnt ledger, a mill pin).
- **The ask:** what that person wants from the party now (nothing, a message carried, revenge, a ride).
- **The echo:** a line the players will remember when the front moves again.

## Worked example: the Brandle Ford campaign

**Setup.** The March of Edrevin, a frontier province, is invaded in late autumn by the Ostrine League. The party are irregulars attached to the March's under-strength garrison.

**Front 1, the Brandle Ford (military).** Objective: the League crosses the river before the thaw. Clock (6): scouts seen on the far bank; the ferry village burned; pontoons built in the oak woods; a feint at the northern crossing draws off the garrison; the crossing; the bridgehead holds and the capital road is open. Levers: burn the pontoons (back 2); expose the feint (freeze for a session); kill the chief engineer (back 1, but the League hangs ten villagers in reprisal). Fills: the court flees the capital and blames the party's patron.

**Front 2, the Hungry Winter (supply).** Objective (the season's, not the enemy's): the province starves before spring. Clock (8): requisitions begin; a granary riot; salt runs short; the first deaths; farmers hide their grain; fever in the camps; the garrison eats its horses; the garrison mutinies. Levers: recover the salt barges (back 2); broker a truce with hoarding farmers (freeze); raid a League supply train (back 1, and the Ford ticks forward 1 as the League presses harder).

**Front 3, the Wavering Duke (political).** Objective: Duke Aster of the eastern hills joins the League. Clock (4): his envoy seen at the League camp; his levies stay home; he refuses the summons; he declares. Levers: rescue his captive heir (back 2); prove the League will betray him (back 1); seize his castle first (stops this clock and starts a new front: a rebellion in the hills).

**Session 3, the Mill at Cotter's Rise.**
- **Briefing:** a captured engineer's notebook shows pontoon timber going to a sawmill two miles from the river.
- **Approach:** through the burned ferry village, where a family hiding in the chapel asks to come along (they will slow the party).
- **Lever:** a League company guards the mill; local sawyers are forced to work it. Burn it (fast, ends the sawyers' livelihood) or free the sawyers and spoil the timber (slower, and the sawyers become allies).
- **The war intrudes:** League cavalry sweeps the road on the way out.
- **Ripple:** next session opens with the Ford clock pushed back and the sawyer Hennet Grale walking into the garrison to enlist; if the mill burned with the sawyers' tools inside, he comes to curse the party instead.
