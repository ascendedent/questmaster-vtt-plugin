# High Fantasy Epic: Encounters

Epic encounters are situations where the prophecy's question is tested at a scale the party can touch. Build each with stakes, complications and outs. QuestMaster rates fight difficulty through `plan_encounter`, `rate_encounter` and `propose_encounter`; this file covers shape and meaning.

## Design principles

- **Every encounter carries a piece of the question.** A fight, a talk or a puzzle should leave the party knowing more about which answer they want.
- **Legends can be talked to.** Dragons, giants and ancient guardians have wants. Combat is one option among several.
- **The party plays a slice of epic events.** A whole battle is too big; the gate, the bridge, the banner and the duel are not.
- **Wonder can be a hazard.** A marvel the party stops to admire is a marvel that can collapse, wake or move.
- **Outs are part of the design.** Retreat, bargain, a debt owed, an oath sworn, help arriving at a cost.

## Combat situations

### The dragon on the bridge
- **Situation:** an ancient dragon sits on the only bridge across a gorge the party must cross.
- **Stakes:** the road, and whatever the dragon decides to tell the enemy about them.
- **Complications:** the bridge is older than the dragon and cracking; the enemy's scouts are a day behind.
- **Outs:** tell her a story she has not heard; trade a secret; fight on a crumbling bridge; take a route that costs a week.

### The gate must hold
- **Situation:** the party holds one gate of a besieged city while the great battle rages beyond sight.
- **Stakes:** the refugees sheltering in the square behind the gate.
- **Complications:** the gate's ancient ward fails mid-fight; a famous ally arrives and wants to charge out.
- **Outs:** hold until dawn, collapse the arch and seal the gate forever, evacuate through the cisterns.

### The herald's duel
- **Situation:** the enemy's herald offers single combat to decide one thing (a hostage, a road, a relic).
- **Stakes:** the thing named, and the herald's respect.
- **Complications:** the herald fights to show the party something, not to win.
- **Outs:** victory, yielding, a refusal that the herald answers with a warning instead of an attack.

### The corrupted guardian
- **Situation:** a guardian of the old world has been turned by the enemy and now guards the wrong thing.
- **Stakes:** the place it guards; the guardian itself.
- **Complications:** destroying it is easier than restoring it, and restoring it takes something precious.
- **Outs:** destroy, restore at a cost, trick it into guarding the right thing again.

### The hunt for the candidates
- **Situation:** the enemy's hunters pursue one of the prophecy's candidate answers (a foundling, a monk, a character).
- **Stakes:** that person, and an answer to a prophecy line.
- **Outs:** escape, decoy, turn the hunters (they were promised something they will not get).

## Social situations

### The council of realms
- **Situation:** kings, elders and free cities gather to decide whether to face the enemy together.
- **Stakes:** an alliance, or a scattered world.
- **Complications:** each power has its own reading of the prophecy, and each reading crowns them.
- **Outs:** a united front, a partial alliance, or the party walking out with the few who would follow.
- Give each delegate one want and one fear the party could discover in advance.

### The oath at the stone
- **Situation:** an ancient people will help only if the party swears an oath that binds their descendants.
- **Stakes:** an army, a road, a song of passage.
- **Outs:** swear, negotiate the terms, or refuse and find another way, each recorded as a thread.

### Persuading the reluctant chosen
- **Situation:** the person the world says is chosen does not want to be.
- **Stakes:** a prophecy line, and a person's freedom.
- **Outs:** they agree, they refuse and the party carries it, they agree to a version the party invents with them.

## Exploration situations

### The forest that rearranges
- **Situation:** an elder forest moves its paths each night.
- **Clock:** supplies, and the enemy closing on the forest's edge.
- **Outs:** earn a guide, learn the forest's song, burn a path (and lose its trust forever).

### The sky stair
- **Situation:** a stair of light climbs to a ruined citadel; it fades a step at a time behind whoever climbs.
- **Complications:** wind, heights, a stretch where the stair is broken.
- **Outs:** climb fast, leave a beacon to keep it lit, turn back before it is gone.

### The ruin of the first age
- **Situation:** a city older than any living memory, mapped in layers.
- **Stakes:** a prophecy fragment in its original tongue.
- **Complications:** the ruin is still inhabited by people who never left and do not know the age has turned.

## Puzzle situations

### The prophecy as riddle
- **Situation:** a sealed door opens only when the party speaks the correct reading of a line.
- **The design:** at least three readings open it, each opening onto a different chamber. The reading they choose is how they answered the line.

### The song lock
- **Situation:** a vault opened by a melody whose verses are scattered across the map.
- **Complications:** one verse is known only to the enemy.

### The seal of five keys
- **Situation:** each key is a decision, not an object (forgive someone, give up a gift, speak a true name, carry a weight, let someone go).

## Escalation

Move outward one ring at a time: personal (a friend in danger), local (a town), regional (a realm), the world. Each ring adds witnesses and makes the next choice heavier.
