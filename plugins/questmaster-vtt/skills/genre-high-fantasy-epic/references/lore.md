# High Fantasy Epic: Lore

Epic lore is deep time made touchable. Players should feel the ages under their boots without sitting through a history lecture: a worn step, a song with a wrong verse, a name that means something in a dead tongue.

## Textures of the world

- **Layers of ages.** Every place sits on older places. A market square paved with the floor of a palace; a shepherd's wall built from a temple's columns.
- **Names have weight.** True names, renounced names, names written in star registries. Losing or claiming a name is an act with consequences.
- **Distance is real.** The map is big, roads are slow, and news arrives late and wrong. A journey changes the traveler.
- **Songs remember what books forget.** Folk songs preserve history the archives edited out, often in the chorus nobody thinks about.
- **Light and dark are not metaphors only.** Dawn, the long night and a star's rising can be literal forces, but the people who serve them are still people.

## Sensory palette

### The elder forest
- **Sights:** trunks wider than houses, light falling in columns, moss shaped like old carvings, or carvings shaped like moss.
- **Sounds:** a hush that swallows footsteps, a single bell-like birdcall, wind high above that never reaches the ground.
- **Smells:** loam, resin, rain that has not fallen yet.
- **Textures:** bark soft as felt, roots like stair rails, cold water in a hollow stone.

### High places
- **Sights:** cloud seas below, a ruined watchtower aimed at a star that has moved, eagles below eye level.
- **Sounds:** wind in a broken arch, stone ticking as it cools at dusk.
- **Smells:** snow, thin clean air, lichen.
- **Textures:** frost on iron rings, steps worn into shallow bowls, a handhold carved for a hand larger than any human's.

### The ruin of the first age
- **Sights:** doors twice the height they need to be, murals of people with too many stars around them, a lamp still burning with no fuel.
- **Sounds:** echoes that answer a beat late, water running somewhere under the floor.
- **Smells:** dust, old incense, metal that never rusted.
- **Textures:** stone smooth as glass, glyphs warm to the touch, a chair that fits nobody alive.

### The enemy's land
- **Sights:** farms that are tidy and full, people who are fed, banners that are beautiful. Then one thing wrong: no shadows at noon, or children who never sing.
- **Sounds:** a hymn that is lovely until the third verse.
- **Smells:** bread, myrrh, and under it, cold ash.
- **Textures:** perfect cobbles, a hand that is too warm, a gift that cannot be put down.

### The hearth
- **Sights:** a kettle on a hook, a child's drawing of the party, a mended chair.
- **Sounds:** the dog, the neighbors arguing about a fence, the bells at dusk.
- **Smells:** onions, woodsmoke, wet boots by the fire.
- **Textures:** a quilt, a worn doorframe with heights marked on it, a mug with a chip in the same place for years.

## Naming

- **Build a sound palette per culture.** Pick 6 to 8 consonants and 3 or 4 vowels, and stick to them. The vale of Hessary uses h, s, r, l, v, m and short e and a: Hessary, Velm, Sarrel, Mael, Ressa, Harrowby.
- **Elder peoples:** longer names, doubled vowels, soft endings: Ouvaine, Selleth, Iorrae. One element that means something ("-aine", "of the dawn").
- **The enemy's tongue:** give it beauty, not harsh consonants. A language that sounds monstrous tells players its speakers are monsters; that is the wrong lesson.
- **No apostrophe soup.** Use an apostrophe only if it means something (a dropped syllable, a title).
- **Places named in meaning:** Grey Orrin ("the sleeper"), the Weeping Stair, Thornfast. Let one place have a name in the old tongue whose translation surprises the party.
- **Ages named for what ended them:** the Age Before the Bells, the Breaking of the Bridges, the Long Quiet.
- **Artifacts named for their maker or their cost:** Maela's Patience, the Price of Thornfast.

## In-world documents

Each works as a `stage_secret` aimed at the character most likely to find or receive it.

- **The prophecy in three translations:** the temple's ("one born beneath no star"), the miners' ("one born where no star shines"), the original's literal gloss ("one who is owed no star").
- **The hero's song:** the vale's ballad of the last age, with a verse every child sings wrong, and the wrong version is the true one.
- **A star registry page:** births recorded by the star they rose under; one line scratched out and initialed by a high priest.
- **A map with a blank:** an old chart where one valley is carefully left empty, with a note in the margin: "do not".
- **A letter from the last time:** the previous hero writing to whoever comes next, apologizing in advance.
- **A child's primer:** "A is for Age, B is for Bells", teaching a version of history the archives deny.
- **The dragon's ledger:** stories traded, rated and dated; the party's own story appears in it, with a date that has not happened yet.
