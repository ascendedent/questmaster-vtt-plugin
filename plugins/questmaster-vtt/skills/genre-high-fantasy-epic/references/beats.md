# High Fantasy Epic: Beats

Three signature structures carry this genre: prophecy as a question, the chosen-one inversion, and wonder pacing. The arc is a long road; the session runs from hearth to campfire; the scene is the reveal.

## Prophecy as a question

Write each prophecy line on a card:

- **The line:** the words as the world knows them.
- **The question:** in plain words, what this line asks someone to decide.
- **Candidate answers (3 or more):** different people or acts that would fulfill it, each defensible from the text.
- **The cost of each:** what is lost if this answer comes true.
- **The delivery:** how the party might meet it (a fragment, a dream, a translation, a song).

Rules:
- Put the ambiguity in nouns and verbs ("the one who carries", "the mountain that sleeps"), never in vagueness ("something will happen").
- Give every line one literal answer, one metaphorical answer, and one answer the players could make true by choice.
- Translations disagree. Let the original tongue say something a translation dropped.
- Once play answers a line, retire the other candidates. Keep one alive as a later twist only if the DM asks, and only as an option the players can take up.

## The chosen-one inversion

Offer the DM two of these shapes and let them choose:

- **The reluctant chosen:** the prophesied NPC refuses, and is right to. The party decides whether to persuade, protect, or carry the burden themselves.
- **The wrong chosen:** everyone believes it is the knight; the text, read properly, points elsewhere.
- **The many chosen:** the prophecy fits a dozen people, and the enemy is hunting all of them.
- **The claimed destiny:** the prophecy names a deed, not a person; whoever does it becomes the chosen one.
- **The chosen enemy:** the villain is named too, and believes it with all their heart.

## Wonder pacing

Each wonder beat runs in four steps:
1. **Ordinary ground:** a scene of mundane texture first (mending boots, a toll keeper's complaint about his knees).
2. **The hint:** a sound, smell or small wrongness before any sight (the river runs a little uphill; the birds go quiet).
3. **The reveal:** one impossible detail and one human-scale detail (a stair of light, worn smooth in the middle by centuries of feet).
4. **The witness:** an NPC reacts, showing what it means here (an old guide weeps; a child laughs; a soldier kneels).

Pacing rules:
- At most one marvel per session. Two in one session cancel each other out.
- Follow a marvel with rest or a personal scene, never another spectacle.
- Escalate scale over the arc, not within a session.
- Bring an early marvel back later, changed (the stair of light, broken).

## Arc: the long road (8 to 15 sessions)

1. **The small world.** Start at a home worth saving, with names and habits.
2. **The question arrives.** A fragment, a herald, a sign. The party's first choice is whether to care.
3. **The widening.** The map opens; the first marvel; the first ally from far away.
4. **The question sharpens.** A second fragment or translation reframes the first. Candidate answers become people the party knows.
5. **The cost.** Something precious is lost or given up. The ancient enemy's present-day face makes a good argument.
6. **The answer.** The party answers the prophecy by a choice, at a place the map promised them.
7. **The return.** Home, changed, and the world remembers what they did.

## Session: hearth to fire

- **Hearth:** an ordinary scene and something small to care about.
- **Road:** travel as a choice (two routes, two costs).
- **Threshold:** the wonder beat, or a dread beat that makes the next wonder land harder.
- **Trial:** an encounter where the prophecy's question is tested.
- **Fire:** a camp scene where characters talk; one personal beat that feeds the question.

## Worked example: The Ninth Bell (arc opening, 5 sessions)

**Setup.** Nine great bells hang in the towers of the vale of Hessary and have rung each dusk since the last age. This year, eight have cracked. The prophecy, as the vale sings it: "When the ninth bell's voice is still, one born beneath no star shall wake the mountain that sleeps, and the long night will name its price."

**Line cards.**
- *"One born beneath no star."* Literal: Pell, a foundling stable hand with no recorded birth sign. Metaphorical: a child born in the deep mines, where no sky shows, now held hostage by the enemy. By choice: anyone who strikes their name from the temple's star registry, renouncing their family (a character could). Costs: a frightened boy's safety; a rescue into enemy ground; a living family cut off.
- *"Wake the mountain that sleeps."* Literal: Grey Orrin, a dormant volcano that would bury the enemy host and the vale's farms. Metaphorical: the stone giant asleep in its heart, who might fight for either side. By choice: the Sleeping Monastery, whose monks dream the ward over the vale; waking them ends the ward and frees them.
- *"The long night will name its price."* The enemy's herald names the price, not fate. Who pays is negotiated in play.

**Inversion.** The temple has declared Ser Callow Aument the chosen one, born at midnight under heavy cloud ("no star"). He knows it is a stretch and is terrified. The party can prop him up, expose the stretch, or stand beside him.

**Wonder schedule.**
- Session 1: dusk across the vale, each bell a different note; the one cracked bell's silence where a note should be.
- Session 2: a shard of a cracked bell hums when carried past one particular standing stone.
- Session 3: the Sleeping Monastery; two hundred monks breathing in unison, frost on the windows drawing the shapes of their dreams.
- Session 4: no marvel; a dread beat at the cracked ninth tower, which sets up the next.
- Session 5: beneath Grey Orrin, the giant's heartbeat makes every lamp flame dip at once.

**First choice to offer.** The enemy's agents are cracking the bells. Protect the ninth bell (delay the prophecy, learn why the bells matter) or let it fall silent (start the prophecy on the party's terms).
