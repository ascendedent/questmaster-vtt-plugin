# High Fantasy Epic: Pitfalls

The ways an epic goes wrong, how to spot each one, and the fix.

## The chosen one steals the game
- **Spot:** one character (or NPC) is destined, and everyone else is a bodyguard.
- **Fix:** invert it. The chosen one is an NPC with a reason to refuse, or the prophecy names a deed anyone can claim. If a player wants to be chosen, the choosing still happens at the table.

## The prophecy as spoiler
- **Spot:** the DM wrote the ending in verse in session one, and every player choice just walks toward it.
- **Fix:** every line has at least three candidate answers with different costs. The DM does not pick one in advance; play does.

## Wonder inflation
- **Spot:** every session has a dragon, a sky-city and a god, and none of them land anymore.
- **Fix:** at most one marvel per session, set up by ordinary texture and followed by rest. Escalate scale over the arc, not within a night.

## The famous-trilogy pastiche
- **Spot:** a cursed ring, a dark tower, halflings from a pastoral village, elves departing the world. Players recognize the source and start predicting.
- **Fix:** keep the feeling (deep time, a long road, small people mattering) and invent the furniture. Ask what this world's ancient evil wants that no other has wanted.

## Evil races
- **Spot:** a whole people is evil by birth and exists to be killed.
- **Fix:** the enemy is a cause, a congregation or an oath, not a bloodline. Its followers come from every people, including the party's. See safety.md.

## The dark lord with nothing to say
- **Spot:** the villain wants darkness, conquest or destruction, and that is all.
- **Fix:** a present-day face with a grievance that was once just, and a promise people want (an end to hunger, a world without kings, the dead returned).

## The travelogue
- **Spot:** "You travel for three weeks and arrive" every time; or a long description of scenery with no decisions.
- **Fix:** every journey has a route choice with a cost, one encounter that changes someone, and one detail that pays off later.

## The lore lecture
- **Spot:** an NPC or a read-aloud box spends ten minutes on the history of the Second Age.
- **Fix:** history arrives as objects, songs, ruins and arguments. One fact per scene, ideally one the players asked for.

## Stakes too big to feel
- **Spot:** "the fate of the world" is at stake, and the table shrugs.
- **Fix:** tie every world-scale stake to a personal one: the hearth, a named friend, the inn with the knives. The world is saved one person at a time.

## The scheduled mentor death
- **Spot:** the wise old mentor dies at the end of act one, as expected.
- **Fix:** the mentor lives and turns out to be wrong about something, or the mentor's death is a choice the mentor makes, offered as an option, not a schedule.

## Gods in the machine
- **Spot:** a god or an elder being solves the final problem.
- **Fix:** powers greater than the party set conditions, offer bargains and refuse to act directly. The decisive act belongs to the players.

## The fixed ending
- **Spot:** the finale is written before the party reaches it.
- **Fix:** write the finale as a place and a question, with three or more possible outcomes based on which candidate answers the party chose along the road.

## The lonely epic
- **Spot:** the party saves the world with no allies, no armies and no help.
- **Fix:** epics are about many people choosing to stand together. Each leg of the road should add an ally, and the finale should show them.

## Prophecy as homework
- **Spot:** the players are handed a dozen cryptic verses at once and stop reading any of them.
- **Fix:** one fragment at a time, each tied to a place or person they can visit, delivered as a `stage_secret` to one character who decides whether to share it.

## Wonder with no one to witness it
- **Spot:** the marvel is described beautifully and the table moves on without a reaction.
- **Fix:** give every marvel a witness NPC whose reaction shows what it means here, and a question it raises that the players can act on.

## Grimness creeping in
- **Spot:** every marvel is tainted, every ally betrays, every win is hollow.
- **Fix:** high fantasy runs on earned hope. Losses are real, but some wonders stay pure and some promises are kept.
